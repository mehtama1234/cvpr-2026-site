3D from a flat image · how the field actually recovers it

Putting the dimension back — 3D-from-image across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the problem from first principles: a photo deletes depth, and you get shape from one view but metric size only from two. This page shows how the field actually does it — recovering 3D is the single largest research cluster at CVPR 2026 — and how, in a robot, the very same geometry must be metric, real-time and onboard because a wrong depth is a collision. Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)~300+ papers — ~148 dense/neural SLAM, ~70 odometry, ~42 monocular depth, ~42 LiDAR/radar fusion, ~26 event, ~21 stereogeometry that must be metric, real-time and onboard — a whole industry of putting-the-meters-back

Two things to hold onto. In vision, the old calibrate→match→optimize pipeline is collapsing into a single trained forward pass, and every sub-field is bending toward that feed-forward centre. In robotics, the same math faces the opposite acceptance test — can the robot act on it, now, without falling over — so the villain is scale ambiguity, defeated a different way in every pattern.

The uses — from a reconstruction to a robot's ruler

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (one-image depth, stereo, feed-forward reconstruction, camera pose, full solid shape, metric scale); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into metric, live, onboard geometry (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · One image, instant depth — monocular depth foundation models

The hardest version: a single photo, no second view. In principle impossible (a small close object and a big far one give the same image), but learned priors — things shrink with distance, this texture is a wall — make relative depth readable. The field now trains one big model that hands back a depth map for any image, zero-shot.

2 · Two eyes are better than one — stereo & multi-view geometry

Give the machine a second viewpoint and the impossible becomes solvable: the sideways shift of a point across two cameras (parallax) is a direct measurement of distance. The catch is matching — finding each pixel's twin across textureless walls and reflections. The 2026 twist: make matchers zero-shot and real-time.

3 · Images in, geometry out, no calibration — feed-forward pose-free reconstruction

The through-line of 2026 and the field's centre of gravity. Classically you first had to solve for the cameras — a slow, brittle step. The DUSt3R/VGGT lineage threw that out: feed a transformer a pile of uncalibrated photos and it directly regresses the 3D geometry in one pass, recovering the cameras as a byproduct. The corpus is saturated with the resulting *3R/*GGT family (~106 papers, the largest pattern).

4 · Where was the camera — SfM, pose & SLAM

Before or alongside the scene you often need the cameras themselves: where each shot was taken (structure-from-motion), or a robot's live position while it maps (SLAM). Classic solvers are precise but fragile — they choke on low texture, symmetry, darkness and motion. 2026 splices the robust learned priors from pattern 3 into the classical pose machinery.

5 · From flat picture to full solid — implicit surfaces, meshes & occupancy

Depth and pointmaps give only the visible shell — the front of things. A real object is a whole solid with a back you can't see and filled-vs-empty space everywhere. This pattern recovers that: implicit fields (distance-to-surface or inside/outside), meshes, and full 3D occupancy grids, hallucinating the unseen parts from learned shape priors.

6 · Putting the ruler back — metric-scale recovery

Almost everything above recovers shape up to scale — the geometry is right but the size floats free. A single image can't tell a dollhouse from a house. For robots, AR and driving you need real metres, and the scale has to come from somewhere the image doesn't hold. This is the explicit fight to pin it down.

7 · Live metric geometry on the move — visual-inertial odometry & SLAM

Robotics inverts the goal. A moving robot needs to know, moment to moment, where it went and the 3D around it — from its own camera. A lone camera gives motion only up to scale, so robots bolt on an IMU to nail the real metres, and it all runs live. This is the geometry engine under robot navigation (~148 SLAM + ~70 odometry papers).

8 · Metric depth onboard, right now — stereo, RGB-D & monocular scale recovery

A robot dodging a wall or grasping a cup needs depth in real metres, computed on its own hardware, in real time. Two cameras a known distance apart give metric depth directly; a single cheap camera needs the meters forced back in. Both must run fast and generalize to the scene the robot is actually in.

9 · Seeing where cameras fail — event & LiDAR/radar fusion

Ordinary cameras fail exactly where robots operate — fast motion, darkness, glare. Event cameras report per-pixel brightness changes microsecond-fast with no blur; LiDAR and radar give direct metric range. Fusing them buys geometry that survives the hard cases, once the mismatched modalities are aligned and calibrated.

What changes when a robot has to act on the depth

Same geometry at the core — opposite acceptance test.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
What counts as successa faithful reconstruction / low error numberthe robot grasps or navigates — geometry is a means, not the deliverable
Relative or metricrelative depth is often fine (the picture looks right)must be metric — real metres, or the arm misses and the drone hits the wall
Whenoffline, unbounded time and memoryreal-time, in a control loop; a stale depth map is already wrong
Where it runsa datacenter GPUonboard the drone/arm's own limited compute
The scale problemcan often be shrugged off or fixed in postbites hardest — a mono camera can't give metres, so bolt on IMU / stereo baseline / kinematics / LiDAR
The through-line. In vision, recovering 3D is a reconstruction to admire — and relative depth, shape up to scale, is usually enough. In robotics it is a ruler the machine acts on, so it must be metric, live, and onboard, and the scale ambiguity that vision can ignore becomes the central enemy. Same missing dimension; the difference is whether something has to move because of the answer.
