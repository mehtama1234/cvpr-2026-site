Mixture-of-LoRA · how the field actually uses it

The same idea, in the wild — across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued one skeleton from first principles: a frozen foundation, a drawer of cheap specialist patches, and a rule to combine them. This page shows that skeleton wearing real clothes — how researchers at CVPR 2026 and the 2025 robotics conferences (ICRA + IROS) actually deploy LoRA, adapters, and mixture-of-experts, what problem each usage solves, and why the two fields reach for it for different reasons. Every paper named here is drawn from the analyzed corpora.

The skeleton, and how big it is

Strip the vocabulary away and almost every method below is the same move: don't retrain the giant — freeze it, and add something small that specializes or routes. The variable is only what the specialists represent (a visual concept, an art style, a sensor modality, a motor skill) and how they are combined (a learned gate, a static merge, a hypernetwork). Here is the footprint, measured across the two corpora:

corpusLoRA / low-rankadaptersMoE / routingreading
CVPR 2026 — non-generation vision~119~113~74~300+ papers (5–6% of the conference) adapt or route over frozen backbones
CVPR 2026 — image/video generation~45–50 use LoRA/adapters centrally~5 gatedthe densest LoRA cluster — but mostly composition, not routing

Two things jump out before any detail. In vision the pattern is everywhere and its centre of gravity is generation — composing concept- and style-LoRAs. In robotics the same idea is rare: of 127 vision-language-action papers, only four adapt the model parameter-efficiently at all; the field prefers to fine-tune or distill the whole thing. Why those differ is the payoff of this page — but first, the patterns.

The usage patterns — what problem each one solves

Nine recurring uses, ordered roughly from “cheaply adapt one model” to “compose a committee.” Each is a first-principles answer to a real problem; the papers are real exemplars from the corpora (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS). Want each paper opened up — its real problem, approach, and the dot it connects? That's the companion paper-by-paper deep-reads.

1 · Adapt a frozen giant to a new domain or sensor

The most common use, in both fields. Foundation backbones (CLIP, SAM, DINOv3, Depth-Anything) are trained on ordinary photos; your data isn't. Full fine-tuning is too expensive and erases the pretrained prior, so you inject a few low-rank params and freeze the rest. The split between fields is the gap being bridged: vision crosses a domain shift; robotics crosses a sensor/embodiment gap and cares about on-robot compute.
CVPR'26Depth Any Endoscopy — LoRA-adapts a depth foundation model into surgical endoscopy for generalizable self-supervised depth.
CVPR'26CrossEarth-Gate — a Fisher-guided engine that picks per-iteration among Spatial-LoRA / Semantic- / Frequency-Adapter modules for remote-sensing segmentation.
CVPR'26ORION: Orthonormal Text Encoding for Universal VLM Adaptation — LoRA-tunes only CLIP's text encoder with an orthogonality penalty — plug-and-play across backbones.
ROBOTICS'25LoRA-Align: All-Weather Removal for Autonomous Navigation — LoRA + SVD singular-vector alignment so adapting to new weather doesn't forget old weather.
ROBOTICS'25TASeg: Text-aware RGB-T Segmentation by Fine-tuning Vision Foundation Models — LoRA-tunes SAM for RGB-thermal and injects CLIP text embeddings.
ROBOTICS'252D-3D ViT Adapters for Label-Efficient LiDAR Segmentation — cross-attention adapters graft a frozen ViT onto point clouds in the low-label regime.

2 · Compose concepts & styles in generation — the densest cluster

This is where “many LoRAs + a combine rule” is most mature. You have concept- and style-LoRAs and want two subjects, or a subject in a style, in one image. Naive addition causes interference: identities blend, styles leak. CVPR 2026's centre of gravity is content–style disentanglement and training-free fusion — principled rules for combining adapters without retraining.
CVPR'26CRAFT-LoRA: Content-Style Personalization via Rank-Constrained Adaptation and Training-Free Fusion — rank-constrained subspaces learn decoupled content/style, fused at inference with no retraining — names all three core problems at once.
CVPR'26OrthoFuse: Training-free Riemannian Fusion of Orthogonal Style-Concept Adapters — proves the adapters form a manifold and merges them by closed-form geodesic in under a second — the rigorous answer to why adding two LoRAs breaks (spectral distortion).
CVPR'26SplitFlux: Learning to Decouple Content and Style from a Single Image — finds early FLUX blocks carry content, late blocks style; a visual-gated LoRA splits by saliency.
CVPR'26PositionIC: Unified Position and Identity Consistency for Image Customization — a single FLUX LoRA + positional embeddings holds multi-subject identity and placement.
CVPR'26PLACID: Identity-Preserving Multi-Object Compositing via Video Diffusion — an image-to-video LoRA + object-aware tokens composite several subjects without identity blending.

3 · Put an MoE inside the model for capacity & specialization

Conditional computation: more parameters (more specialists) without more per-token compute, because only a few experts fire. Experts specialize by patch, modality, scale, or scene. Both fields do this — vision for perception/reconstruction capacity, robotics for multi-terrain control.
CVPR'26MoRE: 3D Visual Geometry Reconstruction Meets Mixture-of-Experts — adds FFN experts + a linear router to VGGT for adaptive multi-scene 3D reconstruction.
CVPR'26DriveMoE: Mixture-of-Experts for End-to-End Driving — a scene-specialized vision MoE (camera-view selection) + a skill-specialized action MoE — experts you can name.
CVPR'26MoECLIP — routes image patches to specialized LoRA experts inside CLIP for zero-shot anomaly detection.
ROBOTICS'25MoE-Loco: Mixture of Experts for Multitask Locomotion — experts specialize per terrain and gait; the MoE naturally induces behaviour specialization enabling skill composition, on a real quadruped and biped.
ROBOTICS'25Generalizing Motion Planners with Mixture of Experts for Autonomous Driving — an MoE causal-transformer planner where expert routing cures modality collapse across billions of scenes.

4 · Adapter / LoRA banks for continual learning

Keep a small module per task so learning a new task can't overwrite the old ones — parameter isolation as an answer to catastrophic forgetting (the stability–plasticity dilemma), usually without storing old data.
CVPR'26DGS: Dual Gradient and Semantic-Shift Guided LoRA for Class-Incremental Learning — interpolates task-specific and projected LoRA gradients to retain old knowledge.
CVPR'26On Token's Dilemma (LLaVA-DyMoE) — a dynamic MoE with drift-aware token routing for continually-learned VLMs.
CVPR'26Spectral Mixture-of-Experts for Continual Learning — experts specialize in spectral / task subspaces so tasks don't collide.

5 · Multi-task / multi-skill without interference

One frozen backbone, many tasks — but jointly training them causes gradient conflict and negative transfer. Route or switch small modules so tasks stop fighting over shared weights. In robotics this is the dominant MoE motivation: a training-dynamics problem, not a storage one.
CVPR'26Keep It Frozen: Domain-Routed Conditional Residual Modulation for Multi-Domain ViTs — a Domain Router → Parameter Synthesizer produces low-rank residuals per input, backbone frozen — the exact frozen-base-plus-specialists model, on vision.
CVPR'26FAAR: Frequency-Aware Multi-Task PEFT — automatically prunes each task's LoRA rank to harmonize competing objectives.
ROBOTICS'25Expert-Enhanced Masked Point Modeling — Sparse-MoE where fine-tuning updates only the experts (12% of params) over a frozen backbone, explicitly to fight negative transfer.
ROBOTICS'25MoE-Loco (again) — the cleanest demonstration that MoE resolves multi-task gradient conflict and yields composable skills in control.

6 · Merge specialists into one generalist (the static, router-free branch)

The alternative to routing: fold several independently-trained adapters into one frozen blend with a good conflict-resolution rule, and pay zero runtime — trading away per-input adaptivity for free inference. This is the static-merge family the why page flags as the honest competitor to routing.
CVPR'26MergeVLA: Cross-Skill Model Merging Toward a Generalist VLA — diagnoses LoRA parameter selfishness and uses sparse task-mask merging to make specialist adapters mergeable.
CVPR'26Preference-Aligned LoRA Merging — merges LoRA weights while preserving subspace coverage and correcting directional anisotropy.
CVPR'26MapReduce LoRA — trains reward-specific LoRA experts in parallel (Map) then iteratively merges them (Reduce) — a literal map-reduce over experts.
CVPR'26Label-Free Cross-Task LoRA Merging with Null-Space Compression — sets merge weights purely from adapter geometry, no output labels needed.

7 · Fix the routing itself — collapse & balancing

The papers that take the failure mode from question 4 head-on: naive top-k gating collapses onto a few experts and needs auxiliary balancing. These re-engineer the router so specialization is stable and even legible.
CVPR'26ERMoE: Eigen-Reparameterized Mixture-of-Experts — routes by cosine similarity to each expert's orthonormal eigenbasis — no gating logits, no balancing loss, no collapse. The best teaching example of what a router really is.
CVPR'26Teacher-Guided Routing for Sparse Vision MoE (TGR-MoE) — distills the routing decisions from a dense teacher to stabilize a sparse student.
CVPR'26Enhancing MoE Specialization via Cluster-Aware Upcycling — k-means / SVD initialization breaks expert symmetry when turning a dense model into an MoE.

8 · Don't train the adapter — generate or retrieve it

The frontier. Per-subject optimization is slow and community LoRA-zoos are enormous, so predict the adapter from a task description, or retrieve the right one from a library. LoRA-as-output, not LoRA-as-training.
CVPR'26SG-LoRA: Semantic-guided LoRA Parameter Generation — a CVAE generates target LoRA weights from a task's CLIP semantics, with sparse top-K aggregation over an expert bank — zero-shot, open-world.
CVPR'26LoFA: Learning to Predict Personalized Prior for Fast Adaptation — a hypernetwork predicts personalized LoRA weights for fast per-subject personalization.
CVPR'26CARLoS: Retrieval via Concise Assessment of LoRAs at Scale — behavioral fingerprints let you retrieve the right adapter from 650+ community LoRAs — expert selection as a systems problem.

9 · Mixture-of-LoRA proper — many LoRA experts + a router over a shared base

The exact pattern of the hands-on builds: not just one adapter, and not a static merge, but a pool of LoRA experts with a learned gate deciding per input. It exists in both fields — but it is still a small, sharply-defined cluster, which is itself the interesting finding.
CVPR'26TAS-LoRA: Transformer Architecture Search with Mixture-of-LoRA Experts — the most on-the-nose title — a lightweight router assigns shared LoRA experts to subnet configs, curing feature collapse. Names the pattern (MoLE).
CVPR'26RegionRoute: Regional Style Transfer — an explicit LoRA-MoE of style experts on FLUX-Kontext, with attention-supervised localization for mask-free regional style.
CVPR'26CoLoGen: Progressive Learning of Concept-Localization Duality — a noisy top-1 router over a pool of KV-projection experts unifying customization, editing and control.
CVPR'26Sparse Spectral LoRA: Routed Experts for Medical VLMs (MedQwen) — a spectrally-routed MoE of low-rank experts, with a scaling rule aligning the LoRA updates to a full-rank fine-tuned MoE.
ROBOTICS'25MoRE: Unlocking Scalability in RL for Quadruped VLA Models — the one true mixture-of-LoRA in the robotics corpus: multiple LoRA modules as sparse-activated experts inside a multimodal LLM, trained as an RL Q-function over six quadruped skills.

Why CVPR and Robotics reach for it differently

Same skeleton, different flesh — and the differences are not cosmetic; they come from what each field is actually short of.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
What a “specialist” isa visual concept, style, or domain — a subject, an art style, a dataset shifta motor skill or behaviour — a gait, a terrain, a manipulation primitive
The gap being bridgeda domain shift (photos → medical / remote-sensing)a sensor / embodiment gap (RGB → thermal / LiDAR / endoscopic; VLM → a robot's action space)
Why combine at alloften a storage / serving problem — swap thousands of concept-LoRAs from a cataloga training-dynamics problem — stop simultaneously-trained control tasks from fighting (gradient conflict)
The binding constraintquality & non-interference of the compositionon-robot compute, real-time control, and forgetting that breaks the task (safety)
Preferred adaptationkeep the frozen giant, add adaptersdistill the generalist VLA into a small RL expert (Refined Policy Distillation, ACGD, iRe-VLA)
The sharpest contrast: robotics mostly doesn't use mixture-of-LoRA. Of 127 vision-language-action papers only four adapt parameter-efficiently; the default is to fine-tune or distill the whole model. In vision the pattern is pervasive but concentrated in generation, where the hard part is composing concept-LoRAs without interference — and even there, genuine gated mixture-of-LoRA (a learned router over a pool) is only a handful of papers. The composition is usually static (merge / disentangle) rather than routed.

The white-space this reveals

Put the two surveys together and a gap appears exactly where the lab builds live. The mature, crowded work is at the ends: single-adapter domain adaptation (everywhere) and static merge / disentanglement (generation). The thin middle — a learned router over a pool of LoRA experts, jointly trained, load-balanced — is rare in generation (≈5 papers) and almost absent in robotics (one: MoRE). That is not because it doesn't work; it is because it is harder to train (routing collapse, the exact problem §5 stress-tested) and the easier static-merge answer often suffices. The first-principles takeaway of the whole theme lands here:

The field has converged on the skeleton and is still exploring the joints. Freezing a giant and bolting on cheap specialists is settled practice across both vision and robotics. How to combine those specialists — merge vs route, static vs learned, per-image vs per-token vs per-skill — is the live question, and routed mixture-of-LoRA is the least-explored, highest-ceiling corner of it. The why page is the argument; this page is the evidence that the field agrees on the argument and is still building out the answer.
