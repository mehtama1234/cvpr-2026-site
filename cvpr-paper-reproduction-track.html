Decision math

Paper Reproduction Track is not just a screen of labels. It is a measurement problem: the page is trying to decide whether a claim about the conference system should be trusted under pressure. The page summary says: Eight promoted CVPR repos, one per theme, converted into concrete demo reproduction contracts with sample inputs, expected outputs, failure probes, evidence artifacts, replay commands, and next implementation tasks.

Hidden quantity

The quantity underneath this page is repeatability: whether the result belongs to the method and data, not to one lucky run, one cached image, one machine, or one hidden manual step.

Evidence

Use commands, artifacts, fixtures, hashes, environment notes, and replay outputs as evidence. The important question is whether another run has enough information to land on the same claim.

Decision rule

A reproduction page estimates variance: if the same setup is repeated, how much can the output move before the claim changes? Small movement supports the claim; large movement demands a narrower claim.

Failure test

Reproduction fails when the path cannot be rerun, when missing files are silently replaced, or when the displayed output is disconnected from the command that supposedly produced it.

Fundamental principles at play: mle · bias-variance · uncertainty. These are the same mathematical ideas used in the math pages, but here they are attached to operational decisions: what is measured, how uncertain the measurement is, and what action the evidence permits.
