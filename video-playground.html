Can you tell which way time is running?

The why page showed a model can read the direction of time — but only when the motion is irreversible. Here you can be that model. Run a process forward, then flip it to reverse. For an irreversible process the reversed version looks impossible; for a reversible one, you genuinely cannot tell.

Quantity shown. This sandbox exposes temporal state: what must remain coherent across frames.

What the controls mean. Memory, horizon, and noise controls change how much the sequence remembers.

What to watch for. Watch small per-frame errors turn into identity drift or flicker.

The arrow is visible. Forward, the particles start bunched and spread out — the natural way things diffuse and settle. Hit Reversed and watch a scattered cloud spontaneously gather to a single point and speed up. That never happens in the real world, and it is exactly the tell a model learns to read. Irreversible motion carries a direction of time.
No arrow to find. Now the particles just drift at constant speed. Forward they slide one way; reversed they slide the other — and both look completely natural. Nothing about either clip says which is the real forward. When the physics is reversible, the direction of time is genuinely unrecoverable — which is why the model in the run was stuck at chance here.
