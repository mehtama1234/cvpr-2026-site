Feel where depth comes from

The why page made two claims with real numbers: one image can't tell you the size of the world, but two images can. Here you can move the pieces yourself. Switch between one eye and two eyes and watch what the camera actually sees.

One eye can't tell size from distance. As you drag the object farther, it also grows just enough to fill the same cone — and the picture the camera records (the bar on the right) never changes. A tiny close object and a giant far one are the same photo. That is the scale ambiguity, exactly: one image pins down the direction to everything, never the distance.
Two eyes turn shift into distance. Each object lands at a slightly different spot in the left eye than the right — and the near one shifts far more than the far one. That shift (the disparity) is a direct ruler: depth = focal × baseline ÷ shift. Widen the baseline and every shift grows, so depth gets easier to read — which is why your two eyes are set apart, and why a robot wants a wide stereo rig.
