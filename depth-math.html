3D from an image — why it works, mathematically

A camera smashes an infinite ray of points onto one pixel. One eye cannot undo that smash — but two eyes can, and a network learns the shape rules the world already obeys.

The problem, as a quantity

A perspective camera applies the map x = K·[R|t]·X, projecting a 3-D point X through intrinsics K and pose [R|t] to a 2-D pixel x. That map throws away the third coordinate: replace X with λX for any scalar λ > 0 and the pixel does not move. A point and every point along its ray from the camera are indistinguishable to the sensor. Depth recovery is therefore not merely hard — it is underdetermined: a single image equation x = K·X/Z gives two constraints but the original point has three degrees of freedom, so the system is one equation short. No algorithm, however clever, can uniquely recover metric depth from one image without additional information. The whole theme is the art of supplying that missing constraint.

The core ideas — and why they work

One image, infinite solutions (the ray ambiguity)

x = K·X/Z = K·(λX)/(λZ) for all λ &gt; 0 ⇒ scale is unobservable
Perspective projection divides by depth: the 2-D pixel position is (fX/Z, fY/Z). Scaling the entire scene — moving everything twice as far away and making everything twice as large — leaves all pixel positions unchanged, because both numerator and denominator scale by the same λ. This is not an edge case; it is the algebraic structure of the projection. The camera equation is degree-zero homogeneous in X, so scale is a null direction: it produces no image signal whatsoever. The depth-recovery problem is therefore not 'solve for Z given x' — it is 'solve for Z given x, knowing the system is rank-deficient.' You must inject information that lives outside that null direction. Same primitive elsewhere: this is the Underdetermination primitive — one observation cannot pin the answer — that reappears identically in optical flow (a single image-difference equation cannot separate the two velocity components without a smoothness assumption) and in segmentation (a pixel's class is underdetermined without context). The fix is always the same: add a constraint from geometry, time, or a learned prior.

Two eyes pin the depth (stereo triangulation)

Z = f·B / d, d = u_L − u_R, d &gt; 0 iff point is in front
A second camera, displaced by baseline B, gives a second ray through the same 3-D point. Two rays from two known positions intersect at exactly one point — that is triangulation. For a rectified stereo pair (image planes coplanar, identical vertical rows), the algebra collapses to one line: a point at depth Z shifts horizontally by d = f·B / Z pixels between the two images, where f is focal length and B is the camera separation. Rearranging gives Z = f·B / d. The key insight is that disparity d is inversely proportional to depth: near objects jump far across images, distant objects barely move — the same parallax you experience closing one eye and then the other. Disparity is the only signal that breaks the λ-symmetry, because the scale factor cancels in x_L but shifts differently in x_R due to the baseline. Same primitive elsewhere: the geometric principle is Matching — the loss must respect correspondence — which also drives CLIP, DETR, and flow-matching. Both stereo and those methods live and die on finding the right pairing between two sets of things (pixels across views, queries to objects, samples to data).

One image, learned shape prior (monocular networks)

D̂ = f_θ(I), D̂ ≈ s·D_true for unknown scalar s &gt; 0 (scale-invariant, not metric)
Real scenes are not arbitrary: walls are flat, objects sit on floors, faces are convex. A network trained on millions of (image, depth) pairs learns to recognize these regularities — not because the projection physics changed, but because real-world structure restricts the distribution of valid scenes to a tiny, structured corner of all possible 3-D configurations. What the network infers is relative or affine depth: which regions are closer than others, the shape of the depth field up to a global scale and shift. Absolute metric depth (in meters) remains unrecoverable from image statistics alone; to get it, you must anchor one measurement — a known object size, a sparse LiDAR return, or a height-above-ground prior. This is not a failure of the network; it is the λ-symmetry reappearing. The network learns the maximum-information reduction of the scene consistent with the projection, and that reduction is scale-free by law. Same primitive elsewhere: learning a shape prior is Differentiable rendering in disguise — NeRF and 3-D Gaussian Splatting both inject depth supervision by rendering images and comparing to real ones, making the projection x = K·X/Z part of the loss. Monocular depth nets do the same implicitly: the training target is the rendered depth from an RGBD sensor, and the network absorbs the rendering equation into its weights.

Self-supervised depth from motion (ego-motion as a free second view)

L = Σ_{p} ||I_t(p) − I_{t+1}(π(D̂_t, T̂, p))||₁, T̂ from PoseNet, D̂ from DepthNet
If you have video, the camera moves between frames and every consecutive pair is a free stereo rig — except the baseline and orientation change unpredictably with the driver. Self-supervised depth models solve this by jointly training two networks: a DepthNet that predicts per-frame depth and a PoseNet that predicts the camera transformation T̂ between frames. Given D̂ and T̂, you can reproject frame t into frame t+1 via the warp π, and compare the warped image to the real one. The photometric loss L is the only supervision signal — no depth labels, no LiDAR. The system is self-consistent when both networks agree: depth and pose that jointly explain the pixel motion. The degeneracy trap lurks here: a network that predicts infinite depth (all motion comes from the object, none from the camera) or zero pose (no motion at all) can trivially zero the loss. Practical systems suppress this with minimum-reprojection masking (take the best-matching frame among a window) and auto-masking pixels that do not move between frames. The enemy it defeats: the degeneracy trap — the same collapse that haunts SSL (mode-collapse), MoE routing (all tokens route to one expert), and RL (reward-hacking). The fix is always to add a constraint that makes the trivial minimizer non-trivial: here, the auto-mask removes the free solution.

The approaches, as variations on the math

Every method in the theme is a different way of injecting the missing constraint that breaks the λ-symmetry. Stereo triangulation injects it geometrically, via a known baseline and matched disparities; metric depth follows directly from Z = f·B / d. Structure-from-motion generalizes this to a moving monocular camera: accumulate many views over time, solve jointly for camera poses and 3-D points (bundle adjustment), and the scale is pinned by the camera translation. Supervised monocular networks inject it statistically, via a training set of (image, LiDAR depth) pairs; they learn the shape prior but produce scale-ambiguous output unless trained with metric supervision or a scale anchor. Self-supervised monocular networks inject it through temporal photometric consistency, jointly inferring depth and ego-motion from video; elegant and label-free, but vulnerable to degeneracy at moving objects. Diffusion and foundation-model depth inject it through a learned distribution over plausible depth maps, treating reconstruction as a conditional generation problem — highest generalization, still scale-ambiguous. The choice of method is the choice of which constraint to trust: hard geometry (reliable but needs calibration), learned statistics (flexible but needs data), or both together.

1One image, instant depth
d = f(I; θ) where θ encodes shape prior from training data. Loss compares relative scale, not absolute Z.
A single projection x=K[R|t]X loses depth scale—ten meters and ten centimeters look identical. Neural networks sidestep this by learning which shapes are common in the world (flat floors, tall people) from massive training sets. The network outputs relative depth (which is closer), and diffusion or foundation models anchor it with learned distributions rather than equations.
Depth Any Panoramas: A Foundation Model for Panoramic Depth Estimation — Assembles 2M panoramic training samples to learn a monocular prior that maps pixel coordinates to depth without multi-view geometry.
UniDAC: Universal Metric Depth Estimation for Any Camera — Factorizes depth into shape (scale-free) and scale (metric), then predicts both independently so one network handles all camera types.
Iris: Integrating Language into Diffusion-based Monocular Depth Estimation — Augments diffusion-based depth with text descriptions so that semantic scene understanding constrains the learned shape prior.
InfiniDepth: Arbitrary-Resolution and Fine-Grained Depth Estimation — Represents depth as a continuous neural function z = f(x, y) queryable at any resolution, replacing discrete pixel-grid prediction.
2Two eyes are better than one
Z = fB/d where d = x_L - x_R is disparity (pixel offset), f is focal length, B is baseline (distance between cameras).
Stereo triangulation is simple geometry: two viewpoints create a triangle with the 3D point at the apex. The disparity—how many pixels the point shifts between left and right images—is inversely proportional to depth. Matching pixels left-to-right (cost volume search) finds that disparity, and the formula inverts it to recover absolute scale in one step, solving the monocular ambiguity.
Lite Any Stereo: Efficient Zero-Shot Stereo Matching — Builds a cost volume C(x,d) scoring photometric matching confidence for each pixel x and disparity d, then aggregates across scales.
Fast-FoundationStereo: Real-Time Zero-Shot Stereo Matching — Accelerates stereo via divide-and-conquer: coarse-to-fine hierarchical warping that halves layers while maintaining FoundationStereo's cost volume.
PromptStereo: Zero-Shot Stereo Matching via Structure and Motion Prompts — Replaces recurrent refining with a Prompt Recurrent Unit that injects monocular priors from a depth foundation model into multi-resolution stereo decoding.
SPE-MVS: Spatial Position Encoding Enhanced Multi-View Stereo with Monocular Cues — Encodes camera poses and pixel 3D coordinates as explicit spatial tokens, fusing monocular priors through position-aware attention instead of raw feature blending.
3Images in, geometry out
P = Decoder(Encoder(I₁, I₂, ..., Iₙ)) where P is a pointmap or voxel grid and Encoder fuses multi-view features via homography or learned projection.
Instead of solving for camera poses and then reconstructing (Structure-from-Motion + dense matching), feed-forward models learn to bypass geometry entirely. They ingest raw images and output 3D predictions directly. The implicit learned projection is faster than hand-crafted epipolar geometry, and mixture-of-experts layers specialize on different scene types (indoor vs. outdoor, close vs. distant).
VGGT-Omega: Scaling Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction — Scales a single transformer encoder from 0.5B to 10B parameters and measures how feed-forward point prediction improves with model capacity alone.
MoRE: 3D Visual Geometry Reconstruction Meets Mixture-of-Experts — Routes each per-image pointmap through separate expert feed-forward blocks, then combines them so specialization improves multi-view aggregation.
AMB3R: Accurate Feed-forward Metric-scale 3D Reconstruction with Backend Refinement — Predicts sparse per-pixel 3D points in a feed-forward pass, then feeds them into a compact voxel grid backend that re-scores and refines coordinates.
Reliev3R: Relieving Feed-forward 3D Reconstruction from Multi-View Geometric Supervision — Trains feed-forward reconstruction using only weak 2D reprojection loss instead of expensive multi-view photometric or geometric ground truth labels.
4The cameras, and the whole solid
(R, t, X, o) = JointSolver(I₁, ..., Iₙ) where optimization alternates: refine poses (R,t), refine structure (X), refine occupancy map (o).
Single-image reconstruction or multi-view fusion alone is brittle. Hybrid pipelines use Structure-from-Motion to anchor camera poses globally, then feed-forward models to fill in detail, then bundle adjustment (rigidity constraints) to lock everything together. The camera network must be rigid (no two cameras can be in two places at once), and that rigidity is mathematically expressible as constraints on pose updates.
Global Structure-from-Motion Meets Feedforward Reconstruction — Combines rigid SfM bundle adjustment (for camera poses and sparse points) with feed-forward depth to reconstruct the full scene and camera trajectory jointly.
Parallel Rigidity Matters for Bundle Adjustment — Applies parallel rigidity theory to identify when camera-point networks are well-constrained, guiding which parameters bundle adjustment should optimize.
Monocular Open Vocabulary Occupancy Prediction for Indoor Scenes — Fuses pre-trained vision encoders with language embeddings to predict volumetric occupancy grids labeled with open-set object classes from monocular images.
SAM 3D Body: Robust Full-Body Human Mesh Recovery — Extends Segment Anything's promptable encoder-decoder design to take 2D keypoint prompts and output full 3D body mesh vertices and shape parameters.
5Putting the ruler back
Z_metric = Z_relative · s where s is computed from sparse anchors: s = Σᵢ(Ẑ_i / Ẑ_rel,i) / N, averaging over i true reference points.
Monocular depth networks output relative depth (which is closer), not metric depth (actual meters). To convert to real units, the network needs one or more ground-truth anchor points from LiDAR, radar, edges that imply known object sizes, or language describing scale. A single anchor determines global scale; multiple anchors allow per-region scale correction and detect errors in the relative depth output.
The Midas Touch for Metric Depth — Anchors scale-ambiguous relative depth to metric depth using sparse ground-truth points from LiDAR or range cameras as reference measurements.
TR2M: Transferring Monocular Relative Depth to Metric Depth with Language — Uses language descriptions of scene context (indoor/outdoor, room size) to infer absolute depth scale from networks trained only on relative depth.
Radar-Guided Polynomial Fitting for Metric Depth Estimation — Fits polynomial transformations between RGB-predicted depth and sparse but absolute radar range measurements to recover metric scale without expensive LiDAR.
MD2E: Modeling Depth-to-Edge Cues for Monocular Metric Depth Estimation — Exploits edge-to-depth relationships (occlusions reveal depth boundaries) without intrinsic parameters, allowing scale recovery from edge maps alone.
6Metric, real-time, onboard
ε_pose = Cov(Δp) estimated in real-time; Δp = Z_pred ± σ_depth. Gaussian belief propagates uncertainty through pose updates and path planning.
Robots need both depth and confidence bounds to navigate safely. Modern methods predict not just depth but per-pixel variance, building a Gaussian map where each voxel stores mean and covariance. Kinematics (the robot's rigid body) acts as a second ruler—joint angles and motor commands anchor scale independent of vision. Uncertainty feeds directly into safety filters (control barrier functions) that modulate acceleration to avoid collision even when depth is noisy.
MAC-VO: Metrics-Aware Covariance for Learning-Based Stereo Visual Odometry — Predicts full 3D error covariance ellipses Σ(x) for each matched feature instead of point estimates, enabling principled uncertainty-aware pose estimation.
KineDepth: Utilizing Robot Kinematics for Online Metric Depth Estimation — Inverts forward kinematics: robot joint angles constrain where the end-effector appears, turning the arm into a metric measuring stick for camera calibration.
Monocular One-Shot Metric-Depth Alignment for RGB-Based Robot Grasping — Aligns pre-trained depth to metric scale via single interaction without retraining: grasp attempt → measure grasp pose error → scale correction applied.
A Control Barrier Function for Safe Navigation with Online Gaussian Splatting — Constructs safety filters h(x) directly from Gaussian primitive covariances in live 3D scene maps to guarantee collision-free robot trajectories in real time.
The through-line. The whole theme rests on one algebraic fact: perspective projection is homogeneous in depth, so scale vanishes into a null direction that no image signal can excite. Every method is a different route around that null direction — a second eye (stereo), a second moment in time (ego-motion video), a learned shape prior (monocular network), or a direct range sensor (LiDAR). The two moves that run underneath all of them are break the symmetry with a new constraint and match observations across views or frames. Depth is the purest instance of the Underdetermination primitive: one flat image cannot pin the answer, so the whole field is an elaborate negotiation over which extra constraint is cheapest to obtain and most reliable to use.
