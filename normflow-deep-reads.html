1 · The invertible generative engine: exact-likelihood modeling

Normalizing flows are invertible neural networks that reshape simple distributions—like standard Gaussians—into complex data distributions through a learned bijective transformation. The invertibility guarantee means you can both push forward data into latent space and pull back samples via the inverse map. The change-of-variables formula gives exact log-likelihood: log p(x) = log p_base(f(x)) + log|det J|, where J is the Jacobian. This tractable likelihood opens doors: provable density estimation, principled training without auxiliary losses, and latent-space control. Recent work extends flows to high-dimensional vision (pixels, video, motion) by marrying them with autoregressive factorizations and efficient sampling schemes, proving flows can compete with diffusion models while keeping the mathematical purity of invertible transport.
CVPR'26FARMER: Flow AutoRegressive Transformer over Pixels
Problem
Autoregressive pixel-level generation faces intractable sequence lengths and high dimensionality; standard autoregressors over raw pixels neither scale nor provide exact likelihoods needed for reliable density estimation.
Approach
FARMER unifies normalizing flows with autoregressive factorization: an invertible autoregressive flow transforms images into latent sequences; self-supervised dimension reduction partitions latent channels into information-rich and redundant subsets, enabling tractable AR modeling. One-step distillation and resampling-based guidance accelerate inference and boost quality.
Why it matters
By combining invertible maps (exact likelihood) with autoregressive structure (tractable factorization), FARMER achieves provable likelihood while scaling to raw pixel data—neither pure flows (too rigid) nor pure autoregressors (no density) alone could do both.
CVPR'26STARFlow-V: End-to-End Video Generative Modeling with Autoregressive Normalizing Flows
Problem
Video generation with flows has lagged: standard flow architectures require prohibitively expensive bijections for high-dimensional video; sequential autoregressive frame generation is slow due to causal dependencies.
Approach
STARFlow-V scales flow modeling to video via global-local architecture where autoregressive NFs model inter-frame dependencies and a 3D causal VAE encodes video into compact latents. Training uses flow-score matching—a consistency condition between NF density and a causal denoiser—for stable objectives. Block-wise Jacobi iteration parallelizes NF decoding across spatial blocks, delivering 15x speedup.
Why it matters
The invertible flow generates exact samples and likelihoods even at video scale; flow-score matching bridges flow training with modern score-based reasoning; Jacobi parallelization proves invertible sampling need not be sequential, making flows competitive with diffusion for large-scale generative video.
CVPR'26Bidirectional Normalizing Flow: From Data to Noise and Back
Problem
Standard flows require explicit invertibility and tractable Jacobians, constraining architecture choices; autoregressive flows (e.g., TARFlow) sacrifice speed for expressiveness, demanding thousands of sequential steps and blocking parallelization.
Approach
BiFlow breaks the invertibility requirement by decoupling forward and reverse: train a forward model mapping data to noise via NF likelihood; learn a separate reverse model as an approximate inverse using hidden alignment (learnable projections align reverse hidden states with forward states across the trajectory). Bidirectional non-causal Transformer attention enables single-pass generation.
Why it matters
Removing the explicit invertibility constraint enables flexible architectures and full trajectory supervision; the learned reverse model outperforms mathematically-required inverses, proving that approximate but expressive reversal beats exact but rigid inversion—a paradigm shift unlocking 200x speedups while improving quality.
CVPR'26Gaussian-Mixture Latent Flow for Stochastic 3D Human Motion Prediction
Problem
Human motion futures are multimodal—many plausible continuations exist; deterministic and simple stochastic models fail to represent the full distribution of motion possibilities.
Approach
Combines Gaussian mixture modeling in latent space with continuous normalizing flows: the mixture captures the multimodal landscape of motion futures, while the flow provides flexible density modeling within each mode. Sampling draws from the learned multimodal posterior over motion.
Why it matters
Flows supply exact likelihood for each mode under the mixture; invertible transformations let you condition on observed motion and ancestrally sample diverse futures. The invertibility ensures uncertainty propagates correctly through pose space, grounding stochastic prediction in tractable probability.

2 · Invertibility as a feature: reversible networks

Normalizing flows and invertible networks exploit perfect reversibility as a computational feature, not merely as a mathematical property. By constraining transformations to be bijections, these models unlock exact reconstruction without auxiliary decoding, enable verifiable information flow tracing, and make the reverse pass as tractable as the forward. Across vision tasks from adversarial robustness to medical registration, invertibility becomes an active inductive bias: the architecture itself enforces information conservation, leading to cleaner loss landscapes, better feature alignment in multi-task settings, and physically-grounded transformations that respect topological structure.
CVPR'26RevINN: An End-to-End Invertible Neural Network for Reversible Adversarial Examples
Problem
Reversible adversarial examples require fooling classifiers while allowing authorized receivers to perfectly restore the original image. Existing methods decouple perturbation and hiding into separate stages, causing error accumulation and poor visual quality.
Approach
RevINN operates entirely in the wavelet domain with end-to-end invertibility. A Coarse Frequency Modulation Attention module performs bidirectional coupling of low/high frequencies, while a High-Frequency Perturbation Enhancement module applies fine-grained modulation across subbands. Three losses jointly optimize adversarial strength, imperceptibility, and perfect reconstruction.
Why it matters
Perfect invertibility ensures zero information loss in the attack-hide-recover chain. The bijective constraint eliminates separate pipelines and their compounding errors, allowing unified optimization of adversarial and reversibility objectives in a single architecture.
CVPR'26Target-Aware Invertible Encoder with Reconstruction Guidance for Infrared Small Target Detection
Problem
Infrared small target detection degrades weak thermal signatures through aggressive downsampling in standard deep networks. Rescale-induced information loss undermines detection of critical maritime and precision-guidance targets.
Approach
InvDet uses an invertible encoder whose inverse path reconstructs features from latents, making information loss directly observable. Target-Aware Reconstruction Modulation gates latent refinement, while a Geometry-Content Tolerance Metric focuses reconstruction on semantically relevant regions without altering forward features.
Why it matters
Invertibility reveals where downsampling destroys information, enabling direct measurement and mitigation at the source. The reversible path creates a supervision signal for representation learning that preserves detection-relevant content without adding decoder parameters.
CVPR'26NexusFlow: Unifying Disparate Tasks under Partial Supervision via Invertible Flow Networks
Problem
Multi-task learning with structurally disparate outputs (dense vs sparse) and domain-partitioned partial supervision leads to representational collapse and poor alignment across tasks with different annotation sources.
Approach
NexusFlow inserts invertible affine coupling layers per task that map heterogeneous features into a shared canonical space through dimensionality reduction and bijective transformations. These preserve task-relevant information while enabling explicit latent-space alignment without reducing model capacity.
Why it matters
Invertible couplings prevent information loss during task alignment, guaranteeing that dense predictions preserve fine-grained details while sparse tasks extract high-level semantics. The bijection ensures that alignment does not sacrifice expressiveness, allowing a single network to handle diverse structural requirements.
CVPR'26Learning Diffeomorphism for Medical Image Registration with Time-Embedded Architectures
Problem
Medical image registration must learn transformations that align anatomical structures while preserving topology and invertibility. Current methods struggle to maintain smooth, physically-plausible deformation fields over complex anatomical variation.
Approach
Learns diffeomorphic transformations through time-embedded neural networks that parameterize velocity fields integrated continuously via neural ODE. This ensures invertibility and smoothness by construction through the continuous dynamics framework.
Why it matters
Invertibility guarantees that deformations are diffeomorphic, preserving topological properties essential for medical validity. The continuous formulation naturally enforces the bijection constraint while reducing training complexity through shared forward-backward dynamics.

3 · Density is the signal: flows for anomaly detection and uncertainty

Normalizing flows compute exact probability densities via invertible transformations. This enables a direct readout: low density = abnormal. Unlike discriminative classifiers that learn which specific examples are anomalous, flows estimate p(x), so any point can be scored by how improbable it is relative to the learned distribution. This density-aware approach yields three superpowers. First, anomaly detection without labeled negative examples: train on clean data alone, then flag test points with low likelihood. Second, uncertainty quantification: regression errors can be scaled by local density—detections in low-probability regions of feature space are intrinsically harder to predict. Third, robustness to distribution shift: flows detect when input distributions drift, enabling graceful failure modes or recalibration. These four papers show flows winning across scene understanding, 3D detection, robotic manipulation, and sensor fusion—each leveraging density as the core anomaly signal.
CVPR'26BUSSARD: Normalizing Flows for Bijective Universal Scene-Specific Anomalous Relationship Detection
Problem
Scene-specific anomalous relationship detection requires recognizing unusual spatial and semantic relationships (e.g., objects in inappropriate locations, unusual human-object interactions). Existing counting-based approaches fail on long-tail distributions where many object-relationship pairs appear rarely in training data, leading to poor generalization.
Approach
BUSSARD uses normalizing flows to learn distributions over scene graph relationships via bijective transformations. Objects and relationships are converted to semantic vector representations using pretrained language models, then mapped to a standard normal distribution. Anomaly scores are computed from likelihood estimates with autoencoder-based dimensionality reduction.
Why it matters
Because flows give exact likelihood, every relationship triplet gets scored by its probability under the learned distribution. Rare or unprecedented relationship combinations naturally receive low scores, making them detectable without any labeled anomalous examples. This density-aware approach handles long-tail distributions gracefully.
CVPR'26RC-NF: Robot-Conditioned Normalizing Flow for Real-Time Anomaly Detection in Robotic Manipulation
Problem
VLA models trained via imitation learning fail under out-of-distribution conditions in dynamic robotic environments, lacking real-time monitoring for robot-object state-task alignment. Prior monitors either enumerate anomalies explicitly or use slow vision-language model reasoning incurring multi-second latency, while robotic intervention requires sub-100ms detection.
Approach
RC-NF is a conditional normalizing flow (Glow-based) that fuses robot states (joint angles, gripper, pose), task embeddings, and object point sets via a novel RCPQNet affine coupling layer. Dual-branch encoding separately models dynamic shape and positional residuals. Negative log-likelihood from the flow serves as the anomaly score, with a calibration-set threshold triggering task-level replanning or state rollback.
Why it matters
The flow directly estimates p(state | task, context), so any manipulation trajectory state outside the learned distribution receives low probability and high anomaly score. This enables unsupervised OOD detection without labeled failure examples, with <100ms latency sufficient for real-world closed-loop control.
CVPR'26Query2Uncertainty: Robust Uncertainty Quantification and Calibration for 3D Object Detection
Problem
DETR-style 3D object detectors produce confidence scores that are poorly calibrated and do not reflect actual detection reliability, especially under corrupted or out-of-distribution inputs. Standard post-hoc calibration ignores the latent query features that encode detector uncertainty implicitly.
Approach
Query2Uncertainty fits a RealNVP normalizing flow to 256-dimensional query features from the detector's final decoder layer, trained class-conditionally on true positive detections. At test time, normalized log-density and standardized density deviation are computed per query. Three density-aware calibration variants are proposed: density-aware temperature scaling, density-aware Platt scaling, and density-aware isotonic regression, with density modulating both confidence and bounding-box regression variance.
Why it matters
Query features with high likelihood under the flow indicate clean, typical detections; low-likelihood queries indicate atypical detections in noisy feature regions. By conditioning calibration temperature, regression variance, and isotonic mapping coefficients on this density signal, the method produces uncertainty estimates robust to input corruptions and distributional shift.
CVPR'26mmWaveFlow: Unified Enhancement and Generation of mmWave Human Point Clouds
Problem
mmWave point clouds are sparse and noisy with limited annotated datasets, hindering adoption for fine-grained human pose and shape sensing. Enhancement and generation are treated as separate tasks, yet both involve mappings between sparse and dense representations of the same underlying signal.
Approach
mmWaveFlow proposes a unified flow-matching framework learning invertible bidirectional transport between dense and sparse point clouds. Cross-modal Latent Alignment enforces semantic correspondence, and Origin-Aware Flow Matching mitigates path crossing issues in the transport. The same learned invertible map can both enhance sparse clouds and generate missing detail.
Why it matters
Flow-matching provides exact probability transport and invertibility, enabling symmetric bidirectional mapping. Dense and sparse clouds both lie on a learned manifold; flows naturally estimate which sparse points are anomalous (low-likelihood) or missing detail (require generation). Exact density estimates guide both enhancement and imputation.

4 · Flow priors for perception and enhancement

Flows provide a tractable generative foundation for vision tasks. Rather than fitting fixed architectures to each problem, these papers attach flexible flow priors—learned continuous distributions—to perception and enhancement pipelines. A flow prior captures structure (topological continuity, region adjacency), enables controllable sampling (text guidance, preference alignment), and inverts smoothly (continuous action control, trajectory generation). The result: models that perceive with structure, enhance with flexibility, and act with fluid precision.
CVPR'26P-Flow: Prompting Visual Effects Generation
Problem
Visual effects generation requires precise control over content and style, which traditional generative methods struggle to offer without detailed manual specification or complex constraint programming.
Approach
Uses flow-based generative models conditioned on text prompts to enable controllable visual effects generation. The flow prior learns the distribution of effects while text guidance steers generation toward user intent.
Why it matters
A flow prior attached to effect generation becomes a flexible, prompt-steerable sampler. Text prompts provide fine-grained control by routing through the learned effect distribution, enabling intuitive high-level specification without manual parameter tuning.
CVPR'26HySeg: Learning Generative Priors for Structure-Aware Remote Sensing Segmentation
Problem
Remote sensing segmentation networks are purely discriminative, producing posteriors from data alone. This misses topological structure—how boundaries connect, regions adjacency—leading to fragmented results and poor cross-dataset generalization.
Approach
Proposes a hybrid generative-discriminative model where a MeanFlow-based generative prior learns semantic topology as a continuous stochastic field. Prior-to-Affinity Projection converts topology into class-conditional affinities; Dynamic Affinity-driven Segmentation leverages these for coherent inference.
Why it matters
A structural flow prior captures what discriminators ignore: how semantic regions should connect and organize. By fusing this prior into the segmentation backbone, the model gains topology awareness and cross-dataset robustness without sacrificing discrimination.
CVPR'26ShowUI-π: Flow-based Generative Models as GUI Dexterous Hands
Problem
Existing GUI agents emit discrete click-or-type tokens, missing the smooth, continuous trajectories required for drags, scrolls, and swipes. No prior flow-based policy exists for continuous GUI action control.
Approach
Couples a vision-language-action backbone with a flow-matching action expert using cross-attention. Actions are (x, y, m) triplets; the flow model learns direct rectified-flow paths from Gaussian prior to target action distributions, trained with direction-consistency regularization.
Why it matters
A flow prior over continuous GUI actions inverts the token paradigm: instead of predicting discrete coordinates, the model samples smooth trajectories from a learned action distribution. The flow enables faithful reproduction of drags and scrolls at 450M scale, outperforming much larger discrete agents.
CVPR'26Fine-Grained GRPO for Precise Preference Alignment in Flow Models
Problem
Aligning generative flow models with human preferences is coarse-grained; current methods score entire generations without capturing fine-grained preference signals needed for precise model tuning.
Approach
Refines Group Relative Policy Optimization for flow models by decomposing preferences at multiple levels (tokens, regions, subsequences) and applying GRPO to these fine-grained units rather than whole outputs.
Why it matters
A flow prior trained with fine-grained preference signals learns nuanced alignment. By breaking preferences into sub-sequence units, the model captures what coarse GRPO misses: local preference patterns that compound into globally aligned generation.
