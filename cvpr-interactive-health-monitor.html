Decision math

Interactive Health Monitor is not just a screen of labels. It is a measurement problem: the page is trying to decide whether a claim about the conference system should be trusted under pressure. The page summary says: Health probes for every sealed interactive command-center surface plus the full-stack validation report.

Hidden quantity

The quantity underneath this page is validity: whether the page is measuring the behavior it claims to measure, rather than a nearby proxy that is easier to count.

Evidence

Look for independent records: rebuild logs, replay receipts, package tests, link checks, fixture hashes, and command outputs. Each record is a noisy measurement of the same hidden truth.

Decision rule

Combine evidence by asking what would have to be false for the page to pass while the system is broken. The more independent facts that must all be wrong, the stronger the validation claim.

Failure test

Validation fails when a test checks the report instead of the underlying behavior. It also fails when a missing artifact is treated as success because there was no row where the failure could appear.

Fundamental principles at play: mle · information theory · uncertainty. These are the same mathematical ideas used in the math pages, but here they are attached to operational decisions: what is measured, how uncertain the measurement is, and what action the evidence permits.
