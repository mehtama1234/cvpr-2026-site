The one machine

This site pulls apart modern computer vision one primitive at a time — 27 of them, each with its own first-principles story, a real run, and the papers. Step back and they are not 27 separate tricks. They are one machine: pixels come in on the left, flow through parts that see, compress, hold, and imagine, and come out the right side as a decision a robot can act on. And the same few ideas — routing, matching, refining, discretizing — keep reappearing in part after part. This page is the map.

The one-sentence version. A giant sequence model (engine room) is pointed at pixels to build perception, squeezed into compact representations, taught to hold a 3D world and to imagine new views of it, and finally wired to a body that acts — and the wiring between every stage is made of the same handful of moves.

The recurring engines

The deeper payoff of learning these as primitives: the same ideas surface in theme after theme, wearing different clothes. Once you can see them, a new paper is rarely new — it is a known engine pointed at a new problem. Eight that show up everywhere:

The vision → robotics inversion

Every theme has a twin life. In vision it makes a picture; point it at a robot and the exact same machinery makes a decision. A world model dreams video — or dreams practice a policy learns inside. RL aligns a generator to taste — or teaches a robot to walk. Object slots separate things to reason about — or things to grasp one at a time. A graph reasons over a scene — or over a robot's own body. The primitive never changes; only the question you ask it does.

Routing & conditional compute

You cannot afford to run all of a giant model on every input — so learn to use only the part you need. Mixture-of-experts routes each token to a few sub-networks; mixture-of-LoRA routes between small skill adapters; attention itself is soft routing, deciding what to read. Bigger capacity, same bill.
↳ Mixture of expertsMixture of LoRAAttention

Matching as the whole trick

Again and again, the learning signal is an assignment. DETR matches each prediction to one object, one-to-one, and duplicates vanish. CLIP matches each image to its caption against all the wrong ones. Flow matching pairs noise to data. Decide what pairs with what, and the loss writes itself.
↳ DETR / set predictionCLIPFlow matching

Refine, don't guess in one shot

The strongest generators refuse to produce the answer at once — they start from noise and iterate. Diffusion removes a little noise per step; flow matching follows a smooth path from noise to data. Trade many cheap steps for one impossible leap.
↳ DiffusionFlow matchingAutoregressive

Discretize, then predict

Turn a messy continuous thing into a small vocabulary and every language-model tool suddenly applies. VQ tokenizes an image; autoregression then writes those tokens one by one, exactly like words. The same move lets one transformer read and write pictures.
↳ Tokenizing / VQAutoregressiveAttention

Collapse is the recurring enemy

The failure mode keeps the same shape across themes: everything caves to one answer. GANs mode-collapse to a single image; self-supervised encoders collapse to a constant; detection queries collapse onto the same object; too-few object slots merge into one; a reward-hacking policy collapses onto the loophole; test-time adaptation over-adapts into nonsense. Half of every method is a trick to stop the collapse.

Learn without labels

Labels are scarce; raw pixels are infinite. So invent a task the data answers itself — predict a hidden patch, match an image to its own caption, tell real time-order from reversed, or let a big teacher label a mountain of unlabelled data for a small student. The supervision comes free from the structure of the world.
↳ Self-supervised learningCLIPVideo & time's arrowKnowledge distillation

Know what you already know — and what you don't

The cheapest knowledge is the knowledge you don't have to learn. Bake a symmetry in and rotation comes free instead of from data. And the flip side of confidence is doubt: a model that measures its own uncertainty knows when to trust itself, flag the unfamiliar, or ask. Priors you build in, and limits you admit — both are knowledge the raw data never had to teach.

All 27, one line each

The whole set, as a launchpad. Each links to its why page — the plain-language, first-principles argument grounded in a real run; from there you can reach that theme's in-the-wild survey, its sandbox, and its papers.

Attention

Let every piece look at every other — the operation under almost everything.

State space / Mamba

Mix a whole sequence in one linear pass — attention's O(n²) rival.

Mixture of experts

Route each token to a few expert sub-networks — scale without paying for all of it.

Mixture of LoRA

Swap in small adapters and route between them — many skills, one frozen base.

Knowledge distillation

Pour a big teacher's knowledge into a small fast student via its soft answers.

Equivariance

Bake a symmetry in so rotating the input rotates the answer — free generalization.

Graph neural networks

Let each node learn by passing messages to its neighbours — reasoning on connections.

Optical flow

Measure how every pixel moved between two frames.

Video & time's arrow

Learn the direction of time — what comes next, and why order matters.

Segment anything

Cut out anything you point at, zero-shot.

DETR / set prediction

Detection as guessing a set — no anchor grid, no clean-up pass.

3D from an image

Lift a flat photo into depth and geometry.

Object-centric / slots

Split a scene into slots that compete — one representation per object.

CLIP

Pull matching image and text together into one shared space.

Self-supervised learning

Learn the structure of images with no labels at all.

Tokenizing / VQ

Turn an image into a short vocabulary of discrete tokens.

Test-time adaptation

Keep learning from the unlabelled test stream after the world shifts.

Gaussian splatting

A scene as a cloud of little blobs you splat to the screen.

NeRF / neural fields

A scene stored inside a network, rendered by marching rays.

Diffusion

Make an image by removing noise, a little at a time.

Flow matching

Generate by following a straight-line flow from noise to data.

Autoregressive

Write an image one piece at a time, like a sentence.

Adversarial

A generator and a critic locked in a duel.

World models

Learn a simulator of the world, then dream futures and plan inside them.

Uncertainty & calibration

Make confidence mean what it says; flag what the model has never seen.

Vision-language-action

One model that sees, reads, and acts — a robot policy.

Reinforcement learning

Learn from reward by trial and error — the native way to acquire skill.
