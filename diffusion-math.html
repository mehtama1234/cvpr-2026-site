Diffusion — why it works, mathematically

Sampling is impossible in high dimensions. Make the leap possible by learning to reverse a known forward process—turn one giant jump into a million tiny, learnable steps.

The problem, as a quantity

Drawing a sample from p(x) directly is intractable in high dimensions—there is no closed-form way to ask 'what does the data distribution look like?' and get an answer. Generative models dodge this impossibility. Diffusion dodges it a different way: instead of trying to sample from p(x) all at once, you sample from something trivial (pure Gaussian noise), then learn how to undo a noising process step by step. The genius is that the noising process is known and invertible—you only have to learn the reverse. But reversing requires the score, the gradient that points toward high-probability regions. And the score is not free—you must learn it from data.

The core ideas — and why they work

The forward chain: turn data into noise by a known path

x_t = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1-ᾱ_t)·ε, where ε ~ N(0,I) and ᾱ_t goes 1 → 0 as t goes 0 → 1
The forward process is a closed-form way to corrupt any image to any noise level without simulating steps. This formula is a linear blend: at t=0 you have pure signal; at t=1 you have pure noise; in between, you interpolate smoothly through Gaussian space. The coefficients √ᾱ_t and √(1-ᾱ_t) sum in quadrature to one, so they stay normalized. The signal and noise are orthogonal (signal points in one direction, noise in another), so you corrupt without destroying the structure—the image remains a valid point in the ambient space, just fuzzy. By choosing the schedule ᾱ_t, you control how fast corruption spreads. This is the foundation: because you can jump straight to any noise level, you can train the network on all of them in parallel, not step-by-step.

The score: the gradient of the log probability

∇_x log p_t(x) is the score. By Tweedie's formula: E[x₀ | x_t] = x_t + σ_t² · ∇_x log p_t(x_t). Therefore: ∇_x log p_t(x) ∝ (x₀ - x_t) / σ_t²
The score is not something you can compute—log p_t(x) is the log of a distribution you do not have a formula for. But here is the rub: if you know how to reverse the forward process, you must know the score. And if you know the noising formula, you can derive what the score must be using Tweedie's formula, a fact from probability theory. Rearranging, the score is proportional to the difference between the clean image x₀ and the noisy image x_t, divided by the noise level. In other words: the direction of highest probability is always back toward the data. Learning the score means learning to identify, from a corrupted image, what the clean image was underneath. Same primitive elsewhere: this score, ∇ log p, is identical to the gradient of the energy function in energy-based models—the two fields are solving the same problem from opposite angles. Diffusion learns it via denoising; energy models learn it via a contrastive loss. The score is the shared primitive.

Predict noise, not the probability

Train ε_θ(x_t, t) to predict the noise ε. Since σ_t² · ε = x_t - √ᾱ_t·x₀, predicting ε is equivalent to predicting the score up to a scale factor.
You cannot optimize the score directly—you have no ground-truth scores. But you have noisy images and you know the noise that went into them (at least during training). So the network predicts noise instead: it sees x_t (the noisy image) and t (the noise level) and learns to output ε (the Gaussian noise that was mixed in). This is a supervised task—you corrupt an image, feed it to the network, and compare its prediction to the actual noise. The beautiful part: this prediction is equivalent to the score up to a scale. The network is learning two things at once—what the data looks like, and how to move toward it. The loss ‖ε − ε_θ(x_t, t)‖² is simple L2 regression, but because the network sees the data at every noise level (via random t sampling), it learns to denoise across all scales. This is a hidden form of data augmentation: one image, many corruptions, many training examples from one pair.

Reverse the SDE: run noise backwards

Reverse SDE: dx = [f(x,t) - g(t)² · ∇_x log p_t(x)] dt + g(t) dW, sampling t from 1 down to 0
The forward process is a stochastic differential equation (SDE) that adds noise over time. Probability theory says you can run any SDE backwards if you know the score. The reverse drift flips sign and subtracts the score direction; at each reverse step, you add a tiny bit of fresh Gaussian noise (the dW term), then move in the direction the score points—back toward data. Over many steps, the noise averages to nearly zero and the score dominates, pulling the sample toward high-probability regions. In practice, this means: start with pure noise, then repeat 'subtract a scaled score direction, add a tiny Gaussian bump' for thousands of steps. The result is an image sampled from p(x). The discrete version is intuitive: x_{t-1} = x_t - (step size) · ∇ log p_t(x_t) + √(step size) · z, where z is fresh Gaussian. Same primitive elsewhere: this SDE reversal (Reshape a Gaussian into your data distribution by running a learned vector field) is the same core primitive carrying normalizing flows, neural ODEs, flow matching, and energy-based models. The vector field changes (one learns a score, another learns a velocity), but the structure is identical: start simple, flow backward through a learned landscape, land in data.

Training loss: match predictions to ground truth

ℒ = E_{t, x₀, ε} [‖ε - ε_θ(x_t, t)‖² ], where x_t = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1-ᾱ_t)·ε
The training loop is straightforward: pick a random timestep t, corrupt the image to x_t using the known forward formula, feed it to the network along with t, and compare the predicted noise ε_θ(x_t, t) to the true noise ε. Minimizing this loss is provably equivalent to minimizing the reverse KL divergence—the gap between your learned distribution and the data distribution—so you are not just fitting randomly, you are fitting to the right objective. Weighting the loss by time (higher weight on high-noise steps, or vice versa) changes what the model prioritizes; this is a knob for trading off coarse structure against fine details.

The approaches, as variations on the math

Text-to-image: inject the text embedding into the score network via cross-attention, so the denoising learns to steer toward images that match the caption. 3D reconstruction: train diffusion over a 3D representation (voxel grid, neural field, or mesh) and condition on multi-view images, using the score to reverse the rendering process—the model learns to solve the inverse problem of 'given images, find the 3D shape that projects to them'. Editing and inverse problems: freeze the learned score network and optimize the image directly, adding a task-specific loss (e.g., semantic segmentation loss) and letting the score guide you to stay on the learned data manifold. Using the score as a metric: in downstream tasks (classification, detection, quality assessment), treat the score itself as a learned similarity measure—how much do I have to denoise this image to make it look like a different one? Robot learning: treat action sequences as high-dimensional objects to denoise, conditioning on observations. The score learns to correct trajectories away from collisions and toward task success. Accelerating sampling: use ODE solvers (deterministic, fewer steps), distill the slow network into a fast one, or learn a faster noise schedule. All trade quality for speed.

A worked numeric example

If clean data is x₀=1, noise is ε=-0.5, and α_t=0.64, the noised point is x_t=√0.64·1 + √0.36·(-0.5)=0.5. Training asks the model to infer the noise that was added. Sampling runs that inference backward.

1Making pixels from meaning
x_t_pixel = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1-ᾱ_t)·ε applied directly to raw pixel space without intermediate VAE encoding; the diffusion network learns the noising schedule and target resolution jointly
Instead of compressing to latent space first, these methods run the entire forward/reverse chain on pixels, trading memory for end-to-end learning. The noise schedule √ᾱ_t and √(1-ᾱ_t) adapt to pixel statistics rather than latent ones, allowing the model to co-optimize data augmentation and architecture for native high-resolution output.
UltraFlux: Data-Model Co-Design for High-quality Native 4K Text-to-Image — Jointly optimizes training data distribution and model capacity to minimize the gap between text-conditioned diffusion output and pixel-perfect image quality.
PixelDiT: Pixel Diffusion Transformers for Image Generation — Eliminates the VAE bottleneck by diffusing directly in pixel space, applying x_t = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1-ᾱ_t)·ε at full resolution rather than in a compressed latent.
StreamDiT: Real-Time Streaming Text-to-Video Generation — Maintains a sliding denoising buffer where each frame occupies a different timestep t, advancing the buffer in parallel to generate video frames at native speed.
LoL: Longer than Longer, Scaling Video Generation to Hour-Long — Resolves positional encoding aliasing by factoring frame indices orthogonally from temporal position embeddings, preventing the periodic collisions that break long-sequence coherence.
2Lifting a 2D prior into 3D
ε_3D = Reproject(ε_2D, Depth_map, Camera_pose); reverse diffusion lifts 2D noise predictions into consistent 3D by conditioning on depth and viewpoint transformations
A pretrained 2D video diffusion model already knows temporal coherence and lighting. By injecting estimated depth and camera geometry, the reverse process denoises noise into 3D structure. The depth acts as a learnable prior that anchors the 2D predictions to a 3D manifold, so the model generates consistent views without explicit 3D training data.
DIFF4SPLAT: Repurposing Video Diffusion Models for Dynamic Scene Generation — Conditions the reverse diffusion SDE on multi-view observations by treating the video model as a pre-trained prior for x_t that respects both temporal smoothness and 3D geometric consistency.
Gen3R: 3D Scene Generation Meets Feed-Forward Reconstruction — Embeds a feed-forward reconstruction encoder into the diffusion decoding loop, using it to map noisy intermediate predictions x_t back into 3D latent space for consistency.
MeshFlow: Efficient Artistic Mesh Generation via MeshVAE and Flow-based — Performs diffusion in a compact continuous mesh latent space via a learned VAE, avoiding discretization by directly flowing ε into mesh parameter gradients.
CUPID: Generative 3D Reconstruction via Joint Object and Pose Modeling — Augments the standard noise prediction ε_θ(x_t, t) to jointly output both geometry and pose parameters, factoring the inverse problem into two coupled denoising objectives.
3Editing and inverse problems
∇_t L = ‖x_t − x_t_target‖² where x_t_target is computed from the degraded observation via x_t = √ᾱ_t·x_obs + √(1-ᾱ_t)·ε; inversion-free methods skip solving for ε and instead gradient-step x_t directly toward the constraint
Rather than invert to find the original noise, edit-by-inversion methods directly optimize the noisy latent x_t so that when denoised it matches both the degradation model and a text prompt. The gradient ∇_t L pulls x_t toward a solution that satisfies the constraint, so denoising naturally produces an edited image that respects the instruction.
FlashIn: Fast and Accurate Image Inversion for Real-time Image Editing — Directly optimizes the initial noise z via gradient descent on a reconstruction loss ‖y - G(x_t(z))‖², replacing full inversion with fast iterative refinement.
Delta Rectified Flow Sampling for Text-to-Image Editing — Compares two trajectories under the flow-matching velocity field v_θ(x, t) — one for the original prompt and one for the edited prompt — to extract only the delta without full inversion.
SliderEdit: Continuous Image Editing with Fine-Grained Instruction Control — Injects low-rank adapter weights into token embeddings h_i, allowing fine-grained steering of the cross-attention score s_ij = (q_i · k_j) / √d during the reverse process.
UniLDiff: Unlocking the Power of Diffusion Priors for All-in-One Image — Unifies multiple degradation types under one latent-diffusion framework by conditioning on a degradation-type token c_deg, amortizing the loss E[‖ε - ε_θ(z_t, t, c_deg)‖²] over all inverse problems.
4The generator as a perception engine
y_pred = Reverse(x_t = noise, conditioning_signal); treat any vision task (depth, pose, segmentation) as a denoising problem where the model learns to denoise task-specific labels instead of pixels
A diffusion backbone already learns rich hierarchical features through image generation. By retraining it to denoise the target signal (geometry, labels, poses) conditioned on input images, the model transfers the learned inductive bias to perception. The forward corruption x_t = √ᾱ_t·y + √(1-ᾱ_t)·ε now applies to task outputs, not pixels, giving a unified framework for any prediction.
UniPercept: A Unified Diffusion Model for Generalizable Visual Perception — Reframes depth, segmentation, and pose estimation as multi-task generation by treating each task's output as a continuous latent that the diffusion model learns to denoise.
Visual Diffusion Models are Geometric Solvers — Applies the reverse-SDE solver f_reverse(x,t) = f(x,t) - g(t)² ∇_x log p_t(x) to geometry outputs, treating the score ∇_x log p_t(x) as the gradient of a geometric constraint.
StableMTL: Repurposing Latent Diffusion Models for Multi-Task Learning — Retrains a frozen, pretrained latent diffusion backbone to output task-specific predictions y instead of images, amortizing multi-task structure into a single ε_θ network.
FE2E: From Editor to Dense Geometry Estimator — Bootstraps from an image editing model that already understands image-to-image correspondence, lifting its cross-image matching attention into 3D geometric constraints.
5Denoising a robot into action
a_t = Reverse(ε_t, state, constraint); diffusion reverses random action noise into trajectories that satisfy dynamics and collision constraints via the learned score ∇_x log p_t(a_t)
Robot policies face the same problem as image generation: they must cover multiple valid behaviors. Instead of regressing to a single action, diffusion models the full distribution of good trajectories. The reverse diffusion process integrates the learned score (equivalent to the gradient of the dynamics log-probability), pulling noisy random action sequences into smooth, feasible paths conditioned on the current state.
The Ingredients for Robotic Diffusion Transformers — Strips down the large-scale image diffusion Transformer to its essential components for action: temporal embeddings, action-dimension normalization, and sparse attention over trajectory steps.
Diffusion-Based Approximate MPC: Fast and Consistent Imitation of Multiple Modalities — Trains the diffusion model to sample from the full distribution of MPC solutions π(a_t | s_t) rather than regress to a single action, recovering multimodal behavior via the reverse SDE.
Full-Order Sampling-Based MPC for Torque-Level Locomotion Control via Diffusion — Recognizes that MPPI sampling obeys ∇_a ∇_a log π(a) = -a / σ², deriving that a diffusion step with score proportional to -ε / √(1-ᾱ_t) is exactly one MPPI update.
PRESTO: Fast Motion Planning Using Diffusion Models Based on Key-Configurations — Trains the diffusion model to generate full trajectories τ = {x_0, x_1, ..., x_T} in one pass, with trajectory smoothness and collision-free confidence encoded in the prior p_data(τ).
6The race for fewer steps
x_{t-Δt} ≈ x_t + Δt · ∇_x log p_t(x_t); fewer-step methods use better ODE solvers (Runge-Kutta, geometric integration) or distill the score into a simpler model that requires fewer evaluations
Standard diffusion requires many reverse steps to integrate the SDE. High-order ODE solvers (RK2, RK4) respect the geometry of the probability flow, letting fewer steps stay on track. Distillation methods train a smaller student network to mimic the teacher score, so each step does more work; combined, these cut the forward pass count from 50 steps to 1-4 with minimal quality loss.
MeanFlow Transformers with Representation Autoencoders — Shrinks the working space via an encoder z = Φ(x), diffuses in the compact z-space to reach one-step generation, and reconstructs via x = Ψ(z).
Phased DMD: Few-step Distribution Matching Distillation via Score Matching — Partitions the denoising trajectory into phases where early steps (high noise) approximate structure and late steps (low noise) refine detail, applying phase-specific distillation to each.
GeoRK2: Geometry-Guided Runge-Kutta Integration for Diffusion Transformers — Replaces Euler ODE integration with a second-order Runge-Kutta solver adapted to the curvature of the score manifold ∇_x log p_t(x), reducing discretization error.
FlowNav: Combining Flow Matching and Depth Priors for Efficient Navigation — Swaps iterative diffusion sampling for Conditional Flow Matching with a learned velocity field v_θ(x, t) that traces nearly straight paths from noise to action in fewer steps.
The through-line. Diffusion inverts the curse of dimensionality by splitting one impossible task (sample from an unknown high-dimensional distribution) into a long chain of simple tasks (denoise at each scale). The score is the compass; the schedule is the map. You learn to reverse a known forward process by predicting the noise at each level, then sample by running that reversal in reverse. Every variant—text conditioning, 3D lifting, inverse problems, robot policies—is just a way to twist the score toward something more useful than general-purpose denoising. The underlying idea is ancient: if you know how to mix two things (noise and data), you can learn to unmix them.
