Bend a Gaussian into your data

The why page says a flow reshapes a simple Gaussian into the data through a stack of invertible layers. Here you can turn the one knob — how many layers — and watch a Gaussian cloud (grey) get warped, reversibly, toward a two-moons target (gold). Every point keeps its identity: the map only re-arranges them, never merges them.

← All themesThe big pictureThe mathThe papers
What to notice. At 0 layers you see the raw Gaussian blob. Each layer applies a reversible shear — it moves one coordinate as a smooth function of the other — so the cloud folds and stretches a little at a time. With too few layers it cannot bend enough to reach the crescent shape; add more and it wraps onto the two moons. Crucially the transformation never collapses two points together (it stays invertible), which is exactly what lets a flow report the exact probability of any point.
