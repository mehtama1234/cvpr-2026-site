Watch a message spread — then blur

The why page says a GNN works by each node repeatedly averaging in its neighbours. Here is that process, live. Drop a signal on one node, then add rounds of message passing: watch it flow out along the edges to fill its community — and then, if you go too far, watch every node melt into the same grey. That melt is over-smoothing.

← All themesThe big pictureIn the wildThe papers
What to notice. At 0 rounds only the seeded node glows — a plain net that reads each node alone sees just this. Add a few rounds and the signal spreads along edges to light up its whole community, but barely crosses to the other one: that neighbourhood-shaped information is exactly what lets a GNN tell the communities apart. Keep going and the two communities bleed together until every node is the same dim grey — over-smoothing, where more depth destroys the very distinctions it was meant to find.
