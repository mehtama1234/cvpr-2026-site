Watch attention's bill explode

The why page says attention costs grow with the square of the sequence length, while a state-space model grows in a straight line. Here you can watch it. Slide the length up and see attention's compute and memory rocket away while the SSM barely moves.

What to notice. At short lengths the two bars are close — attention's extra power is cheap. But because attention compares every token to every other, its work and memory grow as length squared: double the length and its cost quadruples. The state-space model takes one step per token with a fixed-size memory, so it grows in a straight line and its memory never changes at all. Push the slider right and the gap becomes a chasm — which is exactly why, for a long video or a huge image, you reach for the linear-time model.
