Gaussian splatting · how the field actually uses it

A scene of blobs, everywhere — 3DGS across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued splatting from first principles: a scene as a cloud of splattable Gaussian blobs. This page shows how far that idea has spread — it is now one of the largest clusters at CVPR 2026 — and how, in a robot, the same blobs become a live map to act inside. Two things to hold onto. In vision, splatting became the default in barely two years, and the work now is efficiency, one-shot prediction, motion, generation, avatars, and language. In robotics, the same blobs are inverted into a live, metric, onboard map a robot builds while moving and then acts inside.

The uses — from a prettier render to a live map

Eight threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (make the blobs cheaper, predict them in one shot, move them, generate them, rig them to people, tag them with language); 7-8 are the robotics inversion into a live map (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Make the blobs cheaper and sharper — core quality & efficiency

Vanilla splatting wastes millions of Gaussians, grows them by a fragile heuristic, and aliases under scale changes. The largest sub-field attacks the primitives, the optimizer, and the rasterizer directly.
Mathematical move

Quantity. In 1 · Make the blobs cheaper and sharper — core quality &amp; efficiency, the recurring quantity is the finite set of 3D blobs that can render the scene.

Rule. The mathematical rule is to optimize blob position, shape, opacity, and color so projection and alpha blending match the photographs.

Why this use exists. Splatting is the same inverse-rendering problem made fast enough to become an editable map.

CVPR'26EDGS: Eliminating Densification for Efficient Convergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting — replaces the fragile grow-where-the-gradient-is-big heuristic with dense correspondence-based initialization — faster and sharper.
CVPR'26FastGS: Training 3D Gaussian Splatting in 100 Seconds — 100-1000x training speedup while keeping usable quality.
CVPR'26Turbo-GS — attacks the actual rasterizer bottlenecks (sorting and key-duplication) for a large real-time speedup.
CVPR'26Exact-GS: Mathematically Rigorous and Accurate 3D Gaussian Splatting — replaces the standard affine-projection approximation in the splat with exact rendering.

What changes when the blobs become a map

Same representation, opposite job — and the robotics constraints invert nearly every computer-vision assumption.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
When it is builtoffline, from a fixed set of posed photosonline, incrementally, from a moving sensor while pose is unknown (SLAM)
What mattershow pretty the render is (PSNR)how geometrically accurate and metric it is — the robot collides with it
Where it runsunbounded time and memoryonboard, real-time, memory-bounded (a whole sub-field just shrinks the blobs)
What consumes ita viewera planner or a safety filter — the output is an action, fused with LiDAR for scale
The through-line. In vision, 3D Gaussian splatting is a camera you point at a finished capture to get a prettier photo. In robotics, it is the map a robot grows out of its own moving eyes — metric, real-time, onboard — and then walks, grasps, and plans inside of. The same cloud of blobs; the difference is whether anyone acts on it.
