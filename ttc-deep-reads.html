1 · Sample and vote: self-consistency and multi-sample reasoning

Test-time compute strategy where models generate multiple diverse candidate answers and aggregate them via voting or consensus. Self-consistency applied to visual and multimodal domains shows that sampling many outputs and selecting the most common answer concentrates probability mass on the correct response even when reasoning is uncertain. This pattern emerges across motion trajectory generation, video reasoning, visual grounding, and adaptive chain-of-thought methods, where inference compute spent during decoding yields accuracy gains without retraining.
CVPR'26Self-Consistency for LLM-Based Motion Trajectory Generation and Verification
Problem
LLMs generate motion trajectory animations from text, but generated trajectories don't always match prompt specifications exactly. Extending self-consistency (proven in NLP) to visual domains requires defining consistency metrics for geometric shapes with allowable transformations.
Approach
Extends self-consistency via Lie transformation groups: sample diverse trajectories, model each as a prototype shape with a transformation group defining allowed variations, cluster trajectories using group-invariant distance metrics, and select the largest cluster centroid as the self-consistent generation. A hierarchy (rigid, similarity, affine) handles different shape families without supervision.
Why it matters
Self-consistency concentrates on correct outputs by aggregating diverse LLM samples; the geometric framework ensures consistency accounts for valid shape variations rather than pixel-level matching. Sampling 4-6% accuracy gain and 11.8% precision improvement in verification show the power of voting among multiple inference-time attempts.

CVPR'26VideoAuto-R1: Video Auto Reasoning via Thinking Once, Answering Twice
Problem
Chain-of-thought reasoning for video understanding increases sequence length and compute, yet many perception tasks achieve direct answering without reasoning. Existing methods struggle to determine when reasoning is necessary, wasting compute on simple tasks.
Approach
Implements thinking-once-answering-twice: model generates an initial answer, performs reasoning, then outputs a reviewed answer. Both are trained with rewards (final answer weighted higher). At inference, a confidence score on the initial answer triggers early-exit, skipping reasoning for confident predictions.
Why it matters
Dynamically sampling both reasoned and direct answers lets the model learn when each is appropriate; the confidence-based voting selects the faster path when it is safe. Average response length shrinks from 149 to 44 tokens while accuracy is preserved by letting multiple inference-time decisions inform the final answer.

Problem

Approach

2 · Visual chain-of-thought: thinking with images before answering

The frontier of multimodal reasoning trades inference tokens—explicit intermediate steps grounded in visual content—for reliability. Rather than producing single-step answers, these models learn to decompose problems hierarchically, examining images repeatedly with structured reasoning units. Visual chain-of-thought binds semantic grounding (where the model attends) to textual reasoning (what it says), enforcing faithfulness through saliency alignment. Whether solving math with dynamically cropped regions, planning robot manipulation through visual dynamics, or reasoning about spatial layout across egocentric frames, models that think visually before answering achieve both interpretability and accuracy. The key insight: intermediate visual reasoning, much like human problem-solving, requires cycling back to the image rather than reasoning purely from static embeddings.
CVPR'26VIRC: Enhancing Visual Interleaved Mathematical CoT with Reason Chunking
Problem
Extending Chain-of-Thought reasoning into multimodal large language models for mathematical tasks is challenging. Existing models either reason textually from static images without dynamic visual acquisition, or indiscriminately inject visual information at every step, causing redundancy and cognitive overload.
Approach
VIRC proposes Reason Chunking, restructuring visual reasoning into Critical Reasoning Units where each encapsulates a self-contained proposition. Within each unit, textual logic validates intermediate steps; across units, visual information is dynamically injected via three tools—crop, scale, and display—guided by which visual content supports the next proposition. A 100K-sample CRUX dataset with three reasoning paths (one correct, two plausible incorrect) trains through progressive stages: instruction-following, practice on realistic problems, then strategic RL on hard subsets.
Why it matters
This approach mirrors Miller's cognitive chunking theory: humans solve math by decomposing into 7±2 chunks of information, repeatedly examining visuals at key decision points rather than passively reasoning. By structuring reasoning into explicit units with selective visual access, VIRC achieves 18.8% average improvement on mathematical benchmarks and 9% on cross-domain high-resolution images, demonstrating that hierarchical visual thinking outperforms flat multimodal fusion.

3 · Adaptive and efficient reasoning: spend compute where it helps

The scaling paradigm has long assumed more reasoning steps and higher-capacity models yield monotonic gains. Recent work reveals the gap: unbounded reasoning chains degrade perception; uniform sampling wastes compute on irrelevant frames; fixed model capacity runs unnecessary overhead. The emerging pattern is surgical: identify where reasoning actually helps—per-sample and per-token—then allocate compute there and nowhere else. This theme spans the perceptual cost of deep reasoning, scene-aware feature adaptation, hierarchical temporal rewards, and selective frame processing: all share the principle that stopping over-thinking is sometimes better than thinking harder.
CVPR'26Deeper Thought, Weaker Aim: Understanding and Mitigating Perceptual Impairment during Deep Reasoning
Problem
Visual reasoning models suffer from a fundamental trade-off: extending chain-of-thought reasoning chains for abstract reasoning causes the model to lose visual grounding and fine-grained perception. Longer thought does not simply improve performance; it erodes the perceptual accuracy needed for robust multimodal understanding.
Approach
Quantifies the perceptual degradation phenomenon across reasoning depths and proposes selective mechanisms—attention gating and feature preservation—that let the model maintain visual detail even during multi-step reasoning. The key insight is not to reason longer everywhere, but to protect perception where it matters.
Why it matters
Over-thinking can actively degrade performance by decoupling reasoning from grounding. By preserving visual features and detecting when reasoning should pause, the method realizes that optimal depth varies per task and per sample: sometimes weaker reasoning with intact perception beats stronger reasoning with degraded vision.

4 · Verify and rerank: best-of-N with a critic

At inference time, generating many candidate outputs and selecting the best one with a scorer (not training a separate verifier model) is a simple yet powerful scaling law. This section covers best-of-N selection strategies ranging from test-time reranking with learned reward models, to training-free attribution schemes that recover fine-grained scores from coarse process rewards, to self-consistency and geometric verification for visual generation. These methods share a common theme: compute scales with inference-time sampling, allowing weaker base models to approach or exceed the performance of larger ones when paired with an effective critic or scoring function.
CVPR'26Self-Consistency for LLM-Based Motion Trajectory Generation and Verification
Problem
LLM-generated motion animations often fail to follow prompt specifications precisely. Extending the self-consistency principle (which clusters diverse NLP outputs) to visual domains requires defining geometric consistency metrics that handle shape families with allowable transformations, not just pixel-level matching.
Approach
Sample diverse LLM trajectories via diversity-encouraging prompts. Model trajectory families using Lie transformation groups (rigid, similarity, affine) to define invariant distance metrics. Cluster trajectories using these group-theoretic metrics, then select the largest cluster centroid as the consistent output or verify new trajectories against the cluster center.
Why it matters
Geometric consistency metrics account for the full family of valid shape transformations without supervision. Clustering diverse samples recovers consensus automatically, achieving 11.8% precision gain and 5.6% F1 improvement in verification over VLM baselines through unsupervised best-of-N selection.

CVPR'26Unlocking Token Rewards via Training-Free Reward Attribution
Problem
Current RL fine-tuning suffers from coarse-grained outcome rewards that lack intermediate step feedback, while process reward models provide step-level scores but remain ambiguous across hundreds or thousands of tokens per step. Token-level credit assignment is essential but requires either expensive training with noisy labels or heuristic proxies.
Approach
Introduce P2T (Process-to-Token), a training-free method that decomposes process-level rewards into token-level rewards via first-order Taylor approximation. Each token's influence is measured as the inner product of its embedding gradient and the difference between null and original embeddings, reducing attribution from O(N) forward passes to a single backward pass.
Why it matters
Training-free attribution preserves fidelity by building on existing learned process reward models, avoiding expensive retraining. Gradient-based decomposition efficiently identifies which tokens contribute most to the process score, enabling fine-grained credit assignment. Results show 11.5%–14.2% gains on mathematical reasoning tasks with ~4× faster convergence, proving that reranking via a trained process scorer with learned token attribution outperforms outcome-reward-only approaches.

5 · Training-free scaling: capability added purely at inference

Test-time compute unlocks capability without retraining: iterative refinement, multi-step reasoning, or adaptive sampling applied only at inference over frozen weights. These methods trade off latency for accuracy—extra forward passes, backprop-guided selection, or test-time parameter updates adapted per-sample via unlabeled feedback. This theme covers the inference-only frontier where models improve on distribution shifts, fine-grained tasks, or sparse-feedback control by working harder on each test input rather than learning new weights offline.

Why it matters
Adds inference-time adaptation loop: uncertainty signals guide which augmented trajectories to evaluate, and environment feedback (reward or success signal observed after rollout) retrospectively refines the policy. No weight updates, only test-input-specific trajectory reweighting.
