00the frame

The one bridge — pixels are just numbers

▸ scrub left→right: read a patch of brightness numbers into a meaning; scrub back: write meaning into pixels

This is the whole game. A camera stores the world as a grid of brightness numbers — nothing in it literally says “fox.” Half the field reads those numbers into meaning (perceive); the other half writes meaning back into numbers (generate). Watch a patch of pixels get read into a label, and a label get written into pixels.

01generation

Diffusion — an image sharpening out of static

▸ scrub the slider = move through the denoising steps; watch structure rise from noise

A generator doesn’t “draw.” It starts from pure static and, guided by a prompt, removes a little noise at every step until an image is left behind. Drag the slider slowly: the same picture is already there at every step — each step just makes it less noisy. That’s why it’s reversible, and why fewer steps (next panel) is even possible.

02step-distillation

Few-step generation — same destination, fewer stops

▸ scrub time: the 4-step path finishes while the 25-step path is still going

If the image is present at every step, can you take bigger steps? Distillation (an LCM-LoRA) teaches exactly that — a few large, well-aimed steps instead of many tiny ones. Scrub to the middle: the 4-step runner is already done; the 25-step one is halfway. Live, that was 0.55s vs 3.51s — 6.4× faster, same picture.

03data distillation

Dataset distillation — 100 images that are engineered, not picked

▸ scrub = training iterations: a few synthetic tiles get optimized while the accuracy climbs

The key misconception to kill: this is not choosing 100 good photos (random-100 does worse — we tested it). The 100 synthetic tiles start as noise and are optimized, iteration by iteration, so that a model trained on them lands where the full 50,000-image set would. Scrub and watch the tiles sharpen and the accuracy gauge climb toward ~61%. The real set (faint, behind) is the fixed target, never moved.

043D perception

Monocular depth — distance painted onto every pixel

▸ scrub the scan line: on its left, the flat photo becomes a depth map (bright = near)

One photo has no distance information — depth here isn’t measured, it’s inferred from what the network learned about the look of near vs far. Drag the scan line across: to its left the ordinary photo is replaced by the model’s depth estimate, pixel by pixel. That per-pixel depth is the raw material for 3-D and navigation.

05robustness

Adversarial — a point nudged across an invisible line

▸ scrub the perturbation: the sample barely moves, but it crosses the decision boundary

Why can a change you can’t see flip the answer? Because the model’s decision boundary sits closer than you’d think. The picture barely changes — but in the model’s space the sample slides a hair’s width across the line from “dog” into “wolf.” Live, a 4/255 perturbation flipped ResNet-50 Samoyed → “white wolf.”

06modular knowledge

Mixture-of-LoRA — two corrections fighting for the same weights

▸ scrub: first the two task-edits pile onto the same cells (collision), then route apart

Freeze a giant model; steer it with a tiny correction on a few weights. But train task A and task B separately and their edits land on the same cells — scrub the first half and watch them collide (live, 73.8% of each one’s parameters were exclusive to its task). Scrub the second half: give each its own cells (routing / sparsity) and they coexist.

07the frontier

The reasoning bottleneck — where the error actually enters

▸ scrub the pipeline: SEE → COUNT → REASON, and watch the mistake happen at COUNT, not REASON

“Think step by step” only helps if the model sees right first. Scrub through the pipeline: perception miscounts (3, truth is 5), then the reasoning does flawless arithmetic on that wrong number — and lands on a wrong answer. The error entered at perception, upstream of the thinking. That’s why the field trains grounding with reinforcement learning, not just prompting.
