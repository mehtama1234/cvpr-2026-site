The recurring primitives

Eleven mathematical ideas that surface again and again. Once you can see them, a new paper is rarely new — it is a known primitive pointed at a new problem. Each links to the themes that embody it.

Softmax — turning “pick one” into something you can differentiate

softmax(z)_i = exp(z_i) / Σ_j exp(z_j) # a smooth, differentiable argmax
Almost every time a model has to choose — which token to read, which expert to run, which caption matches, which slot owns a pixel — it cannot use a hard pick, because a hard pick has no gradient. Softmax replaces it with a temperature-controlled soft selection whose weights are positive and sum to one, so choice becomes a differentiable convex combination you can train through.

Reverse a corrupting process — many easy steps beat one impossible jump

data →(add noise)→ noise ; learn to walk it backwards, one small step at a time
Sampling a rich distribution in one shot is intractable. So define a process that gradually destroys structure (Gaussian noise, or a straight-line transport) and learn to undo one small step; composing many easy, well-conditioned steps inverts a distribution no single network could. Diffusion reverses a stochastic process; flow matching integrates a deterministic one.

Low rank & compression — the useful signal lives in few dimensions

ΔW = B·A with rank r ≪ d ; a fixed-size state ; a short codebook
Over and over, the thing that looks high-dimensional is really low-dimensional: a fine-tune moves weights in a tiny subspace, a scene compresses to a few latents, a model's knowledge fits in a smaller student, an image reduces to a short vocabulary. Whenever the intrinsic dimensionality is small, a thin factor, a compact state, or a few codes captures it cheaply.

Matching — for sets and pairs, the loss must respect correspondence

loss = min over one-to-one assignments σ of Σ_i cost(pred_i, target_σ(i))
When the output is an unordered set or a pairing, comparing it to the target requires deciding what corresponds to what first. DETR matches predictions to objects one-to-one (which also kills duplicates); CLIP matches images to captions against all the wrong ones; slot attention assigns pixels to slots; flow matching couples noise to data. Decide the correspondence and the loss writes itself.

The gradient of an expectation of a black box

∇_θ E[R] = E[ ∇_θ log π_θ(a)·(R − b) ] # the score-function / log-derivative trick
Sometimes the objective is a sampled, non-differentiable outcome — a reward you can only try, or a loss measured at the input. The log-derivative trick turns “maximize a black-box expectation” into a gradient you can follow by weighting each action's log-probability by how much it beat a baseline. The very same input-gradient that trains a net is what an adversary follows to break it.

Propagation as smoothing — helpful a little, destructive in excess

H' = Â·H·W # multiply by the (normalized) adjacency = one step of diffusion on a graph
Averaging each element with its neighbours is a low-pass filter. A few rounds mix in useful context; too many wash out every distinction toward a single blur — this is over-smoothing in graph networks, and the same averaging underlies attention and the iterative assignment in clustering. Propagation spreads information, but repeated propagation erases it.

The degeneracy trap — every strong objective has a trivial minimizer

a shortcut that scores perfectly while learning nothing (collapse)
Push hard on almost any loss and optimization finds the cheap way out: self-supervised encoders collapse to a constant, routers send everything to one expert, codebooks let most codes die, object slots merge, a policy games its reward, test-time adaptation caves to one class, GANs mode-collapse. Half of every method is not the objective but the force that forbids its degenerate solution — a balancing loss, a stop-gradient, a leash.

Symmetry as a constraint on the function class

f(g·x) = ρ(g)·f(x) # the network commutes with the transformation
If you know the world has a symmetry — a rotated object is the same object, relabelled nodes are the same graph — you can bake it into the architecture instead of learning it from data. Enforcing that the model commutes with the group means one example teaches the whole orbit, so generalization across the symmetry is exact and free, not approximated from millions of augmentations.

Underdetermination — when one observation cannot pin the answer

one equation, two unknowns # so add a prior, a second view, or a set of hypotheses
Many vision problems are ill-posed: one photo cannot fix metric scale, one pixel's intensity cannot fix 2D motion (the aperture problem), one click cannot fix which object you meant. The answer has more degrees of freedom than the data constrains. The cure is always the same shape — add a second viewpoint, a smoothness prior, or represent the answer as several scored hypotheses rather than one averaged guess.

Differentiable rendering — put the physics in the loss

C(ray) = Σ_i T_i·α_i·c_i # alpha-composite along a ray; the whole map is differentiable
If the forward map from a 3D representation to a 2D image — the physics of light along a ray — is written differentiably, then images themselves become the supervision for the 3D world. Gradient descent carves a neural field (NeRF) or a cloud of Gaussians (splatting) until its renders match the photos. The same projective geometry, run forward, is what makes depth recoverable.

Factorize the joint by the chain rule

p(x) = ∏_i p(x_i | x_<i) # any joint distribution, exactly, as ordered conditionals
A whole image or trajectory is an enormous joint distribution you cannot write down — but the chain rule factors it exactly into a product of one-piece-at-a-time conditionals. That turns generation into repeated prediction (autoregression) and a world model's future into a rollout of one-step predictions. The price is that errors in early pieces condition all the later ones, so the sequence drifts.

The deepest through-line. Nearly all of it is one meta-move: take something you cannot do directly — pick discretely, sample an intractable distribution, optimize a black box, satisfy a symmetry, invert physics — and replace it with something smooth and differentiable that gradient descent can climb. The softmax, the reverse process, the score-function trick, the differentiable renderer, the group constraint are all ways of making the impossible thing differentiable. And wherever you do, a degenerate shortcut appears that you must actively forbid. Optimization by gradients, and the fight against its cheap solutions — that is the whole game, across all 27 themes.
