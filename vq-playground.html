Snap it to the nearest word

The why page turns an image into tokens by snapping each patch to the nearest word in a codebook. Here is that snapping, live and in two dimensions. Each dot is an encoded patch; each ring is a word in the codebook; every dot takes the colour of its nearest word. Change the vocabulary size, and flip between a codebook that lets words die and one that revives them.

The collapse, in miniature. A naive codebook starts its words bunched together, so the words on the edge grab everything and the ones buried inside never win a single patch — they go grey and dead. You are paying for a big vocabulary and speaking in a fraction of it, and the far clusters get crudely quantized. This is exactly what happens to a VQ-VAE's dictionary on real images.
Reviving the dead words. Now every word that wins no patches is picked up and dropped back into the thick of the data. Run it and the vocabulary spreads to cover every cluster — almost no dead words, and the average snapping error drops. Same trick the real tokenizers use (EMA updates plus dead-code reset) to keep the dictionary alive.
