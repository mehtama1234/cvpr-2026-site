Autoregressive generation · how the field actually writes pixels

One piece at a time, everywhere — autoregressive generation across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the idea from first principles: build the output one piece at a time, each conditioned on all before it, the way a language model writes a sentence — powerful and unifying, but sequential (slow) and prone to drift. This page shows how far that reaches. In vision it is the second-largest generative paradigm behind diffusion, and almost every paper is a response to those two costs; in a robot, the same next-token recipe writes an action. Real papers for every use, from the analyzed corpora.

Two things to hold onto. In vision, AR wins on unification (one model reads and writes) and per-token efficiency, but keeps borrowing diffusion's parallelism and self-correction to close the gap. In robotics, the same recipe hands a policy in-context learning, scaling, and one unified model — at the cost of a latency bottleneck a moving robot cannot ignore.

The uses — from a GPT that writes pixels to a policy that writes actions

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (next-scale, parallel/masked, continuous-token, video/3D, unified, faster/self-correcting); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into next-token action (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Coarse thumbnail, then finer — next-scale prediction

Classic AR predicts thousands of tokens one-by-one in raster order — slow, and with no global blueprint. The VAR recipe replaces next-token with next-scale: generate the whole image at a tiny resolution first, then predict each higher-resolution residual conditioned on all coarser scales. Far fewer steps, global structure early — the dominant efficient AR recipe.
Mathematical move

Quantity. In 1 · Coarse thumbnail, then finer — next-scale prediction, the recurring quantity is the next piece conditioned on all previous pieces.

Rule. The mathematical rule is to factor a hard joint distribution into a chain of next-step predictions.

Why this use exists. The method is powerful because each local prediction is simple, and fragile because early errors feed the future.

CVPR'26Markovian Scale Prediction: A New Era of Visual Autoregressive Generation — makes next-scale Markovian (depend mainly on the immediately coarser scale) for a leaner, faster generator.
CVPR'26FVAR: Next-Focus Prediction for Visual Autoregressive Modeling — swaps the downsampling pyramid for a defocus-to-focus pyramid, giving alias-free coarse-to-fine generation.
CVPR'26PointNSP: Autoregressive 3D Point Cloud Generation with Next-Scale Level-of-Detail Prediction — ports next-scale prediction to 3D point clouds, matching diffusion quality while staying permutation-invariant.

2 · Many tokens at once — parallel & masked AR

AR's core weakness is strict sequentiality: N tokens = N forward passes. This breaks that by predicting many tokens at once — masked-token modeling that fills many positions per step, row/grid-parallel decoding exploiting 2D structure, or multi-token heads — for diffusion-like parallelism with a token generator.

3 · Drop the codebook — continuous-token AR

Standard AR quantizes the image into discrete VQ tokens for classification-style next-token prediction, but quantization throws away detail and caps quality. This keeps the AR backbone but predicts continuous tokens with a small diffusion or flow head per token — diffusion-level fidelity from an AR generator.

7 · Write the action like the next word — AR policies

Robotics inverts the target: point the exact next-token recipe at CONTROL. Turn continuous motion into a small vocabulary of action tokens, and let a transformer predict them autoregressively — the robot inherits the whole LLM toolkit. The catch: token-by-token is slow, so a real-time cluster shrinks the action vocabulary so a big model still hits control rates.

8 · Read a few demos, continue the pattern — in-context imitation

The most literal transplant of the LLM recipe: just as an LLM does a new task from a few examples in its prompt, these stuff a handful of demonstration trajectories into the context and autoregressively predict the next action for the new situation — no gradient steps at test time. How you tokenize the actions makes or breaks stability.

9 · Roll out the plan, but fast — trajectory & parallel decoding

The action being generated is a whole trajectory — a future path or driving plan — produced one step at a time. And because a manipulator cannot wait, a distinctly robotics cluster attacks the sequential decode itself: produce action tokens in parallel or compress the vocabulary to recover real-time rates. This branch barely exists in text (a chatbot can wait).

What changes when a token is a motor command

Same recipe — predict the next piece — opposite output and opposite pressure on speed.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
What a token isa patch, a scale, a frame, a mesh facean action, or a chunk of a trajectory
What it buysunification (read and write in one model) and per-token efficiencythe whole LLM playbook — in-context learning, scaling, one unified policy
The sequential bottleneckan efficiency annoyance — a slow imagea latency problem — a robot cannot wait, so parallel decoding is load-bearing
Exposure bias / driftglobal incoherence, long-video decaya compounding error is a failed grasp or a crash
The through-line. Autoregressive generation is the language-model recipe pointed at pixels or at actions: build the thing one piece at a time. In vision that buys unification but pays in sequential speed and drift, which the whole field is busy fixing. In robotics the same recipe lets a policy borrow everything a language model can do — but a robot must act now, so the slow one-token-at-a-time price becomes the thing to beat. Same sentence-writing machine; one draws, the other moves.
