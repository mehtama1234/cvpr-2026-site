The problem — from first principles

Video and world models tackle a tension at the heart of computer vision: translating between what we see (pixels) and what the world actually is (geometry, physics, causality). When you watch a scene unfold—a hand grasping an object, a car turning a corner, weather clouds sweeping across a landscape—your brain isn't just predicting raw pixels. It's reasoning about invisible forces: gravity, friction, momentum, intent. It's tracking spatial relationships in 3D even as the camera moves. This is the fundamental problem: teach machines to understand what comes next in a video by learning the hidden rules that govern how scenes change.

The difficulty is that pixels lie. Two very different physical states can look identical in an image. Motion blur, viewpoint changes, occlusions, and appearance variations all mask the true dynamics underneath. A model that memorizes patterns in pixel space will fail when conditions shift slightly—a different camera angle, a new object, a modified physics parameter. To truly predict the future, a model must learn a representation of the world's state that captures geometry and physics, not just surface statistics.

This is why world models matter for robotics, autonomous driving, and interactive systems. A system that merely generates plausible-looking video frames is not enough; it must respect 3D geometry, obey physics constraints, and handle causality in a way that transfers to real-world control and planning. The field has converged on this insight: the best video generators are implicitly world models, and the best world models must learn to generate video as a byproduct of understanding dynamics.

The families — how the field tackles it

Diffusion transformers as the backbone

The dominant architecture for both video generation and world modeling is the diffusion transformer—a hybrid of diffusion models (which gradually denoise random noise into structured outputs) and transformers (which attend to spatial and temporal relationships). Papers like Attention Surgery, ReHyAt, and VMonarch optimize the core diffusion transformer for efficiency and quality, tackling the challenge that video requires exponentially more computation than images. Attention Surgery uses global subsampling and a knapsack optimizer for token efficiency; ReHyAt reformulates attention as a chunk-wise RNN to enable on-device generation of arbitrarily long videos; VMonarch uses structured Monarch matrices to sparse attention without losing expressiveness. Meanwhile, D2Cache and DisCa accelerate inference by caching and reusing intermediate features across denoising steps, while Phased DMD partitions the denoising trajectory into subintervals to avoid oversaturation. This family solves the engineering problem: how to make video generation fast enough to be practical.

Physics and geometry awareness

A second wave of work embeds physical understanding directly into the generation process. NS-Diff enforces Navier-Stokes fluid dynamics constraints in the latent space via reinforcement learning; Physical Simulator In-the-Loop Video Generation interleaves an MPM (material point method) simulator during generation to ensure physical plausibility; Goal Force encodes forces as visual overlays so users can condition generation on explicit physics; PHANTOM integrates inference of latent physical properties into generation itself. For 3D-aware generation, Captain Safari enforces long-horizon 3D consistency via pose-aware persistent world memory; GaussianDWM unifies scene understanding and generation using 3D Gaussians with explicit spatial-linguistic alignment; GEM is the first LiDAR world model with dynamic-static disentanglement for autonomous rollout. These papers share a conviction: a video generator that ignores physics and 3D structure will fail at generalization.

Latent world models for planning and prediction

A parallel research direction learns compact latent representations of scene dynamics that support both prediction and control. Planning in 8 Tokens compresses planning-relevant information into just 8 discrete tokens, prioritizing semantics over photorealism for 100x speedup; TraceGen operates in 3D trace space (motion trajectories), enabling invariance to camera and embodiment while preserving geometry for manipulation; Motus uses optical flow as a universal action representation bridging unlabeled video pretraining and labeled robot learning; POINTWORLD unifies state and action in shared point flow representation, enabling zero-shot transfer to real robots; DreamSAC combines Hamiltonian-based world models with symmetry-aware exploration, where curiosity is driven by understanding conservation laws. VideoWorld 2 and Chain of World decouple appearance and dynamics, learning robust action representations that transfer across domains. These models assume that if you understand the hidden state and the laws of change, prediction and planning follow naturally.

Identity and appearance preservation

A major practical challenge is keeping objects and people consistent across video frames. Identity-Preserving Image-to-Video Generation uses direct facial reward-guided optimization without auxiliary modules; Lynx adds lightweight dual adapters (ID + Ref) for high-fidelity identity without full fine-tuning; AnyID uses multi-reference identity encoding with RL fine-tuning from human feedback; EvoID drives identity preservation through dual-path rewards (objective metrics plus MLLM assessment) and teacher-student stabilization. ConsID-Gen explicitly leverages multi-view guidance and unified representation alignment before diffusion; GLorIA uses character-centric content anchors with RoPE-based weak conditioning to resolve conflicts between multiple references. For pose-guided animation, MultiAnimate, DeX-Portrait, and PoseAnything all decouple identity from motion, enabling multi-character pose control without architectural retraining. The underlying insight: appearance and motion are separable problems, and solving them independently is more stable than joint optimization.

Temporal consistency and long-horizon generation

Video is inherently long—seconds to minutes of coherent frames. LoL achieves indefinite streaming video by addressing sink-collapse through multi-head RoPE jitter, demonstrating 12-hour continuous generation; ReHyAt enables arbitrarily long on-device generation through chunk-wise RNN reformulation; TempoMaster uses next-frame-rate prediction for hierarchical coarse-to-fine synthesis; Free-Lunch Long Video extends generation without explicit retraining via layer-adaptive OOD correction; Endless World combines conditional autoregressive training with detached gradient conditioning and global 3D fusion for real-time infinite generation. For multi-shot narratives, OneStory uses learnable frame selection over cross-shot memory; STAGE anchors generation to storyboards; HoloCine combines directorial precision with computational efficiency for minute-scale cinematic narratives. The common pattern: break long sequences into chunks or stages, track memory across boundaries, and use 3D or geometric priors to maintain consistency.

Control and conditioning mechanisms

Users want fine-grained control over what gets generated. ControlNet-style approaches like V-RGBX unify video inverse rendering, intrinsic-conditioned synthesis, and sparse keyframe propagation; TGT grounds trajectories in text for locally-controlled generation; FlexTraj uses unified point-based trajectories enabling dense, sparse, and unaligned control; LAMP generates both object and camera trajectories jointly from natural language via motion-as-program synthesis; Geometry-as-context interleaves geometry and RGB tokens in an autoregressive model, replacing non-differentiable 3D rendering; Taming Video Models for 3D and 4D Generation achieves trajectory control through intra-step refinement and flow-gated fusion. For temporal control, AlcheMinT assigns reference tokens synthetic temporal frequencies for fine-grained temporal blending; TEMPOCONTROL manipulates cross-attention to temporally localize specific words; SwitchCraft aligns attention to temporally-localized events through dynamic query steering. These papers recognize that predictable control requires exposing the right geometric or semantic levers.

Evaluation and safety

As generation models become powerful, measuring quality and ensuring safety matter. Ref4D-VideoBench provides fine-grained reference-based evaluation with transparent, auditable constraints; VGA-Bench unifies technical and aesthetic quality assessment with dedicated multi-task evaluators; TiViBench specifically evaluates reasoning in image-to-video models; SLVMEval comprehensively benchmarks long-form generation; SVBench evaluates social reasoning grounded in developmental psychology. On safety, Skyra detects AI-generated video via grounded artifact reasoning; TEAR red-teams temporal vulnerabilities by distributing unsafe content across frames; Anti-I2V defends images against malicious video generation via perturbations in color and frequency domains; ActivityForensics localizes manipulated activity in videos using denoising feature regularizers. SoliReward addresses reward hacking in video generation models through binary annotation and win-tie loss. This family solves a meta-problem: how to know if a video generator actually works and whether it's safe to deploy.

Robotics and embodied understanding

For robots to act in the world, they need models that predict consequences of their actions and generalize to new scenarios. RehearseVLA integrates geometry-aware world models with VLM-based rewards for safe post-training; SafeDrive combines sparse world modeling with fine-grained safety reasoning for end-to-end driving; Spatial Retrieval Augmented Autonomous Driving uses offline geographic images as complementary context; GenieDrive decouples physics prediction (occupancy) from rendering, enabling explicit physical reasoning; SRPO uses latent world representations to compute rewards from successful in-batch trajectories, eliminating external reward engineering. NIL (No-Data Imitation Learning) uses a text-conditioned video diffusion model as an on-demand reference generator, replacing the need for pre-collected demonstrations. ORV uses 4D occupancy-centric conditioning for robot video generation and sim-to-real transfer; PAM unifies sim-to-real hand-object interaction generation. These papers embody a philosophy: if a model can predict how the world changes and what actions cause those changes, it can learn to control and plan without separate task-specific training.

Where it stands

Video generation has become practical and high-quality. Diffusion transformers can now generate photorealistic 4K video, handle arbitrary-length sequences, and respond to fine-grained spatial and temporal controls. The engineering is mature: caching accelerates inference, structured attention reduces memory, and distillation brings sampling steps down to 4-8 denoising iterations. Identity preservation works. Multi-shot narrative generation works. Even physics-informed generation is no longer a fantasy—models can enforce constraints on fluid dynamics, rigid-body motion, and energy conservation without breaking the generative backbone.

What remains open is transfer and robustness. A model trained on internet video often fails gracefully on out-of-distribution scenarios: novel objects, extreme physics, unusual viewpoints, or rare interactions. World models trained on video alone struggle to discover the true latent dynamics; adding physical priors (forces, occupancy, 3D structure) helps, but the integration is still brittle. Robotics systems built on video world models don't yet reliably transfer from simulation to reality or to novel morphologies. Long-horizon planning in latent space works on toy domains but breaks on complex, real environments. And the fundamental question remains unsolved: Can a model learn the laws of physics purely from visual observation, or does it need explicit supervision of forces, masses, and constraints? The field is racing to answer that question, because the machine that can learn physics from video alone will be close to understanding the world the way we do.
