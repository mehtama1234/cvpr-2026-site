Mixture-of-experts · how the field actually routes

Many specialists, one router — MoE across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the idea from first principles: split a network into many small experts and send each input to just a few — the capacity of a giant model at the cost of a small one, if the experts divide the labor. This page shows how far that reaches. In vision, MoE became a general specialization tool; in a robot, it is how one body does many jobs that would otherwise fight each other. Real papers for every use, from the analyzed corpora.

How big it is

corpusfootprintthe shape of it
CVPR 2026 (vision)~65-75 genuine-MoE papers (~93 name it; 253 mention experts, 104 routing)shifted from a scaling trick to a specialization tool — experts own different modalities, tasks, degradations, regions
Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)~7 explicit + a ring of multi-expert policies — emerging, strongest in locomotionone body doing many conflicting jobs (skills, terrains) that fight in a dense net — gradient conflict

Two things to hold onto. In vision, the dominant use is not make it bigger but let different experts own different sub-problems so a unified model stops fighting itself. In robotics, MoE hands each skill or terrain its own slice of capacity so they stop cannibalizing each other.

The uses — from a sparser giant to a robot that can switch skills

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (modality experts, router design, load balance vs collapse, efficiency, specialization, generation); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into conflict-free multi-skill control (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Keep the senses separate — multimodal & VLM experts

When one model must juggle image tokens, text tokens, sometimes audio or 3D, a single shared network blurs the modalities together and picks up interference. These give the model modality- or task-aware experts and a router that sends each token to the right specialist, letting a VLM scale sparsely and keep its senses separate.
CVPR'26Soft Modality-Guided Expert Specialization in MoE-VLMs — scores each token's modality softly so text and image experts stop learning overlapping representations, without hard routing.
CVPR'26G2VLM: Geometry Grounded Vision Language Model with Unified 3D Reconstruction and Spatial Reasoning — pairs a geometry-perception expert with a semantic-perception expert in a shared-attention mixture, giving a VLM real 3D grounding.
CVPR'26MOON2.0: Dynamic Modality-balanced Multimodal Representation Learning for E-commerce Product Understanding — a modality-driven MoE that adapts to which modalities are present, fixing modality imbalance.

2 · The fragile heart — routing & gating design

The router is where an MoE lives or dies — bad routing wastes experts, blocks gradients, or collapses onto a few favorites. These redesign how inputs are assigned: content-aligned, RL-optimized, teacher-distilled, uncertainty-gated, drift-aware routing. The most methodologically deep cluster.
CVPR'26ERMoE: Eigen-Reparameterized Mixture-of-Experts for Stable Routing and Interpretable Specialization — reparameterizes experts in an orthonormal eigenbasis and routes by alignment — stable utilization without auxiliary balance losses.
CVPR'26Teacher-Guided Routing for Sparse Vision Mixture-of-Experts — distills routing decisions from a teacher to overcome the gradient blocking that starves unselected experts.
CVPR'26MoE-GRPO: Optimizing Mixture-of-Experts via Reinforcement Learning in Vision-Language Models — casts expert selection as an RL policy so routing escapes rigid deterministic top-K and uses more of the pool.

3 · Keep every expert working — load balance vs collapse

Left alone, routers over-use a few experts and starve the rest — collapse, symmetry, homogenization — wasting the extra capacity. This small, pointed cluster attacks utilization directly: better initialization, homogenization-breaking specialization, or removing the crude auxiliary losses that fix balance but kill specialization.
CVPR'26Enhancing Mixture-of-Experts Specialization via Cluster-Aware Upcycling — breaks expert symmetry at initialization by seeding experts from semantic clusters of the pretrained activation manifold.
CVPR'26VidPrism: Heterogeneous Mixture of Experts for Image-to-Video Transfer — designs against expert homogenization by binding each expert to a distinct temporal scale.

4 · Cheap in practice — efficiency, upcycling & deployment

MoE promises cheap inference, but real models still pay for it, and turning a dense model into an MoE (upcycling) is delicate. These cut cost by skipping low-value experts, use MoE for conditional compute, or convert and compress models.
CVPR'26MoDES: Accelerating Mixture-of-Experts Multimodal Large Language Models via Dynamic Expert Skipping — training-free skipping of low-value experts, tuned to layer importance and per-modality behavior.
CVPR'26YOLO-Master: MOE-Accelerated with Specialized Transformers for Enhanced Real-time Detection — MoE conditional compute for detection: activate more experts on complex scenes, fewer on trivial ones.
CVPR'26OneSparse: A Unified Framework for Sparse Activation Layers in Vision Models — unifies MoE and memory-based sparse layers into one design space for principled hybrid sparse-activation.

5 · One model, many jobs — specialization & heterogeneity

The single biggest use. When one model must cover heterogeneous sub-problems — many modalities to track, many degradations to restore, long-tail classes, sequential tasks — a shared backbone compromises everything. Give each sub-problem its own expert and let routing pick per input.
CVPR'26MoRE: 3D Visual Geometry Reconstruction Meets Mixture-of-Experts — routes features to domain-specific experts (indoor/outdoor/object/human) to scale a 3D geometry foundation model across heterogeneous data.
CVPR'26UETrack: A Unified and Efficient Framework for Single Object Tracking — a modular MoE lets one tracker handle RGB, depth, thermal, and event modalities without per-modality bespoke designs.
CVPR'26SemLT3D: Semantic-Guided Expert Distillation for Camera-only Long-Tailed 3D Object Detection — language-guided expert routing gives rare, safety-critical driving classes their own specialists.
CVPR'26Spectral Mixture-of-Experts for Continual Learning — spectral-routing MoE gives each sequential task specialization to resist catastrophic forgetting.

6 · Split the generative workload — generation & editing

Unified generation/editing models suffer task interference — one shared diffusion transformer must satisfy conflicting goals (generate vs edit, local vs global, many styles). MoE splits the workload so experts specialize by task, style, or modality.
CVPR'26TAG-MoE: Task-Aware Gating for Unified Generative Mixture-of-Experts — ties local routing to global task semantics, resolving interference in unified image generation and editing.
CVPR'26CARE-Edit: Condition-Aware Routing of Experts for Contextual Image Editing — routes editing expertise by content and user intent for context-sensitive edits.
CVPR'26Mixture of Style Experts for Diverse Image Stylization — dynamic routing by style characteristics so one model covers many diverse styles.

7 · Stairs and gravel without forgetting — multi-terrain locomotion

Robotics inverts the point. A legged robot must walk on flat ground, stairs, slopes, and gravel and switch gaits — but a single dense network makes them interfere (what you learn for stairs un-learns flat ground: gradient conflict). Give each terrain or gait its own expert sub-policy, routed at runtime, often from the robot's own joint history. The strongest robotics MoE use.
ROBOTICS'25MoE-Loco: Mixture of Experts for Multitask Locomotion — MoE inside RL with experts specialized per behavior, explicitly framed as mitigating gradient conflict across quadruped and biped gaits.
ROBOTICS'25VMTS: Vision-Assisted Teacher-Student Reinforcement Learning for Multi-Terrain Locomotion in Bipedal Robots — a mixture-of-experts teacher (one per terrain) distilled into a vision student, on a real biped.
ROBOTICS'25BeeTLe: Blind Terrain-aware Learned Locomotion — a multi-expert policy stores terrain-specific gaits; an RNN infers terrain from actuator history to pick the gait.

8 · Skills that stop fighting — multi-skill & VLA manipulation

A manipulation policy that must pick, place, insert, and pour — or a big VLA fine-tuned on mixed-quality data — suffers task interference: training on one skill degrades another. Give each skill or data regime its own expert and route per situation, so skills compose without stepping on each other.
ROBOTICS'25MoRE: Unlocking Scalability in Reinforcement Learning for Quadruped Vision-Language-Action Models — sparse-activated MoE-of-LoRA experts inside a multimodal LLM, trained as an RL Q-function, targeting task interference on mixed data.
ROBOTICS'25MuST: Multi-Head Skill Transformer for Long-Horizon Dexterous Manipulation with Skill Progress — parallel per-skill expert heads in one transformer, with a learned progress value as the switching signal to chain skills.

9 · Rare scenarios get their own capacity — driving & planning

A learned driving planner faces wildly different scenarios (merges, roundabouts, dense urban) and multiple modalities; a single dense model collapses onto the easy common cases. Routing to scenario or modality experts during training gives rare scenarios their own capacity and scales cleanly with data.
ROBOTICS'25Generalizing Motion Planners with Mixture of Experts for Autonomous Driving — a decoder-only planner with an MoE backbone; expert routing explicitly fixes modality collapse and reward imbalance, scaled on billions of scenarios.
ROBOTICS'25Body-Hand Modality Expertized Networks with Cross-attention — a body-expert stream and a hand-expert stream trained with an MoE-style ensemble loss and cross-attention fusion.

What changes when the jobs actively fight

Same mechanism — route to specialized experts — opposite motivation.

The through-line. Vision uses MoE to grow the brain without growing the bill — capacity is cheap if only a few experts fire per input. Robotics uses MoE to keep a body's many jobs from destroying each other — each skill or terrain gets its own slice so the gradient that improves one stops un-training another. Same router, same experts; one chases capacity, the other chases coexistence.
