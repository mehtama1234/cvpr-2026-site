Optimization landscapes and grokking — why it works, mathematically

Training can find a memorizing valley first and the real rule later. Grokking is the moment the simpler circuit finally wins.

The problem, as a quantity

A loss landscape contains many ways to fit the training set. Some are lookup tables: perfect on seen examples, useless off them. Others are compact rules: also perfect on seen examples, but correct on unseen combinations. The puzzle of grokking is temporal: why does optimization sometimes reach zero training error early, sit there for a long time, and only much later jump to high test accuracy?

The core ideas — and why they work

Train loss does not identify the rule

many θ satisfy L_train(θ)=0, but L_test(θ) differs
The training set is a finite list of constraints. Many functions can satisfy that list exactly. A memorizer stores the answers one by one; a rule learner discovers the operation that generated them. Both look identical to the training loss after interpolation. The missing information is not in L_train alone. It is in the bias of the optimizer, architecture, data coverage, and regularization toward simpler functions. Same primitive elsewhere: this is Underdetermination in optimization form — one finite dataset cannot pin down one unique function.

Weight decay changes which zero-loss solution survives

min θ L_train(θ) + λ||θ||²
Once the model can fit the data, the training loss stops distinguishing memorization from structure. The regularizer keeps speaking. Weight decay charges large, brittle parameter configurations every step, so a compact algorithmic circuit can eventually become cheaper than a memorizing one. This is why grokking is often delayed: the model first finds any zero-loss path, then the optimizer slowly moves along the flat valley toward a simpler zero-loss path that also generalizes.

A circuit forms when features align with the task symmetry

f(a,b)=a+b mod p requires equivariance to cyclic shifts
For modular arithmetic, the correct rule is not a table of pairs; it is a symmetry of the group. The same relation holds after cyclic shifts. A network that discovers Fourier-like features can represent that rule compactly, while a lookup table needs one memory per pair. The universe permits grokking because the task has hidden structure that can be compressed. No structure, no late miracle. Same primitive elsewhere: this is Symmetry as a constraint on the function class, appearing without being hand-coded.

The jump is a threshold crossing, not magic

test accuracy ≈ 1[circuit strength > memorizer strength]
The circuit can strengthen smoothly while the reported accuracy looks flat, because the final answer is discrete. Until the rule beats the memorizer on enough examples, test accuracy barely moves. Once it crosses the decision margin, many examples flip at once and the curve jumps. This connects grokking to emergence: the underlying optimization may be gradual, while the measured capability is thresholded.

The approaches, as variations on the math

Grokking studies vary the data fraction, weight decay, model size, optimizer, and task algebra to see when the solution changes from table lookup to rule. Landscape views track movement along zero-training-loss valleys. Circuit views inspect whether interpretable features, often Fourier-like for modular tasks, have formed. Scaling views ask why more data sometimes removes the delay by making the rule easier to identify earlier.

A worked numeric example

The run at analysis/grok/toy_grok.json trained modular addition over p=97. Training accuracy reached 1.0 by step 100, while test accuracy stayed around 0.40 for many checkpoints. Around step 2600, test accuracy crossed 0.968 and then reached 1.0. That gap is the point: fitting the observed table happened quickly; discovering the compact rule took longer.

The through-line. Grokking is a microscope on generalization. It shows that zero training loss is not the destination; it is a plateau containing many possible explanations of the data. Optimization, regularization, symmetry, and scale decide which explanation survives. That makes grokking the bridge between scaling laws, information bottlenecks, equivariance, and the degeneracy trap: the model must abandon the cheap memorizing solution and settle into the compact rule.
