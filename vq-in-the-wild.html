Tokenizing the visual world · how the field actually does it

Pixels into words, everywhere — visual tokenizers across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the idea from first principles: snap each continuous patch to the nearest word in a learned codebook, and a picture becomes a sequence of tokens you can model like text. This page shows how far that spread — the tokenizer has quietly become the bottleneck for generating images and video — and how, in a robot, the same trick tokenizes what it does. Two things to hold onto. In vision, tokenizing is a dominant paradigm — the make-or-break component feeding every GPT-style image generator, so it is reworked from every angle. In robotics, the same VQ trick is a promising newcomer: it turns motor commands into a vocabulary a language-model-style policy can predict, but it is not yet the mainstream way robots act.

The uses — from pixels-into-words to actions-into-words

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (better quantizers, tokenizers for generators, video, semantic tokens, compact 1D, unified multimodal); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into a vocabulary of what a robot does (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Fighting collapse — better quantizers

The classic pain of vector quantization: most of the codebook goes dead, a few words hog everything, and image quality is capped. This cluster redesigns the quantizer itself — pack the code space better, make the snap differentiable, or drop discrete codes for continuous ones.

7 · Snapping actions to words — action tokenization

Robotics inverts the target: the continuous thing is action, the exact stream of motor commands. Snap chunks of motion to a codebook of action words and a policy predicts its next move the way a language model predicts its next token — taming the many-right-ways spread of actions and reusing the whole LM toolkit.

What changes when a token is something to execute

Same VQ machinery — the codebook, the snap, the autoregressive predictor — but the meaning of a token flips.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
What a token isa patch of image — something to rendera chunk of motion — something to execute
What you generatean image or video, one visual word at a timean action or trajectory, one motion word at a time
How dominantthe mainstream — the bottleneck for all AR generationa small, emerging minority (~9 papers); diffusion policies still lead
The payoffbring the whole transformer/LLM toolkit to pixelsbring discrete action heads, next-action prediction & interpretable skill vocabularies to control
The through-line. In vision, tokenizing snaps pixels into words so a model can generate a picture; in robotics, it snaps motor commands into words so a model can generate an action — the same VQ-VAE, only the meaning of a token changes: something to render versus something to do. It is what lets a robot policy borrow the entire language-model playbook. Honest caveat: in vision this is the dominant paradigm; in robotics it is still the promising minority.
