Video & the arrow of time · how the field actually models time

Time as a first-class axis — video across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the idea from first principles: a video is time passing, not a stack of frames, and time has a direction a model can learn. This page shows how far that reaches. In vision the move is to keep a running memory of the past and spend compute only where the video changes; in a robot, the arrow runs forward into a plan — predict the future so you can act before it is too late. Real papers for every use, from the analyzed corpora.

How big it is

corpusfootprintthe shape of it
CVPR 2026 (vision)~400-450 papers (~10% of the conference) fundamentally about temporal modelingkeep a running memory of the past, spend compute only where the video changes — what changed and when
Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)~150+ papers — ~89 trajectory forecasting, ~40 world models, ~49 anticipation/temporal-policy, ~19 temporal consistencypredict a future that has not happened yet so the robot can act early

Two things to hold onto. In vision, the field stopped sampling a few frames and calling it a video — the unit of work is now what changed and when. In robotics, time is not something to describe but something to bet on: the prediction is the input the controller commits to.

The uses — from remembering time to betting on it

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (understanding, prediction, consistency, long-video memory, arrow-of-time self-supervision, efficient temporal models); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into forecasting, world models, and anticipation (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · What happened, and when — temporal understanding

A model that answers questions about a video must know not just what is on screen but when it happened and in what order — did she pour the water before or after lighting the stove? These push video-language models to localize moments, track a referred object, and chain evidence across time instead of guessing from one lucky frame.
CVPR'26Chain-of-Frames: Advancing Video Understanding in Multimodal LLMs via Frame-Aware Reasoning — makes the model cite specific frame indices in its reasoning so answers are grounded in when things happened.
CVPR'26GroundVTS: Visual Token Sampling in Multimodal Large Language Models for Video Temporal Grounding — samples visual tokens non-uniformly, concentrating on the moments a query asks about, to pin down start/end times.
CVPR'26Understanding Temporal Logic Consistency in Video-Language Models through Cross-Modal Attention Discriminability — diagnoses why video-LLMs get order wrong (blurry cross-modal attention) and intervenes at that layer.

2 · What happens next — prediction & forecasting

Anticipation separates a passive observer from an agent. These predict the future — the next frames, the next action a person takes, the path a hand or car will follow. The hard part is that the future is multi-modal (several plausible things could happen), so many model a distribution of futures rather than one.
CVPR'26Gaussian-Mixture Latent Flow for Stochastic 3D Human Motion Prediction — predicts several plausible future human motions (a mixture, not one guess), capturing genuine uncertainty.
CVPR'26Perceiving the Near, Reasoning the Distant: Coherent Long-Horizon Trajectory Prediction — splits near-future (perceived) from far-future (reasoned) so long forecasts stay physically coherent.
CVPR'26Prototypical Action Reasoning Facilitated by Vision-Language Alignment for Egocentric Action Anticipation — anticipates the next action from first-person video by reasoning over learned verb/noun prototypes.

3 · Stop the flicker — temporal consistency

Run an image model on every frame independently and the output shimmers — depth jitters, a segmented object flickers, an edited face wobbles. These add explicit temporal glue: propagating information between frames, anchoring to a reference, or regularizing motion so nothing pops in or out. Video super-resolution, editing, and 4D reconstruction are the biggest customers.
CVPR'26STCDiT: Spatio-Temporally Consistent Diffusion Transformer for High-Quality Video Super-Resolution — uses an anchor frame to keep an upscaled video structurally faithful and flicker-free, adding only 7% parameters.
CVPR'26MoCha: End-to-End Video Character Replacement without Structural Guidance — swaps a character throughout a video with no per-frame masks, leaning on the diffusion model's own temporal tracking.
CVPR'26ReScene4D: Temporally Consistent Semantic Instance Segmentation of Evolving Indoor 3D Scenes — keeps object identities stable across a changing 3D scene observed only intermittently over time.

4 · Hours, not seconds — long-video memory

A one-minute video is already tens of thousands of tokens; an hour is impossible to feed a transformer whole. These make long video tractable by forgetting well: compressing old frames into compact memory, selecting the few frames that matter, or streaming causally so the model never holds the whole thing at once.
CVPR'26FluxMem: Adaptive Hierarchical Memory for Streaming Video Understanding — an adaptive-threshold memory that automatically decides how aggressively to compress tokens as scenes change.
CVPR'26Question-guided Visual Compression with Memory Feedback for Long-Term Video Understanding — compresses frames using feedback from the question being asked, so the memory keeps what is relevant.
CVPR'26Wavelet-based Frame Selection by Detecting Semantic Boundary for Long Video Understanding — treats frame selection as signal processing, picking frames at narrative boundaries rather than uniformly.

5 · Learn from the arrow itself — temporal self-supervision

Time gives free labels: the correct order of frames, the fact that the future follows the past and not vice-versa, the ability to hide part of a clip and reconstruct it. These pretrain by exploiting that structure — and this is where the arrow of time appears most explicitly, deliberately breaking past-future symmetry to force learning dynamics, not static appearance.
CVPR'26Recurrent Video Masked Autoencoders — explicitly models temporal asymmetry — a recurrent asymmetric-masking scheme reconstructing a masked future from an unmasked past. The clearest arrow-of-time paper in the corpus.
CVPR'26Temporal Inversion for Learning Interval Change in Chest X-Rays — uses temporal inversion as a direct supervisory signal to learn order-aware representations of how a scan changed between visits.
CVPR'26A Stitch in Time: Learning Procedural Workflow via Self-Supervised Plackett-Luce Ranking — casts temporal ordering as a list-wise ranking pretext task, learning workflow order probabilistically.

6 · Spend compute where time is dense — efficient temporal modeling

Attention over a long video is quadratic and brutal. Two families dominate: state-space models (Mamba) that scan the sequence in linear time and generalize from short clips to long videos, and cross-frame token reduction — since adjacent frames are mostly redundant, drop or merge tokens before the expensive transformer sees them.
CVPR'26MS-Temba: Multi-Scale Temporal Mamba for Understanding Long Untrimmed Videos — a dilated multi-scale Mamba capturing both short bursts and long-range structure in untrimmed video, cheaply.
CVPR'26StreamingTOM: Streaming Token Compression for Efficient Video Understanding — training-free, strictly causal pre-LLM token reduction with a fixed per-frame budget for predictable streaming latency.
CVPR'26Attend Before Attention: Efficient and Scalable Video Understanding via Autoregressive Gazing — removes redundant patches before the vision encoder runs, hitting 4-100x token reduction on long video.

7 · Where the pedestrian will be — trajectory forecasting

Robotics inverts the goal: model time to guess what happens next, and act before it is too late. A robot shares space with people and cars that keep moving; to plan safely it must guess where each will be seconds from now, not where they are. The largest temporal cluster in robotics — and the futures are multi-modal, so many sample a spread of paths.
ROBOTICS'25Curb Your Attention: Causal Attention Gating for Robust Trajectory Prediction in Autonomous Driving — suppresses irrelevant non-causal agents so the prediction is not thrown off by cars that do not matter.
ROBOTICS'25GDTS: Goal-Guided Diffusion Model with Tree Sampling for Multi-Modal Pedestrian Trajectory Prediction — predicts a spread of pedestrian paths fast enough to be usable, guided by where the person is likely heading.
ROBOTICS'25Enhancing Context-Aware Human Motion Prediction for Efficient Robot Handovers — lightweight motion prediction cheap enough to run in real time so a robot can meet a human's hand mid-handover.

8 · Imagine the next state — world models

A robot picking an action wants to know if I do this, what happens — but the real world is expensive and dangerous to experiment in. So it learns a world model: a predictor of how the scene will evolve given an action, then plans by rolling that model forward and picking actions with good imagined futures. The forward arrow of time is literally the planning substrate.
ROBOTICS'25Imagine-2-Drive: Leveraging High-Fidelity World Models via Multi-Modal Diffusion Policies — a diffusion world model imagines whole future observation sequences at once so one-step errors do not snowball.
ROBOTICS'25X-MOBILITY: End-to-End Generalizable Navigation via World Modeling — an auto-regressive latent world model drives navigation and transfers sim-to-real zero-shot.
ROBOTICS'25SF-TIM: A Simple Framework for Enhancing Quadrupedal Robot Jumping Agility by Combining Terrain Imagination and Measurement — imagines the elevation map ahead so a quadruped can jump gaps and platforms.

9 · Act a beat early — anticipation & stable perception

Two threads. A policy needs temporal context — several frames of history — and, better, needs to anticipate the human's next move so the robot helps before being asked. And perception must stay stable frame-to-frame — the same car keeps its identity — or plans flicker and turn unsafe.
ROBOTICS'25PACE: Proactive Assistance in Human-Robot Collaboration Through Action-Completion Estimation — estimates how far along a human is in their subtask so the robot times its help instead of sitting idle.
ROBOTICS'25Failure Forecasting Boosts Robustness of Sim2Real Rhythmic Insertion Tasks — forecasts an impending failure mid-task and triggers a lift-and-retry before the insertion fails.
ROBOTICS'25TrackOcc: Camera-Based 4D Panoptic Occupancy Tracking — tracks occupancy consistently over time so space and identity are both maintained for the planner.

What changes when a robot must act before it happens

Same axis — time — opposite direction of use.

The through-line. A video is time passing, and its direction is real information. In vision, the win is to keep a memory of the past and reason over what changed; the model looks backward to describe a finished artifact. In robotics the arrow runs forward straight into a plan — where the pedestrian will be, what the scene will look like after this action, what the human is about to do. Vision remembers time; robotics bets on it.
