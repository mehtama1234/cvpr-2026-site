Why an edge lies about its motion

The why page showed that you can only match motion where there is 2D structure. Here is that limit you can feel. Watch a moving striped pattern through a small window: you will only ever see it move across the stripes, never along them. Set the true motion direction, then switch the window from a plain edge to a corner and watch the truth come back.

The edge lies. The grey arrow is the true motion you set. Through the window, though, the stripes only ever appear to slide perpendicular to themselves — the accent arrow — no matter which way they are really going. Point the true motion nearly along the stripes and the perceived motion barely moves at all: the along-edge component is invisible. This is the aperture problem, and it is why a single edge cannot tell you its own motion.
A corner tells the truth. Now the window holds a plaid — stripes in two directions, so their crossings are real 2D landmarks you can lock onto. The perceived motion (accent) now matches the true motion (grey) exactly, in any direction. Two directions of structure is all it takes to pin motion down completely — which is why optical flow is trustworthy at corners and guesses everywhere else.
