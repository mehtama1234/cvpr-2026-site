1 · Energy-based generation and composition

Energy-based models (EBMs) treat probability distributions as unnormalized energy landscapes: low-energy states are high-probability, high-energy states are forbidden. The key insight is additive composition—combine behaviors by summing energy functions—and sampling via energy descent. This section surveys four papers that ground energy models in motion synthesis, bimanual coordination, part-level decomposition, and physics-inspired anomaly detection, showing how energy landscapes unify discrete primitives, continuous control, and multimodal alignment.
CVPR'26Towards Decompositional Human Motion Generation with Energy-Based Diffusion Models
Problem
Human motion generation treats generation as end-to-end mapping without capturing compositional structure. Complex motions are sums of primitives, yet existing diffusion models lack mechanisms to decompose and recombine them.
Approach
Models motion as composition of energy-based primitives: each learned energy function E_i scores a motion primitive, and total motion probability is exp(−sum E_i). Diffusion process operates over primitive energies, enabling flexible combination of learned motion components.
Why it matters
Compositional structure improves generalization to unseen combinations and provides interpretable, editable motion components. Energy landscape naturally encodes primitive compatibility—low-energy combinations are preferred, high-energy combinations forbidden.

CVPR'26EnergyAction: Unimanual to Bimanual Composition with Energy-Based Models
Problem
Extending single-arm manipulation to dual-arm tasks requires bimanual training data, which is scarce. Naive concatenation fails due to arm conflicts and asynchrony.
Approach
Models each arm policy as an EBM: E_left(s,a) and E_right(s,a). Bimanual composition is E_combined(s,a_left,a_right) = E_left(s,a_left) + E_right(s,a_right) + E_interaction, where interaction energies enforce spatial-temporal coherence via constraint potentials.
Why it matters
Additive energy structure transfers pre-trained single-arm policies without retraining. Energy constraints naturally prevent arm collisions and enforce synchronized timing—lower energy for coordinated pairs, higher energy for conflicts.

2 · Score matching and distribution matching: learning the gradient of density

Modern generative models learn to generate by optimizing distribution matching and score matching—methods that sidestep intractable partition functions Z by instead learning the score, the gradient of log-density. Rather than explicitly modeling p(x) with a normalizer, these approaches learn s(x) = ∇log p(x) = −∇E(x), enabling principled training without computing Z. This theme spans diffusion-based distillation (few-step generation via subinterval score matching), reward-flow guidance (steering generation toward learned reward signals), and video restoration (amortized posterior matching against diffusion priors). The unifying thread: elegant mathematics of score and distribution matching replaces the computational burden of density normalization.
CVPR'26Phased DMD: Few-step Distribution Matching Distillation via Score Matching within Subintervals
Problem
One-step distilled diffusion models collapse to low diversity and limited fidelity. Multi-step distribution matching distillation (DMD) preserves quality but explodes memory cost and computational depth. Existing truncation strategies degrade to single-step quality.
Approach
Phased DMD partitions the signal-to-noise ratio into SNR subintervals, training a separate expert per phase. Within each subinterval, a novel score matching objective is derived without access to clean samples x₀, computing unbiased score estimates at intermediate timesteps. Phases chain via backward simulation, progressively mapping distributions p(xₜₖ₋₁) → p(xₜₖ).
Why it matters
Learning the score (gradient of log-density) within SNR phases sidesteps the full diffusion trajectory, reducing memory while the mixture-of-experts structure mirrors human learning's progression from coarse to fine. The key insight: the score itself suffices; there is no need to normalize across the full chain. Theoretical derivation of subinterval score estimation without clean samples makes this mathematically sound.

Problem

CVPR'26InstantViR: Real-Time Video Inverse Problem Solver with Distilled Diffusion Prior
Problem
Video inverse problems (inpainting, super-resolution, deblurring) demand strong priors but iterative MCMC and variational solvers run at <0.3 FPS, making them impractical. The challenge: leverage diffusion priors at real-time speed.
Approach
InstantViR frames restoration as learning an amortized posterior q_φ(x|y) minimizing KL divergence to p(x|y). This KL decomposes into likelihood (reconstruction) and prior (score matching against a video diffusion teacher). A causal-autoregressive student with intra-block bidirectional attention enables KV caching; a single forward pass restores video.
Why it matters
The score-matching term in the KL objective binds the student to the pretrained diffusion teacher's learned score, eliminating expensive iterative refinement. Amortized inference—learning to predict the posterior directly rather than optimizing per-instance—trades optimization cost for learned gradient structure. The score (gradient of the prior) captures temporal and spatial coherence without explicit iterative diffusion.

3 · Energy for the open world: detecting the unknown

Energy-based models treat detection as an inference problem where energy measures how in-distribution a point is—low energy signals familiarity, high energy flags novelty. Across open-world object detection, zero-shot anomaly recognition, LiDAR perception, and test-time adaptation, energy functions provide a unified principle for discovering unknowns: not just learning what is familiar, but learning an active representation of what is anomalous. Four papers show how energy scoring adapts across modalities and tasks, from remote sensing to autonomous driving to industrial inspection.
CVPR'26DEUS: Detecting Unknown Objects via Energy-based Separation for Open World Object Detection
Problem
Open-world object detection requires detecting unknown classes without supervision while incrementally learning new known classes. Standard approaches rely only on energy scores from known objects to spot unknowns, missing explicit unknown representations and suffering cross-task interference during memory replay.
Approach
DEUS builds two orthogonal ETF subspaces—one for known objects, one for unknowns—computing Helmholtz free energy in both. Energy-based Unknown Separation uses contrastive margins to drive unknowns away from the known-class energy landscape, while Energy-based Known Distinction uses contrastive scores across old and new classifiers to prevent interference during continual learning.
Why it matters
By modeling an explicit unknown subspace and computing energy jointly from both known and unknown representations, the detector learns what unknowns look like rather than only what they are not. Energy becomes a geometric signal in high-dimensional space, enabling principled separation under domain shift.

CVPR'26Energy Waveify and Redistribution for Test-Time Adaptation: A Control System Perspective
Problem
Energy-based models for test-time adaptation require expensive iterative Langevin or Monte Carlo sampling to estimate energy gradients, making deployment slow when processing many test instances. Current methods suffer mode collapse and convergence instability under distribution shift.
Approach
A control-system view models energy as a complex-valued wave where amplitude encodes uncertainty and phase tracks evolution. Probability current conservation redirects probability flow away from high-energy (error-prone) regions toward low-energy (accurate) ones, enabling adaptive redistribution without additional stochastic sampling.
Why it matters
Energy as a wave field allows efficient inference through deterministic probability flow, reducing adaptation time to 1/3–1/7 of sampling-based methods. The control-system framing preserves energy landscape normalization while adapting to distribution shift, making energy practical for streaming deployment.

4 · Diffusion bridges and inverse problems: the score in action

Diffusion bridges run a learned score field between two distributions—from degraded to clean, or from constraint-free to constraint-respecting. Unlike unconditional diffusion, bridge models fix their endpoint at the target domain (not noise), turning restoration and inverse problems into guided score-based transport. Four papers establish how score-field design, noise alignment, amortization, and energy-based refinement unlock sharp images, real-time video, and safe autonomous trajectories. This section shows the score field in action across vision and planning.
CVPR'26Residual Diffusion Bridge Model for Image Restoration
Problem
Image restoration via diffusion bridges requires adaptive control: naive global noise injection distorts intact regions during reconstruction, while existing methods treat all pixels uniformly without accounting for varying degradation levels. Prior unification lacks theoretical grounding, leaving the space of bridge designs uncharacterized.
Approach
Residual Diffusion Bridge Model (RDBM) reformulates the bridge via Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes and Doob's h-transform, deriving a closed-form solution where trajectories blend residual and noise using a pixel-dependent residual-to-noise ratio R(t,i,j). This ratio governs when each pixel is reconstructed, enabling spatially adaptive restoration. The framework unifies flow matching, VE/VP bridges, Brownian bridge, and OU bridge as special cases.
Why it matters
Adaptive noise perturbation steered by learned residuals preserves intact image regions while selectively reconstructing degraded ones. The unifying theoretical lens clarifies how existing methods tune a single design knob, informing new architectures. RDBM achieves state-of-the-art across eight restoration tasks by exploiting this residual modulation.

CVPR'26Resolving Endpoint Underfitting in Diffusion Bridges via Noise Alignment
Problem
Diffusion bridge models exhibit endpoint underfitting as t approaches the target: the network input follows a bridge noise profile (rising then falling) while the training objective score monotonically decreases, creating irreconcilable trend mismatch. This ill-conditioned learning caps restoration quality at the final refinement steps.
Approach
NADB (Noise-Aligned Diffusion Bridge) redesigns the stochastic interpolant so input noise and training objective noise trends align near t=0, resolving magnitude failure. A mean network then predicts the expected clean image as an intermediate step, correcting directional failures. The two components jointly address dual failure modes across the entire time horizon.
Why it matters
Noise-level consistency makes the learning problem well-posed, enabling stable training through final refinement. The mean network bridges large distributional gaps. NADB outperforms prior diffusion bridges on image deblurring, JPEG restoration, super-resolution, and translation by directly addressing root causes of endpoint degradation.

CVPR'26GuideFlow: Constraint-Guided Flow Matching for Planning in End-to-End Autonomous Driving
Problem
Generative planners enable diverse trajectories but struggle to enforce safety and physical constraints during generation. Imitative planners avoid the problem but collapse to single modes. Autonomous driving needs both mode diversity and hard constraint satisfaction.
Approach
GuideFlow uses flow matching with three constraint enforcement strategies: Constraining Velocity Field to correct predicted velocity, Constraining Flow States to steer deviating paths, and Refining Flow by Energy-Based Model to unify flow matching with learned constraint discovery. Driving aggressiveness parameterizes control for conservative or aggressive behavior.
Why it matters
Embedding hard constraints directly within generation avoids post-hoc optimization and infeasible trajectories. Energy-based refinement discovers autonomous constraints alongside learned flow dynamics. GuideFlow achieves SOTA EPDMS 43.0 on NavSim by steering the score field toward both feasible and diverse action spaces.
