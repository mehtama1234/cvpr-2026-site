Learning without labels — SSL across CVPR & Robotics

This page shows how the field actually does it — and the quiet headline is that SSL has become infrastructure: most vision papers now build on a frozen self-supervised backbone rather than train one. How big it is

Self-supervised pretraining is now the substrate the field stands on — and it is consumed far more than it is invented:

corpusfootprintthe headline
CVPR 2026 (vision)~500 SSL-relevant papers; ~100 use DINOv2/v3 by name2-3x more papers CONSUME a frozen SSL backbone than INNOVATE on the objective
Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)~140 papers (~4%) use SSL / unlabeled representation learningsupervision comes from the robot's own physics; and robots lean on CV's SSL backbones

Two things to hold onto. In vision, SSL has matured from a research topic into plumbing — DINOv2/v3 features are the default, and the frontier is fighting collapse and porting the pretext task to new modalities. In robotics, the supervision itself is different: it comes from time, motion, and the robot's own actions, not from image augmentations.

The uses — how the data grades itself

Eight recurring uses. Uses 1-5 are the vision families (predict the hidden part, match two views, predict in latent space, use the frozen backbone, fight collapse); 6-8 are the robotics cross-over (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Predict the hidden part — masked modeling

The simplest label-free task: hide a chunk of the input and make the network fill it in. A high mask ratio forces it to infer semantics (what should be there), not just copy texture — so the features it builds along the way turn out to be broadly useful.
CVPR'26Recurrent Video Masked Autoencoders — asymmetric masking (unmasked source, 95%-masked target) via recurrence — beats VideoMAE, V-JEPA2 and DINOv2 on generalist video tasks.
CVPR'26MuM: Multi-View Masked Image Modeling for 3D Vision — scales masked modeling across multiple views to learn geometric features.
CVPR'26SARMAE: Masked Autoencoder for SAR Representation Learning — million-scale SAR corpus with speckle-aware masking — the pretext task re-invented for radar imagery.
CVPR'26NeighborMAE — reconstructs neighboring Earth-observation tiles, injecting real spatial dependency into the masking task.

2 · Match two views — joint-embedding & self-distillation

Two random augmentations of one image should land at the same embedding. Pull the pair together and the encoder learns what is invariant (the object) versus incidental (the crop). The danger — and the whole art — is collapse: the trivial solution of mapping everything to one point, prevented with negatives, a momentum teacher, or prototype clustering (the DINO family).
CVPR'26Towards Foundation Models for 3D Scene Understanding (PointINS) — teacher-student self-distillation + prototype clustering (DINO mechanics) for point clouds.
CVPR'26TimeBridge — extends iBOT to video — start/end joint-embedding plus in-between reconstruction, matching AND reconstructing.
CVPR'26SPECTRE: Scaling Self-Supervised and Cross-Modal Pretraining for Volumetric CT — a two-stage recipe — DINO-style self-distillation, then vision-language alignment — on open medical data.

3 · Predict in latent space — JEPA

Instead of reconstructing raw pixels of the hidden part, predict its embedding. That skips wasting capacity on low-level detail and keeps the target abstract; collapse is held off by a stop-gradient target encoder. Notably, at CVPR 2026 JEPA is mostly imported (V-JEPA-2 as a black box) rather than re-derived — a sign the idea has matured into a tool.
CVPR'26Inference-time Physics Alignment with Latent World Models (WMReward) — uses V-JEPA-2's latent surprise as a physics-plausibility reward for video generation.
CVPR'26PHANTOM: Physics-Infused Video Generation — dual-branch flow-matching inside V-JEPA2's physics-aware latent space.
CVPR'26PriVi: General-Purpose Video Model for Primate Behavior — continues pretraining a V-JEPA video model on domain data; frozen-classifier evaluation.

4 · Just use the frozen backbone — SSL as infrastructure

The quiet headline: most vision papers now consume a self-supervised backbone rather than train one. DINOv2/v3 features are so semantically clustered that many tasks need no training at all — just cluster or nearest-neighbor on the frozen features.
CVPR'26INSID3: Training-Free In-Context Segmentation with DINOv3 — segmentation emerges from frozen DINOv3 features by clustering — no decoder, no labels. The cleanest proof SSL features are structured for free.
CVPR'26A Mixed Diet Makes DINO An Omnivorous Vision Encoder — parameter-efficient adaptation of frozen DINOv2 to align RGB, depth and segmentation.
CVPR'26One Patch to Caption Them All — zero-shot captioning directly on frozen foundation-model patches.

5 · Fight the collapse — what makes features good

Because collapse is the universal enemy, a distinct line of work studies what a good representation is and how to keep it from degenerating into a trivial or noisy one.
CVPR'26Suppressing Non-Semantic Noise in Masked Image Modeling (SOAP) — identifies positional-noise directions in masked-modeling features and projects them out — the sharpest what-makes-features-transfer result.
CVPR'26Diversity over Uniformity: Rethinking Representation in Generated Image Detection — explicit anti-feature-collapse learning that prevents low-rank homogenization.
CVPR'26Cloning Deterministic Worlds — reframes world-model failure as latent collapse and adds a temporal slowness + uniformity regularizer.

6 · Borrow CV's backbone for robot perception

The most common robotics pattern: drop a frozen DINOv2/MAE backbone into the pipeline. Robots have tiny, narrow datasets; a backbone pretrained by SSL on internet images gives generalizable dense features for free, and freezing it prevents forgetting when fine-tuning on scarce robot data.
ROBOTICS'25Watch Your STEPP: Semantic Traversability from Pose-Projected Features — frozen DINOv2 dense features drive legged-robot traversability with no geometric map.
ROBOTICS'25Cage: Causal Attention Enables Data-Efficient Generalizable Manipulation — frozen DINOv2 + a diffusion action head — 43% success in unseen environments where baselines score 0, from 50 demos.
ROBOTICS'25ReVLA: Reverting Visual Domain Limitation of Robotic Foundation Models — robot fine-tuning erases DINOv2's generality; model-merging reverts the backbone — a distinctly robotics problem.

7 · Let the robot's own experience be the label

The category with no computer-vision analog. A robot's supervision comes from the physics of embodiment — multi-view geometry, the passage of time, its own actions, its force and proprioception. No human ever annotates; the data stream grades itself.
ROBOTICS'25VertiCoder: Self-Supervised Kinodynamic Representation Learning — masked-patch prediction on terrain-interaction data — one representation serves forward/inverse dynamics and behavior cloning.
ROBOTICS'25ORB-SfMLearner: Self-supervised Visual Odometry — multi-view photometric consistency turns raw monocular video into free depth and ego-motion supervision — the classic robot SSL trick.
ROBOTICS'25Canonical Representation and Force-Based Pretraining of 3D Tactile — force prediction as the pretext task for a tactile backbone; 78% success on contact-rich tasks.
ROBOTICS'25SALON: Self-supervised Adaptive Learning for Off-road Navigation — online self-supervision from experience — adapts traversability in seconds with 100-1000x less data.

8 · Align across sensors and places — cross-modal contrastive

Robots must match across appearance, viewpoint and modality gaps where labels are impossible — RGB to LiDAR, ground to aerial, day to night. Contrastive learning builds the shared embedding space directly from co-occurrence.
ROBOTICS'25U2Frame: Unified Unsupervised Framework for LiDAR Loop Closing — temporal-spatial correlation as self-supervision — beats supervised methods with zero ground-truth poses.
ROBOTICS'25Contrastive Touch-to-Touch Pretraining — cross-sensor contrastive alignment so a model trained on one tactile sensor transfers to another.
ROBOTICS'25Monocular Visual Place Recognition in LiDAR Maps — cross-modal contrastive image-to-point-cloud descriptors for localization.

What robotics gets that pure vision doesn't

Same principle — invent a label-free task — but robotics draws its supervision from a different well.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
Where supervision comes fromone image + augmentations (crop, mask, jitter)the physics of embodiment — multi-view geometry, time, the robot's own actions and forces
The pretext taskreconstruct a masked patch, or match two viewspredict the next frame, the depth, the dynamics, the pseudo-action from unlabeled video
Relationship to CV SSLinvents the backbonesconsumes them — a frozen DINOv2 is the default perception front-end
Why no labels at alllabels are expensive at ImageNet scalelabels are often physically impossible (loop closure, traversability, off-road terrain)
The through-line. Vision invents ways to squeeze supervision out of a single image; robotics squeezes it out of the world — geometry, motion, and its own body — and then borrows vision's frozen backbones for the semantics. Both are the same move: no human labels, a task the data grades itself on, and collapse held at bay.
