Information bottleneck — why it works, mathematically

Generalization is not remembering the input. It is keeping only the part of the input that still predicts the answer after the accidental details are squeezed out.

← All themesEvery approachThe mathematics ↗

The problem, as a quantity

An input X contains two kinds of information at once: the part that says something about the target Y, and the part that merely identifies this particular sample. A big model can store both. That is the danger. If the representation Z keeps every scratch mark, texture, watermark, and dataset habit in X, training loss can fall while the learned rule is mostly a memory of accidents. The quantity you really want is therefore not maximum information. It is selective information: preserve what predicts Y, discard what only predicts X itself.

The core ideas — and why they work

Keep the label, forget the nuisance

min encoder I(Z;X) − β·I(Z;Y)
The bottleneck objective is a trade: punish a representation for remembering too much about the raw input, then reward it for retaining what still predicts the label. The universe permits this because information is not one substance. Some bits of X are shared with Y; other bits are private to the sample. Compression attacks the private bits first when they are not needed downstream. That is why the bottleneck is not making the model ignorant. It is forcing a question: after I throw away detail, what remains that still changes the answer? Same primitive elsewhere: this is Low rank and compression in its purest form — the useful signal lives in fewer dimensions than the measurement that carried it.

A representation is a channel, not a copy

X → Z → Y, p(y|x,z)=p(y|z)
The Markov chain says the prediction must pass through Z. Once Z is known, the model is not allowed to sneak back to the raw input. That makes Z a channel with limited capacity, not a storage drawer. A limited channel has to spend its bits where they buy predictive power. If background color and class label are correlated in the training set but not in the world, compression has a chance to drop the background because it is expensive to carry and unstable as evidence. This is why representation learning is not just feature extraction. It is a controlled loss of memory.

Noise makes compression differentiable

z = μ(x) + σ(x)·ε, ε ~ N(0,I), capacity ≈ KL(q(z|x) || p(z))
A hard rule that deletes bits is awkward to optimize, but a noisy channel is smooth. If the encoder must send x through z = μ + σε, then small details survive only by spending variance and moving the posterior away from the prior. The KL term is the price of that movement. Details that do not help the label become too expensive to keep. Same primitive elsewhere: this is the same Gaussian-channel accounting used by variational autoencoders and uncertainty models: noise is not decoration, it is the ruler that measures how many distinguishable messages the representation can carry.

Too much squeeze destroys the answer

β ↑ ⇒ I(Z;X) ↓, but if I(Z;Y) ↓ too, accuracy ↓
Compression is only virtuous until it erases the evidence. The bottleneck has a phase boundary: weak compression memorizes nuisance; moderate compression keeps the label-bearing invariant; excessive compression makes different classes indistinguishable. This matters because there is no magic in simplicity by itself. A representation generalizes when its remaining degrees of freedom match the true causal degrees of freedom of the task, not when it is merely small.

The approaches, as variations on the math

Methods differ mainly in how they approximate the impossible mutual information terms. Variational bottlenecks use a noisy encoder and a KL price to limit channel capacity. Contrastive methods keep the part of two views that agrees while dropping view-specific nuisance. Minimal sufficient representation methods search for the smallest Z that still predicts Y. In vision, this shows up whenever augmentation, masking, distillation, or token pruning asks the same question: which information survives a squeeze and still supports the answer?

A worked numeric example

In the toy run at analysis/ib/toy_ib.json, the representation has one label bit and one nuisance direction. With β=0, the proxy information about the input is high, I(Z;X)=2.07, but accuracy is lower because nuisance survives. At β=0.3, nuisance kept drops to 0.40 and accuracy peaks at 0.938. At larger β, the label information also falls, so compression stops helping. The finding is the bottleneck in numbers: keep less of X, but not less of the part of X that predicts Y.

1Variational bottlenecks
L = E[-log p(y|z)] + β·KL(q(z|x) || p(z))
The KL term charges the encoder for each distinguishable message it sends about the input. Prediction loss pays back only the bits useful for the target.
Toy bottleneck run — β=0.3 gave the best toy accuracy, showing moderate compression removed nuisance before erasing the label.
2Invariant views
keep information shared by x₁ and x₂, drop information private to one view
Two augmented views share the object but not every crop, color shift, or noise pattern. Agreement becomes a filter for stable information.
Connection to self-supervised learning — The shared-view signal is the mutual information worth keeping; view-specific detail is the nuisance to drop.
3Token pruning
choose S with |S|≪n while preserving p(y|tokens_S)
A finite token budget forces the model to keep the visual evidence that changes the answer and discard redundant patches.
Connection to efficient vision models — Pruning is a discrete bottleneck: spend a small token budget only where prediction changes.
The through-line. The information bottleneck is the cleanest statement of why generalization is possible at all: the world repeats structure, but accidents do not. A representation should be just wide enough to carry the repeated structure that predicts the target and too narrow to carry every accident of the measurement. That is the parent idea behind compression, invariance, distillation, token pruning, and the hope that a model learns the rule instead of the dataset.
