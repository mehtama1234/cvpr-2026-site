1 · Continuous-time dynamics: learning how things evolve

Neural ODEs make network depth continuous by letting hidden states evolve according to learned differential equations: dh/dt = f(h,t). Rather than stacking discrete layers, the model learns smooth dynamics that flow through time, unlocking extrapolation far beyond training horizons and graceful handling of asynchronous or variable-length observations. This section explores four papers that harness continuous-time representations across dynamic scene synthesis, temporal memory, multimodal system identification, and deformable medical imaging.
CVPR'26Node-RF: Learning Generalized Continuous Space-Time Scene Dynamics with Neural ODE-based NeRFs
Problem
Dynamic NeRF methods capture motion only within observed training boundaries and fail to extrapolate beyond seen sequences, limiting long-range temporal prediction and preventing generalization across unseen initial conditions in 4D scenes.
Approach
Node-RF couples Neural ODEs with dynamic NeRFs: scene state evolves as a continuous latent vector following a learned ODE, decoded at query time by a NeRF renderer to produce geometry and appearance. Training uses only photometric loss on multi-view image sequences without optical flow or depth supervision.
Why it matters
Integrating Neural ODE latent dynamics with NeRF rendering creates an implicit spatio-temporal model that naturally extrapolates beyond training frames and learns trajectory patterns that generalize to novel initial conditions, making the learned ODE a reusable model of scene behavior.
CVPR'26Coupling Liquid Time-Constant Encoders with Modern Hopfield Memory
Problem
Continuous-time recurrent networks force a single hidden state to encode both rapid input changes and slow contextual signals, causing fast fluctuations to overwrite longer-term memory and lose historical information.
Approach
Couples Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) networks with Modern Hopfield Networks (MHN): at each step, the LTC state is queried against content-addressable Hopfield memory, retrieved patterns are concatenated with the LTC activation, then fed to a readout layer. This decouples fast adaptive encoding from slow associative retrieval.
Why it matters
The resulting continuous-time architecture maintains both rapid responsiveness through LTC dynamics and stable long-term context via Hopfield memory, with theoretical guarantees that gradient contraction flattens the optimization landscape and enables stable learning from time-series data.
CVPR'26EMMA: Extracting Multiple physical parameters from Multimodal Data
Problem
Extracting dynamical system parameters (mass, drag, thrust) from video alone fails when states are occluded, actuation is hidden, or initial conditions are unknown; isolated modalities miss coupled phenomena like audio signatures of friction or chart-recorded reference signals.
Approach
EMMA fuses video, audio, and chart-extracted time-series through a Liquid Time-Constant network that learns latent dynamics constrained by physics-informed loss over governing differential equations. The framework jointly recovers explicit parameters, infers hidden dynamics, and calibrates unknown coordinate frames without masks or differentiable rendering.
Why it matters
Modeling dynamics as a continuous-time ODE constrains the learned latent evolution to satisfy conservation laws, enabling recovery of physical parameters and unmeasured forces from raw multimodal observations where no single sensor sees the full picture.
CVPR'26Learning Diffeomorphism for Medical Image Registration with Time-Embedded Architectures
Problem
Medical image registration must learn deformable transformations that preserve topology while aligning complex anatomy; current methods struggle to maintain invertibility and smoothness across large anatomical variation.
Approach
Learns diffeomorphic transformations by parameterizing velocity fields through time-embedded neural networks integrated via neural ODE framework, ensuring invertible and smooth deformation fields by construction through continuous evolution of registered coordinates.
Why it matters
Modeling deformation as integration of a continuous velocity field (ODE formulation) guarantees topological preservation and invertibility by design, while the time-embedded structure naturally represents gradual alignment across anatomical boundaries.

2 · The continuous flow: transport as an ODE

Neural ODEs reframe generative modeling as continuous transport: instead of predicting discrete jumps between distributions, flows learn time-dependent velocity fields that smoothly map one distribution to another. Flow matching—learning a vector field whose solution follows a chosen trajectory—bridges theory and practice. This section explores how continuous dynamics power modern motion, pose, and video generation: optical flow as learned velocity fields, multimodal motion prediction via latent flow mixtures, video synthesis with normalizing flow transport, and interleaved text-video co-generation where the flow updates pixels while language guides semantics. Each instantiation reveals the same core idea: continuous paths are more stable, more theoretically grounded, and more controllable than their discrete predecessors.
CVPR'26Optical Flow Matching: Reframing Optical Flow as Continuous Transport Dynamics
Problem
Classical optical flow estimation treats motion as discrete frame-to-frame pixel correspondences, severing motion from its underlying physics. Networks infer where pixels move without capturing how velocity fields smoothly evolve through time.
Approach
Optical Flow Matching learns a time-dependent velocity field vθ(xt, t) that continuously transports pixel coordinates from one frame to another via ODE integration. Triangle Velocities Synergy reconciles Flow Matching's theoretical guarantees with practical training by introducing auxiliary trajectories and constant-marginal-velocity paths.
Why it matters
Physical motion obeys continuous dynamics. By reformulating optical flow as transport along learned velocity fields, OFM restores the connection between acceleration, velocity, and displacement—achieving state-of-the-art temporal stability and cross-dataset generalization on Sintel and KITTI while maintaining the same RAFT-like architecture.
CVPR'26Gaussian-Mixture Latent Flow for Stochastic 3D Human Motion Prediction
Problem
3D human motion prediction exhibits inherent stochasticity with multiple equally plausible futures. Deterministic and simple stochastic methods fail to represent multimodal distributions, collapsing predictions toward mean trajectories.
Approach
Combines Gaussian mixture modeling in latent space with continuous normalizing flows to learn multimodal distributions of future motions. The flow transports samples through latent trajectories parameterized by learned time-dependent transformations.
Why it matters
Mixture flows elegantly decompose prediction uncertainty: the mixture component captures discrete modes (reach vs. wave vs. walk), while the continuous flow component learns the smooth trajectory within each mode. This yields superior diversity-accuracy tradeoffs on Human3.6M compared to VAE and deterministic baselines.
CVPR'26STARFlow-V: End-to-End Video Generative Modeling with Autoregressive Normalizing Flows
Problem
Normalizing flows have been sidelined in video generation because exact likelihood requires prohibitively expensive bijective architectures, while autoregressive sampling is slow due to frame-by-frame dependencies.
Approach
Global-local autoregressive normalizing flow with flow-score matching training: the global component models inter-frame transport via learned ODE-like dynamics, while flow-score matching derives a consistency condition between the NF density and a causal denoiser operating on latent sequences. Block-wise Jacobi iteration decodes in parallel.
Why it matters
Flow-score matching sidesteps expensive likelihood evaluation, providing a stable training signal. The 7B model achieves competitive VBench scores (79.70) and 15x inference speedup—the first normalizing flow text-to-video system at scale, proving that continuous transport ODE solvers can rival diffusion quality.
CVPR'26TV2TV: A Unified Framework for Interleaved Language and Video Generation
Problem
Video generation models struggle with complex outputs requiring repeated semantic reasoning about what happens next, yielding poor long-form coherence and limited user controllability when generating 30+ seconds of footage.
Approach
Mixture-of-Transformers with interleaved text and video sequences: each segment contains [text], [noisy latent], [clean latent], decoded via flow matching (rectified flow) on noisy frames while attending to text history. BOF tokens trigger ODE solver steps on frame latents; text generation resumes until the next video segment.
Why it matters
By decomposing video generation into text-and-flow interleaves, semantic complexity (deciding what happens) moves to the language component while flow transport handles pixels. CS:GO experiments show 91% preference over text-to-video baseline and 19-point controllability gain; sports domain beats competitors on prompt alignment.

3 · Dynamics-aware models: physics, time, and their fragility

A learned continuous dynamical system—modeled as an ODE or vector field—captures how systems evolve over time in a physics-consistent way. This representation enables inference at any timestep, spatial location, or unobserved trajectory. But modeling the world as a smooth, differentiable vector field also creates new vulnerabilities: perturbations at early timepoints are amplified by the ODE solver itself, and flows trained on continuous paths can be hijacked for malicious actions while appearing kinematically normal. These papers explore both the power of continuous-time representations for scene understanding and generalization, and the critical attack surface they expose in robotics and vision pipelines.
CVPR'26FlowHijack: A Dynamics-Aware Backdoor Attack on Flow-Matching Vision-Language-Action Models
Problem
Flow-matching VLAs (e.g., pi_0) generate continuous robotic actions via ODE-integrated vector field dynamics rather than discrete tokens, making them vulnerable to a new class of backdoor attack that exploits the mathematical properties of ODE solvers themselves.
Approach
FlowHijack targets the learned vector field vθ by injecting a τ-conditioned corruption that redirects the field toward malicious actions only at early ODE timesteps. A Dynamics Mimicry regularizer ensures the malicious vector field matches the kinematic profile of benign actions, evading anomaly detection while exploiting the ODE solver's amplification of early errors into full trajectory deviations.
Why it matters
A learned continuous ODE vector field makes the system more elegant and physically plausible—but that same mathematical machinery (early errors amplified by the solver) becomes an attack vector. The backdoor hijacks the vector field dynamics while maintaining kinematic stealth.
CVPR'26Image Diffusion Preview with Consistency Solver
Problem
Diffusion models are slow to sample from, and existing acceleration methods fail to ensure consistency between rapid low-step previews and full-step final outputs in interactive workflows.
Approach
ConsistencySolver is a trainable high-order ODE solver optimized via reinforcement learning to deliver fast, high-quality previews while maintaining deterministic consistency to the full-step trajectory via a shared PF-ODE path.
Why it matters
Diffusion can be viewed as solving an ODE in latent space; learning a continuous solver that respects the underlying ODE dynamics allows previews and full outputs to stay on the same trajectory while radically cutting preview steps.
CVPR'26Node-RF: Learning Generalized Continuous Space-Time Scene Dynamics with Neural ODE-based NeRFs
Problem
Existing dynamic NeRF methods capture motion only within observed time windows and cannot extrapolate far beyond training sequences or generalize to unseen trajectories, limiting 4D reconstruction and scene forecasting.
Approach
Node-RF represents scene state as a latent vector evolved by a learned Neural ODE, then decodes this evolving latent via a NeRF renderer at any queried timestep. Training uses only multi-view photometric supervision, no optical flow or depth ground truth.
Why it matters
Modeling scene dynamics as a continuous ODE latent trajectory enables long-range temporal extrapolation beyond observed frames and generalization across multiple motion sequences by learning a shared continuous dynamical system.
CVPR'26VIAFormer: Voxel-Image Alignment Transformer for High-Fidelity Voxel Refinement
Problem
Vision Foundation Models produce coarse, noisy voxel grids from multi-view images—with occlusions, surface noise, and missing geometry—unsuitable for high-fidelity 3D generation pipelines without refinement.
Approach
VIAFormer aligns multi-view image tokens to voxel space via an Image Index (rendering a coordinate-encoded mesh to create 2D-to-3D mappings), then trains a hybrid transformer with Correctional Flow—learning constant-velocity refinement along a straight ODE path from noisy to clean voxel latent.
Why it matters
Correctional Flow replaces diffusion with a learned continuous velocity field, allowing the model to refine voxels via ODE integration rather than iterative noise prediction, enabling fast, deterministic improvement of Vision Foundation Model outputs.
