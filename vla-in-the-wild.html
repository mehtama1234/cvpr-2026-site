The uses — six ways to build a policy, and the vision crossover

Uses 1-6 are how robotics builds a VLA (the action head, grounding, the backbone, data, speed, hierarchy); 7-8 are the vision crossover into driving (ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS · CVPR'26 vision).

1 · The action head — sample, don't average

The load-bearing choice: how to turn understanding into an action. Demonstrations are multimodal (left OR right around an object), so a regression head averages them into the invalid middle. Generative heads — a diffusion policy or an autoregressive action tokenizer — model the whole distribution and commit to one mode. This one fact is why regression policies were displaced.
ROBOTICS'25The Ingredients for Robotic Diffusion Transformers — the canonical recipe for a diffusion action head (DiT-Block Policy), ablated end to end.
ROBOTICS'25Diffusion-Based Approximate MPC: Fast and Consistent Imitation of Multi-Modal Action Distributions — the cleanest statement of the thesis — diffusion learns the full multi-modal distribution where a regressor collapses it.
ROBOTICS'25Discrete Policy: Learning Disentangled Action Space for Multi-Task Robotic Manipulation — the token alternative — a VQ-VAE quantizes actions so they can be predicted like words (the RT-2/OpenVLA lineage).
ROBOTICS'25Action Tokenizer Matters in In-Context Imitation Learning — proves the action tokenizer itself is a first-order design choice, not plumbing.

2 · Ground the language to behavior

An instruction is symbolic; the world is continuous pixels and torques. Grounding must bind open-vocabulary words to this scene and compositional relations to action, and generalize to words absent from the tiny robot dataset. The universal trick: inherit grounding from an internet-pretrained VLM and let language be a shared latent across modalities.
ROBOTICS'25STEER: Flexible Robotic Manipulation via Dense Language Grounding — dense per-skill language annotation exposes a compositional interface for zero-shot novel behaviors.
ROBOTICS'25Beyond Sight: Finetuning Generalist Robot Policies with Heterogeneous Sensors via Language Grounding — uses natural language as the cross-modal bridge to attach new sensors (touch, audio) to an existing policy.
ROBOTICS'25Robo-MUTUAL: Robotic Multimodal Task Specification via Unimodal Learning — makes text, image and goal representations interchangeable in a shared latent so grounding survives modality swaps.
ROBOTICS'25KALIE: Fine-Tuning Vision-Language Models for Open-World Manipulation Without Robot Data — grounds language to point affordances from human-labeled 2D images only — no robot data.

3 · Adapt a VLM backbone into a policy — cheaply

A VLA is rarely trained from scratch: you graft an action head onto a pretrained vision-language model to inherit its visual and semantic generality. Two hard problems — fine-tuning erases that generality (catastrophic forgetting), and full fine-tuning of a multi-billion-parameter model is expensive, so the LoRA adapters from this site's first theme show up here.
ROBOTICS'25ReVLA: Reverting Visual Domain Limitation of Robotic Foundation Models — the forgetting paper — robot fine-tuning wrecks the DINOv2 backbone's OOD vision; recovers it via gradient-free model merging.
ROBOTICS'25MoRE: Unlocking Scalability in RL for Quadruped Vision-Language-Action Models — multiple LoRA modules as a sparse mixture-of-experts inside a multimodal LLM, trained as an RL Q-function — PEFT-into-VLA in one paper.
ROBOTICS'25iRe-VLA: Improving Vision-Language-Action Model with Online Reinforcement Learning — alternates RL exploration with supervised stabilization to fine-tune a large VLA without instability.
ROBOTICS'25BYOVLA: Run-time Observation Interventions Make VLA Models More Visually Robust — a weights-frozen alternative — edit task-irrelevant pixels at run time to harden any off-the-shelf VLA.

4 · Feed it — data, cross-embodiment, sim-to-real

Real robot demonstrations are the binding constraint: every teleoperated episode is expensive. Three escape routes — synthesize data, share it across different robot bodies, and cross the sim-to-real gap.
ROBOTICS'25ReBot: Scaling Robot Learning with Real-to-Sim-to-Real Robotic Video Synthesis — replays real trajectories in sim and inpaints real backgrounds to manufacture VLA training video — improves OpenVLA by up to 21.8%.
ROBOTICS'25DexMimicGen: Automated Data Generation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation — turns 60 human demos into 21,000 synthetic ones in simulation.
ROBOTICS'25EgoMimic: Scaling Imitation Learning via Egocentric Video — co-trains one policy on human egocentric video and robot data, using humans as a data source.
ROBOTICS'25Motion Tracks: A Unified Representation for Human-Robot Transfer in Few-Shot Imitation — 2D pixel motion tracks as a cross-embodiment action shared by human hands and robot end-effectors.

5 · Make it fast enough to actually run

A billion-parameter diffusion or autoregressive VLA is far too slow for a control loop at tens or hundreds of Hz — a laggy action is a stale action, and the robot goes unstable. So a whole sub-field accelerates generation without retraining.
ROBOTICS'25PD-VLA: Accelerating VLA Models with Action Chunking via Parallel Decoding — reframes autoregressive VLA decoding as a fixed-point system solved in parallel — 2.52x faster, training-free, with guarantees.
ROBOTICS'25Quart-Online: Latency-Free Multimodal LLM for Quadruped Robot Learning — action-chunk discretization compresses actions into a small vocabulary for a 50 Hz real-time quadruped VLA.
ROBOTICS'25Fast Policy Synthesis with Variable Noise Diffusion Models (Streaming Diffusion Policy) — fully denoises only the immediate action and streams the rest, instead of stalling per step.

6 · Split slow reasoning from fast control — hierarchy

Long-horizon, semantically complex tasks are hard end-to-end. Split the stack: a slow VLM reasons about what to do (subgoals, code, costs) while a fast controller handles how. This is numerically the largest VLA pattern, and where the end-to-end and planner families fuse.
ROBOTICS'25GHIL-Glue: Hierarchical Control with Filtered Subgoal Images — inserts a filter between a generative subgoal planner and a goal-conditioned policy — fixing the planner-controller interface, SOTA on CALVIN.
ROBOTICS'25SARO: Space-Aware Robot System for Terrain Crossing via Vision-Language Model — a VLM reasons over an RL low-level locomotion controller.
ROBOTICS'25Local Policies Enable Zero-Shot Long-Horizon Manipulation (ManipGen) — pose-invariant local policies composed under a foundation-model planner — beats OpenVLA / SayCan / VoxPoser by 36-76%.

7 · The vision crossover — end-to-end driving as a VLA

Vision-language-action is robotics-native, but its clearest crossover into computer vision is self-driving: end-to-end driving is a VLA in disguise — perceive the scene, output an ego trajectory. The vision community brings enormous driving data and strong perception backbones.
CVPR'26DriveMoE: Mixture-of-Experts for End-to-End Autonomous Driving — scales the VLA recipe with scenario and modality experts, targeting the long tail where a monolithic driving policy fails.
CVPR'26HybridDriveVLA — an explicit driving VLA fusing visual chain-of-thought with tree-of-thought evaluation to output ego trajectories.
CVPR'26ResAD: Normalized Residual Trajectory Modeling for End-to-End Autonomous Driving — predicts the ego trajectory as residuals over an anchor — a clean action parameterization.
CVPR'26GuideFlow: Constraint-Guided Flow-Matching Planner — a flow-matching (generative) planner emitting feasible ego trajectories.

8 · Imagine before you act — world models for driving

The vision community's distinctive edge: a world model that generates the future to plan against it. Roll out imagined futures in a latent space, then choose the action — perception and generation feeding control.
CVPR'26DriveLaW: Unifying Planning and Video Generation in a Latent Driving World — one model rolls out a latent driving world (video) and outputs the ego trajectory — the clearest world-model-feeds-action example.
CVPR'26LCDrive: Latent Chain-of-Thought World Modeling — combines latent reasoning with a world model to imagine futures before emitting the trajectory.
CVPR'26Think Before You Drive: World Model-Inspired Multimodal Grounding — grounds language to the scene through a world-model lens, then outputs a driving decision.

Two halves of one problem

Vision-language-action is where robotics and computer vision meet — and each brings the half the other lacks.

axisRobotics 2025 (the home field)CVPR 2026 (the crossover)
What it hasreal bodies, a closed control loop, physical action multimodalityhuge scale (driving data), strong perception backbones, generative world models
What it works onthe action head, grounding, cheap backbone adaptation, data, real-time speedend-to-end driving, imagining futures to plan, reasoning-to-trajectory
What it lacksscale and perception muscle (borrows CV's backbones)real hardware, closed-loop control, the messy multimodality of action
The capstone. Vision-language-action needs every other idea on this site at once: a transformer that sees, a way to read language, a generative head to act, adapters to specialize the giant backbone, self-supervised features underneath, and robustness so the action can be trusted. It is the point where seeing, saying, and doing become one system.
