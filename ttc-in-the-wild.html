Thinking harder at inference — test-time compute across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued it from first principles: a fixed model gets more accurate if it spends more compute at inference — sample many answers and aggregate, or reason step by step — with diminishing returns and a dependence on sound aggregation. This page shows the surge of that idea across 2026 multimodal models: sampling and voting, visual chain-of-thought, verifiers, and knowing when to stop.

What changes between vision and a robot

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025
What extra compute buysa more reliable answer from a fixed modela safer plan from a fixed policy
The aggregationmajority vote, verifier reranking, chain-of-thoughtverify a plan before executing it
The honest failureover-thinking hurts; a noisy verifier caps the gaina wrong verifier ships a wrong plan
When to use itthe hard queries, where one shot is unreliablehigh-stakes decisions worth the extra latency
The through-line. Test-time compute trades inference for accuracy: sample many answers and aggregate, or reason in steps, on a model whose weights never change. In vision it powers self-consistency, visual chain-of-thought, and best-of-N reranking; in robotics it verifies plans before acting — the same primitive, a better answer versus a safer action, and both gated by whether the aggregation (vote or verifier) is trustworthy.
