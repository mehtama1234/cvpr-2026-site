Roll downhill, with a little noise

The why page says you sample an energy model by walking downhill in energy plus a dash of noise — Langevin dynamics — needing only the gradient, never the normalizer. Here is that walk, live. The background is a learned energy (bright = low energy = likely); the dots are samples flowing through it. The one knob is the noise.

First-principles guide

Quantity shown. This sandbox exposes an energy landscape: which states are low-cost explanations.

What the controls mean. Temperature and sampling controls change how the system searches valleys.

What to watch for. Watch sampling get stuck in a local low-energy shortcut.

What to notice. With low noise the samples slide straight into the nearest well and sit there — sharp, but they never leave, so they never discover the other modes (poor mixing). With more noise they jitter, climb out of wells, and hop between all three modes — better mixing, but the cloud is looser and blurrier. That is the fundamental Langevin tension the run measured: too cold and you get stuck, too hot and you smear. Taming it is exactly what the diffusion reformulation was built to do.
