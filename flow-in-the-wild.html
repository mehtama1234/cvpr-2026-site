Optical flow · how the field actually finds correspondence

Finding the same point twice, everywhere — flow across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the problem from first principles: for a point in one frame, where did it go in the next — and why you can only answer where there is 2D structure (the aperture problem). This page shows how far that reaches. In vision, correspondence has become the scaffolding under dynamic 3D and generative video; in a robot, the same match becomes its own motion, a threat's motion, or a live actuator command. Real papers for every use, from the analyzed corpora.

Two things to hold onto. In vision, the win is no longer a better number on a flow benchmark — it is using dense correspondence to reconstruct and generate. In robotics, correspondence is never the deliverable: it is the raw material for the robot's own motion, the motion of threats, or a control signal — computed onboard, at control rate, where a wrong match is a wrong move.

The uses — from a motion field to a robot's move

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (estimate flow, track a point, match for geometry, scene flow, beat the hard cases, correspondence-as-infrastructure); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into egomotion, servoing, and obstacle motion (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Estimating the motion field — better, faster, reframed

Given two frames, produce a dense arrow for every pixel. The modern recipe builds a giant all-pairs cost volume and iteratively refines — accurate but heavy. 2026 pushes three ways: make it cheaper, make it work in the dark and without labels, and re-frame flow as a continuous physical process.
CVPR'26Efficient All-Pairs Correlation Volume Sampling for Optical Flow Estimation — notices only ~1.6% of the cost volume is ever used, so it computes it block-sparsely: 99% less memory, same accuracy.
CVPR'26Optical Flow Matching: Reframing Optical Flow as Continuous Transport Dynamics — treats flow not as a single jump but as a smooth velocity field transporting pixels over time.
CVPR'26FlowFM: Advancing Dark Optical Flow Estimation with Flow Matching — a one-step flow-matching regressor plus frequency cues to get clean flow in low light where noise swamps motion.
CVPR'26U2Flow: Uncertainty-Aware Unsupervised Optical Flow Estimation — outputs flow and a per-pixel confidence with no ground truth, softening its smoothing exactly in occluded and textureless regions.

2 · Follow one point through the whole clip — track-any-point

Flow only links neighbouring frames; to follow one specific point across a long video — through occlusion, appearance change, and disappearing and coming back — you need point tracking. It is the backbone for object motion and, increasingly, for supervising 4D reconstruction.
CVPR'26CoWTracker: Tracking by Warping instead of Correlation — drops the expensive cost volume and tracks by warping features toward the query point, unifying tracking and flow at high resolution.
CVPR'26AnthroTAP: Learning Point Tracking with Real-World Motion — a real-world human-motion dataset that closes the synthetic-to-real gap plaguing trackers.
CVPR'26MV-TAP: Tracking Any Point in Multi-View Videos — uses cross-view attention and camera geometry to beat the occlusion, blur, and depth ambiguity that break single-view trackers.

3 · Match the same point across wide views — correspondence for geometry

To recover camera pose or reconstruct 3D from photos, match the same physical point across images from very different angles — often with no reliable texture. Detector-free semi-dense matchers feed structure-from-motion and localization; the theme is efficiency and robustness in bland regions.
CVPR'26Scalable Feature Matching via State Space Modeling and Sparse Correlation — replaces quadratic transformer matching with a linear-complexity Mamba backbone so semi-dense matching scales to high-res.
CVPR'26TextFM: Robust Semi-dense Feature Matching with Language Guidance — injects vision-language cues to disambiguate matches in the killer cases — textureless regions and large viewpoint change.
CVPR'26UniCorrn: Unified Correspondence Transformer Across 2D and 3D — one model matches image-to-image, image-to-point-cloud and point-cloud-to-point-cloud instead of three specialists.

4 · Motion in full 3D — scene flow

Optical flow lives in 2D; scene flow asks where each point moved in 3D — essential for driving and robotics, from LiDAR, stereo, or events. Harder because you need depth and motion jointly and ground truth is scarce.
CVPR'26SEA-Flow3D: Simplified, Efficient, and Accurate Scene Flow via Spatial Vector Sampling and Multi-scale Refinement — samples correlations directly in 3D, exploiting 3D structure for more robust dense scene flow.
CVPR'26ARES: Unifying Asymmetric RGB-Event Stereo for Probabilistic Scene Flow Estimation — fuses texture-rich RGB with high-speed events as a probabilistic stereo problem, robust under blur and lighting change.
CVPR'26x2-Fusion: Cross-Modality and Cross-Dimension Flow Estimation in Event Edge Space — jointly estimates 2D optical flow and 3D scene flow by projecting images, LiDAR, and events into one shared space.

5 · Where every method dies — occlusion, blur, textureless, events

Every method above fails in the same conditions: fast motion (blur), things vanishing behind others (occlusion), and blank or repetitive surfaces where every match looks identical (the textureless / aperture ambiguity). A distinct cluster attacks these — and event cameras, sensing brightness change at microsecond speed, are the favourite weapon against blur.
CVPR'26From Contrast to Consistency: Rethinking Event-based Continuous-Time Optical Flow Estimation — replaces the standard contrast-maximization objective with spatio-temporal structural consistency, fixing jittery event-flow trajectories.
CVPR'26When Lines Meet Textures: Spatial-Frequency Aligned Diffusion Features for Cross-Sparsity Correspondence — matches sparse line-art against dense photos by aligning frequency content — correspondence when one side has almost no texture.
CVPR'26MER-Tracker: Towards High-Speed 3D Point Tracking via Multi-View Event-RGB Hybrid Cameras — fuses fast-but-sparse events with detailed-but-blurry RGB to track 3D points through motion ordinary cameras miss.

6 · Correspondence as scaffolding — supervising 3D/4D & video

The fastest-growing use of flow is not estimating flow for its own sake — it is flow/tracking as free supervision or a control knob for something else: reconstructing dynamic 3D/4D from ordinary video, editing generative video, animating 3D. This is the biggest through-line: correspondence is now infrastructure.
CVPR'26Flow3r: Factored Flow Prediction for Scalable Visual Geometry Learning — learns feed-forward 3D/4D geometry from unlabeled monocular video using 2D dense flow as supervision, skipping pose/depth labels.
CVPR'26MotionCrafter: Dense Geometry and Motion Reconstruction with a 4D VAE — encodes point-maps and scene-flow into one latent to reconstruct 4D geometry plus dense motion from a single video.
CVPR'26Flow4DGS-SLAM: Optical Flow-Guided 4D Gaussian Splatting SLAM — injects optical flow into 4D Gaussian-splatting SLAM to handle dynamic scenes, driving the temporal evolution of the blobs.

7 · How the robot itself moved — VO/SLAM front-end

Robotics inverts the goal. A moving robot tracks the same features frame to frame; the way they slide reveals the camera's — and the robot's — 3D motion. The dominant robotics pattern, and the whole story is surviving the real world: darkness, textureless walls, legged gait, blur — a single bad match is a wrong pose.
ROBOTICS'25MAC-VO: Metrics-Aware Covariance for Learning-Based Stereo Visual Odometry — learns per-keypoint uncertainty to weight and select matches in the pose optimization.
ROBOTICS'25GeoFlow-SLAM: A Robust Tightly-Coupled RGBD-Inertial and Legged Odometry Fusion SLAM for Dynamic Legged Robotics — dual-stream optical flow drives feature matching for fast-moving, texture-less legged robots.
ROBOTICS'25NeuFlow-V2: Push High-Efficiency Optical Flow To the Limit — real-time dense flow (20+ FPS on a Jetson Orin Nano) built for resource-constrained robot platforms.

8 · A tracked point steers the gripper — visual servoing

For an arm, correspondence becomes a live steering signal: keep tracking a point on the object, and the gap between where it is and where you want it tells the controller how to move. The loop closes at control rate, so the emphasis is staying locked on through occlusion, deformation, and bad light — losing the track is a wrong move mid-task.
ROBOTICS'25CNSv2: Probabilistic Correspondence Encoded Neural Image Servo — replaces brittle hard keypoint matches with soft probabilistic correspondences to drive a servo under bad lighting/texture.
ROBOTICS'25Surgical D-Knot: Augmented Dexterity for Tying Double Knots by Monitoring Optical Flow in Monocular Attention Windows — optical flow in attention windows monitors thread wrapping to tie knots with only a monocular endoscope.
ROBOTICS'25Kalib: Easy Hand-Eye Calibration with Reference Point Tracking — tracks a single reference point on the robot to solve the camera-robot transform.

9 · What is moving toward me — scene flow & event motion

A robot sees everything shift, but some shift is its own motion and some is a genuinely moving obstacle. Scene flow and motion segmentation subtract ego-motion so what is left is the 3D velocity of things that could hit you — and event cameras catch motion too fast for ordinary frames.
ROBOTICS'25SSF: Sparse Long-Range Scene Flow for Autonomous Driving — sparse-convolution scene flow that scales to long-range, sparse point clouds for driving.
ROBOTICS'25VISC: mmWave Radar Scene Flow Estimation using Pervasive Visual-Inertial Supervision — fuses ego-motion, optical flow, and radar to recover the 3D motion of dynamic objects without LiDAR labels.
ROBOTICS'256-DoF Object Tracking with Event-based Optical Flow and Frames — event-based optical flow gives high-rate object velocity, fused with slow RGB pose for blur-free fast tracking.

The through-line. Finding the same point twice is the connective tissue of vision — and in 2026 it moved from being the answer to being the scaffold under 3D, 4D, and video generation. In robotics the same match is never shown to anyone: it becomes how the robot knows it moved, what is moving at it, and how to steer. Vision estimates the motion; a robot acts on it.
