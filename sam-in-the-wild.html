Segment Anything · how the field actually cuts things out

Point at anything, get its mask — SAM across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the idea from first principles: don't memorize a fixed list of classes — learn one general skill, point at a thing and cut out its exact shape, even a kind never seen, and offer several masks because a click is ambiguous. This page shows how far that reaches. In vision SAM became a reusable brick everything snaps onto; in a robot, the mask is not the answer — it is a handle to grasp, track, avoid, or map. Two things to hold onto. In vision, the recipe is freeze SAM/SAM2, add a light adapter or smarter prompt, and either carry it into a hard new domain or harvest its class-agnostic masks as free supervision. In robotics, a good segment is not a high IoU — it is one the robot can act on.

The uses — from a reusable brick to a robot's handle

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (promptable, efficient, video, new domains, free supervision, mask quality); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into a handle for action (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · You point, it cuts — promptable & interactive

The core SAM promise is point-in, mask-out. But a single click is ambiguous — click a shirt and do you mean the shirt, the torso, or the person? These make the interface smarter: better prompts, learning from your corrections, refining a rough prompt into a clean mask, feedback loops that improve as you interact.

5 · A free label factory — SAM as supervision

Here SAM is not the star — it is free labor. Because it produces clean object masks for anything, other tasks use those masks as free supervision: to sharpen depth boundaries, isolate objects in 3D, generate robot training data, or clean moving objects from a scene. No human annotation — SAM does the cutting, the real work is downstream.

7 · Which pixels do I grasp — segment to act

Robotics inverts the deliverable. To pick something up a robot first needs to know which pixels are the thing — and homes and warehouses are full of objects it never trained on. Prompt SAM (a point, box, or name), it isolates the target object or a specific part, and the grasp planner closes the gripper on exactly those pixels. The mask turns grasp something into grasp this.

8 · Cut out what has no name — open-world safety

The world is full of things a robot was never trained to name — bin clutter, a rock in shadow, an obstacle of no known class — and it cannot safely ignore what it cannot label. Promptable, class-agnostic segmentation cuts out every object, including ones outside the training set, so the robot can pick from clutter or avoid a hazard it has no name for.

9 · Masks become the map — mapping & label bootstrapping

Beyond a single grab, a robot needs a map it can reason about and labels it did not hand-draw. Two moves: segment each frame with SAM and lift the cutouts into a 3D map or scene graph the planner queries by name; or use SAM's free masks as automatic labels to bootstrap other robot perception models. The mask becomes durable world knowledge.

What changes when the mask is a handle, not an answer

Same click-in / mask-out — opposite deliverable.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
What the mask isthe deliverable — a cutout scored against ground trutha handle — which pixels to grasp, track, avoid, or map
How it is scoredIoU on a benchmarkwhether the robot could act on it
A good maska high overlap numbera successful grasp, a stable track, an obstacle avoided
A bad maska lower scorea dropped object, a lost target, a collision
The through-line. Promptable segmentation became vision's reusable brick — freeze it, adapt it, or harvest its masks as free supervision for everything else. In robotics the very same cutout is a means to a physical act: the robot has to segment things no benchmark ever labeled, because it has to do something to them. Vision cuts out the answer; a robot cuts out a handle.
