The problem — from first principles

Vision models learn to turn pixels into predictions: what object is in this image, where is it, what does the scene contain? But this process has a hidden fragility. A tiny change to the input—a few altered pixels, a clever pattern printed and held up to a camera, a poisoned example hidden in training data—can shatter that prediction entirely. The model acts confidently wrong. This is not random noise; it is adversarial, exploiting the model's own learned structure.

Why is this hard? Because the gap between human perception and model perception is a chasm. You see a stop sign. A model sees millions of floating-point activations across layers. An attacker can move through that space in ways that leave no trace visible to human eyes. A single pixel difference of 1 part in 256 can flip a classification. The model has learned statistical correlations in images that are alien to human vision—spurious shortcuts, fragile boundaries between classes.

The core challenge is that defense and utility are locked in tension. Make a model more robust to adversarial examples, and it often gets worse at normal tasks. Defend against backdoors (hidden triggers planted during training), and you risk breaking the model's abilities altogether. Detect out-of-distribution inputs (things the model never trained on), and you might falsely reject things it should handle. Every defense costs something.

And the threat surface is vast. Attacks come at training time (poisoning data, injecting backdoors), at test time (adversarial images, jailbreaks via prompt manipulation), and through the model's generative abilities (using its own text-to-image or video generation to encode malicious behavior). Attacks hide in semantic space, not just pixel space. Attackers work without model access (black-box), with gradients, through federated learning where data never leaves users' devices.

The families — how the field tackles it

Adversarial examples: pixels as weapons

The canonical attack: tweak an image in imperceptible ways to fool a classifier. Papers here explore how to craft, transfer, and evade these attacks across models and domains. AdvFM uses flow-matching to perturb velocity fields rather than images directly, improving transferability across defenses. Generative Adversarial Perturbations with Cross-paradigm Transferability targets crowd counting (both point-regression and density-map paradigms at once) with GradCAM-guided perturbations. VCP-Attack operates in semantic space via contrastive projections for superior black-box transferability. The Power of Decaying Steps stabilizes sign-based optimizers (fast, discrete attacks) by enforcing monotone decreasing step-sizes. DASH meta-learns over a pool of 10 heterogeneous attacks, yielding both stronger and stealthier examples. Improving Adversarial Transferability with Local Perturbation Augmentation leverages directional and magnitude characteristics across augmented subspaces. RaPA identifies that adversarial examples rely on small parameter subsets, then randomizes them to broaden transferability. Transform to Transfer learns which augmentations to apply rather than using fixed transformations, adaptively tuning attack effectiveness across models.

Backdoors: the poison in training

An attacker injects triggers into training data or model weights so that under specific conditions (a pattern in the image, a certain prompt, a type of input), the model misbehaves on command. The core tension: backdoors should be invisible in normal operation. Towards Human-Imperceptible Backdoor Attacks on Text-to-Image Diffusion Models maintains visual-textual semantic alignment while encoding triggers imperceptibly. When LoRA Betrays shows backdoors can hide in low-rank adapters (efficient fine-tuning layers), making them harder to spot. FlowHijack targets flow-matching VLA (vision-language-action) models by exploiting ODE amplification of early errors. TINA reveals that visual knowledge persists in text-to-image models even after text-pathway erasure, enabling text-free inversion attacks. Unleashing Stealthy Backdoor Pandemic enables one infected diffusion model to propagate backdoors passively to unlimited downstream classifiers. Exposing Functional Fusion identifies a new class of backdoors operating in semantic space rather than input space, making them harder to detect via pixel inspection. Phantom uses dynamic object interactions and NMS (non-maximum suppression) to create physically-grounded backdoors that adapt across environments. Batman decouples benign and malicious knowledge into orthogonal subspaces so the model learns both without interference. Towards Stealthy and Effective Backdoor Attacks on Lane Detection uses diffusion-based trigger generation optimized via gradient-attention analysis, making backdoors naturalistic.

Out-of-distribution detection: knowing what you don't know

A model trained on cats and dogs should admit uncertainty or rejection when shown a zebra. OOD detection methods learn to identify inputs far from the training distribution. RankOOD applies listwise ranking objectives (from LLM literature) to capture entire output distributions. Neural Distribution Prior for LiDAR OOD Detection adaptively reweights OOD scores to account for class imbalance via learnable priors. Mitigating Simplicity Bias in OOD Detection leverages object co-occurrence patterns to learn complex scene-object relationships instead of relying on spurious shortcuts. The Invisible Gorilla Effect uncovers that artifact-ROI color similarity dominates OOD detection performance—a blind spot. Geometry-driven OOD Detectors Are Class-Incremental Learners unifies classification and OOD rejection via geometric structure (ETF anchors), enabling task routing without separate predictors. ANTS uses large multimodal LLMs to adaptively shape negative textual space at test time. Image-based Outlier Synthesis generates realistic OOD examples from in-distribution data, sidestepping the need for external OOD datasets. Bypassing the Transport Plan shows that complete optimal-transport plans are redundant for OOD detection; dynamic reweighting extracts signals directly from distribution weights. KLIP leverages KL divergence between diffusion priors and posteriors for calibration-free OOD detection that localizes both dataset-level and image-level shifts. Enhancing Out-of-Distribution Detection with Extended Logit Normalization improves both confidence calibration and OOD detection through normalized logits.

Membership inference and privacy attacks: extracting training secrets

Given just a model's outputs, can you tell if a specific image was in its training set? If so, you leak private training data membership. A Unified Perspective on Adversarial Membership Manipulation in Vision Models simultaneously formulates both false assertion (claiming membership when absent) and falsification (hiding true membership). Black-box Membership Inference Attacks on Pre-training Data of Image-generation Models shifts from image-space to text-space perturbations, exploiting that text embeddings remain unnoised and locally overfitted. Stealing Split Learning Bottom Models by Recovering Embedding Geometry recovers model parameters despite defenses by preserving neighborhood structure in embeddings. What Your Features Reveal achieves one-step high-fidelity feature inversion in split DNNs with fewer than 4,096 image-feature pairs. FedRE (Federated learning) mitigates inversion attacks via representation entanglement that blends cross-category information while preserving communication efficiency.

Defense against adversarial attacks and jailbreaks

Once you know attacks work, how do you stop them? Defenses range from adversarial training (robustifying via attack-then-train cycles) to test-time adaptation and input preprocessing. Robustness Under Data Scarcity combines few-shot learning, adversarial robustness, and continual learning for evolving, data-scarce environments. FedCART addresses long-tailed distributions in federated adversarial training by decoupling features and retraining classifiers on balanced prototypes. Taming the Long Tail uses adaptive perturbation scheduling for balanced adversarial training on imbalanced data. AntiStyler removes style via transfer-based preprocessing without training, offering zero-shot defense against adversarial patches on object detectors. Towards Robust Vision Transformers analyzes path dependency in residual connections as the primary bottleneck for ViT robustness, then applies a practical two-stage adversarial training fix. A Combination of Noise and Bilateral Filters proves theoretically that Gaussian noise and filtering achieve supralinear robustness via complementary mechanisms. Test-Time Attention Purification detects backdoors in large vision-language models via attention anomaly analysis. LaSM (Layer-wise Scaling) identifies pop-up attack vulnerabilities in GUI agents through attention divergence patterns and mitigates via selective layer-wise modulation. Mitigating Error Amplification in Fast Adversarial Training corrects gradient errors and adapts optimization in single-step adversarial training.

Vision-language model safety: when text and images collide

Modern multimodal models (VLMs, MLLMs) fuse vision and language. Attackers exploit this fusion—feeding adversarial images paired with specific prompts, hiding unsafe requests in seemingly benign images, or manipulating text-image alignment. DPL: Decoupled Prototype Learning applies prototype learning to VLMs for adversarial and distribution-shift robustness. AGFT preserves cross-modal semantic structure through soft alignment supervision during fine-tuning for zero-shot adversarial robustness. Diagnosing and Repairing Unsafe Channels in VLMs uses causal discovery to identify unsafe FFN channels, then projects them into safe subspaces. Principled Steering via Null-space Projection steer activations without harming utility by constraining perturbations to null spaces. Dynamic Token Reweighting for Robust VLMs defends jailbreaks by dynamically adjusting token importance in KV cache based on safety-relevant signals. Hierarchically Robust Zero-shot VLMs applies hyperbolic geometry to enable exponential margin growth across hierarchy depths. TTP: Test-Time Padding uses spatial padding-induced similarity shifts as a dataset-agnostic adversarial detector, recovering attention with trainable padding. A Provable Energy-Guided Test-Time Defense minimizes input energy under theoretical guarantees. Towards Highly Transferable Vision-Language Attack uses dynamic contrastive interactions to comprehensively explore semantic space. V-Attack identifies value features in attention blocks as superior handles for precise local semantic control. Jailbreaking VLMs via Dissonance-Guided Suffix Optimization leverages misalignment signals from unaligned models to guide attacks with high transferability. LLaVAShield provides comprehensive safety for multi-turn multimodal conversations. Multi-Paradigm Collaborative Adversarial Attack jointly optimizes across learning paradigms with adaptive feature aggregation.

Generative model attacks and defenses: poisoning the pipes

Text-to-image and video models can be attacked at inference time or poisoned during generation. Defenses embed invisible watermarks, detect unauthorized use, or disrupt generation processes. UniDef defends against unauthorized image manipulation by disrupting diffusion distribution consistency globally. PECCAVI addresses visual paraphrase attacks on watermarks, designing watermarking explicitly to resist generative attacks. DeepProtect combines global identity dilution and localized attribute distortion to prevent face-swapping. Anti-I2V applies perturbations in both Lab and frequency domains with layer-wise semantic disruption against image-to-video models. GenBreak red-teams text-to-image models jointly optimizing for toxicity, filter bypass, prompt cleanliness, and diversity via multi-objective GRPO. JANUS studies jailbreak vulnerabilities in text-to-image models through distribution-based analysis. Adapter Shield embeds reversible encryption and protective adversarial coatings for authentication-integrated zero-shot image-to-image protection. Red-teaming Retrieval-Augmented Diffusion Models poisons knowledge bases to attack RAG-diffusion systems. RunawayEvil comprehensively studies jailbreaking vulnerabilities in image-to-video generation. When Safety Collides addresses harmful conflicts in multi-category safety guidance through dynamic category-specific steering. BlackMirror detects backdoors in text-to-image models via instruction-response semantic deviation rather than image similarity. TEAR red-teams text-to-video models by exploiting temporal aggregation—distributing unsafe content across frames to evade per-frame checks.

Physical and multimodal attacks: beyond pixels

Adversarial threats extend beyond digital images. Attackers print patches, wear adversarial clothing, manipulate physical scenes, and exploit multimodal systems. Thermally Activated Dual-Modal Adversarial Clothing combines visible-infrared modality evasion with thermal activation for on-demand invisibility. Physical Adversarial Clothing Evades Visible-Thermal Detectors uses non-overlapping RGB-T patterns for full-360° attacks across fusion architectures. Fractal Camouflage applies bio-inspired fractal geometry for multi-scale infrared attacks optimized via PSO. When Robots Obey the Patch proposes universal transferable patch attacks on robotic systems. SABER designs 3D-consistent, non-invasive adversarial objects for BEV detectors, exposing over-reliance on contextual cues. CamPI demonstrates physical attacks through camera power signal injection at the sensor level. Learning Mutual View Information Graph for Adaptive Adversarial Collaborative Perception captures vulnerabilities across diverse autonomous-vehicle defensive configurations. All Vehicles Can Lie defends collaborative perception in untrusted settings via inter-frame temporal consistency. DualMirror uses CAPTCHAs fusing contour illusions with adversarial perturbations to not only block MLLMs but induce them to reveal their mechanisms. Hierarchical Attacks for Multi-Modal Multi-Agent Reasoning simultaneously targets coordination and multimodal reasoning in multi-agent systems.

Robustness through understanding and adaptation

Some defenses don't fight attacks directly but make models more inherently robust by understanding failure modes or adapting at test time. STAR: Test-Time Adaptation uses Fisher score separability and Legendre-Fenchel pseudo-labels for selective test-time prompt adaptation in VLMs. Back to Source: Domain Compensation decouples domain and semantic shifts via feature alignment with structural regularization. Low-Rank Test-Time Training for Point Cloud Models applies decoder-free parameter-efficient adaptation preserving pretrained robustness. Denoise and Align combines pseudo-label denoising with cross-resolution alignment for robust panoramic segmentation under domain shift. Towards Robust Vision Transformers: Path Dependency Analysis identifies residual connections as bottlenecks and proposes two-stage adversarial training. The Geometry of Robustness grounds fine-tuning in PAC-Bayesian theory, simultaneously optimizing parameter-space curvature and feature-space invariance. When CLIP Sees More uses multi-view guidance and corruption-aware weighting for adaptive test-time defense. Immunizing Models Against Harmful Long-Horizon Fine-Tuning makes fine-tuning contractive to bound attacker progress. Protego combines hypersensitivity loss and 3D pose-invariant deformation for privacy protection against face recognition. SafeLogo integrates adversarial optimization into visual defense prompts with micro-regional constraints. Principled Steering provides null-space constrained activation steering with utility preservation guarantees.

Where it stands

The field has built a rich toolkit. Adversarial robustness via adversarial training works reasonably well in controlled settings, though it remains expensive and imperfect. Out-of-distribution detection is mature for standard benchmarks but brittle when assumptions (about what counts as "far") break. Backdoor defenses exist but must be paired with detection; a model may be robust to attacks yet still harbor a trigger if the attacker is clever. Watermarking and copyright protection show promise via dual-injection and encryption, but generative models can often invert, regenerate, or erase marks via semantic-aware attacks.

The frontier: multimodal safety remains fractured—defenses for one modality or attack type often fail for others. Federated and collaborative settings introduce new attack surfaces (poisoned peers, inference-time inference attacks) that are only beginning to be addressed systematically. Physical attacks scale poorly; once a few adversarial patches work, defenders scramble to catch up. The deepest open question is whether robustness and utility can ever be truly decoupled, or whether they are fundamentally intertwined in the geometry of learned representations. Early signs suggest geometry (loss-landscape curvature, feature manifold alignment, null-space structure) may be the key—but that work is still in its infancy.
