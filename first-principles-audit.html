The product promise

A reader should be able to start with no math, ML, benchmark, optimization, systems, or causal-inference background and still understand what problem each paper family is solving, why the problem exists, what mathematical idea matters, and what breaks without it.

Every explanation should answer: what is the everyday problem, why is it hard, what quantity or tradeoff is underneath, what idea makes progress possible, where else the same idea appears, and what limitation remains.

End-to-end goal

Rewrite the site so every broad theme, subtheme, paper family, individual paper, and math page can be read as one plain-language story: what is the real-world difficulty, what has to be measured or preserved, what tradeoff nature imposes, what mathematical move the paper uses, and why that move matters beyond the benchmark table.

The standard is deliberately strict. Do not assume the reader knows what a representation, latent space, loss, causal intervention, metric, optimizer, scaling law, or system bottleneck is. Introduce each idea from everyday experience first, then connect it to the technical term only after the intuition is stable.

For paper families, the target is not a list of papers that share a keyword. The target is a first-principles family portrait: these papers are all trying to make the same hidden quantity behave, they fail for related reasons, and their methods are different attempts to control the same underlying pressure.
