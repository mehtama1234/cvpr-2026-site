Attacking and defending — robustness across CVPR & Robotics

The why page showed the decision boundary is paper-thin. This page shows what the field does about it: a three-way race of attacks, defenses and (rarely) proofs — and the sharp turn where, in a robot, a fooled percept is not a wrong label but a wrong move. How big it is

corpusfootprintthe shape of it
CVPR 2026 (vision)~90-110 papers (~2.5%) on attacks & defensesattack-heavy (~55%); the target has shifted to VLMs / VLAs / diffusion / 3D; certified robustness is nearly absent
Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)~22 pure adversarial + ~130 broader robustness-as-safetydedicated attacks are a thin slice; “know when perception is unreliable” is one of the largest safety themes

Two things to hold onto. In vision, the fragile boundary is being re-probed on every new model, attacks outnumber defenses, and almost nobody can prove robustness. In robotics, the pure attacks are rarer — but the moment you widen to “catch a bad percept before it becomes a bad action,” robustness becomes one of the field's central obsessions.

The uses — attacks, defenses, and safety

Eight recurring threads. Uses 1-5 are the vision attack/defense race; 6-8 are the robotics turn where robustness becomes physical safety (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Craft a better attack — imperceptible, transferable, physical

The corpus is attack-heavy, and the frontier is making the nudge smaller, more transferable to models you can't see, and realizable in the physical world. Random noise does nothing; the art is aiming the perturbation perfectly while keeping it invisible.
CVPR'26DASH: A Meta-Attack Framework for Synthesizing Effective and Stealthy Adversarial Examples — meta-learns over 10 base attacks with a joint success + imperceptibility (SSIM) objective — stronger AND stealthier on 7 RobustBench models.
CVPR'26AdvFM: Lookahead Flow-Matching Velocity-Field Attacks — perturbs the flow-matching velocity field instead of pixels, staying on the data manifold for imperceptible, transferable, defense-resistant examples.
CVPR'26The Power of Decaying Steps: Enhancing Attack Stability and Transferability for Sign-based Optimizers — fixes the non-convergence of the FGSM/PGD optimizer family with a decaying step size — transfer 59% to 67%.
CVPR'26Improving Adversarial Transferability with Local Perturbation Augmentation — augments the perturbation subspace for 80% attack success across 11 CNN and ViT models.

2 · Break the NEW models — VLMs, VLAs, diffusion, 3D

The decisive 2026 shift is the target. The fragile boundary didn't go away when the field moved to vision-language models, diffusion generators, robot policies and 3D scenes — each new architecture re-opens it with a new attack surface (image tokens, ODE dynamics, Gaussian proliferation).
CVPR'26V-Attack: Targeting Disentangled Value Features for Controllable Adversarial Attacks on LVLMs — attacks attention value features for precise semantic control — +36% success, transfers to GPT-4o.
CVPR'26SABER: Spatially Consistent 3D Universal Adversarial Objects for BEV Detectors — 3D-consistent objects that fool autonomous-driving BEV detectors by manipulating the environment.
CVPR'26RemedyGS: Defend 3D Gaussian Splatting Against Computation Cost Attacks — a denial-of-service angle unique to the new renderers — inputs that trigger runaway Gaussian growth (47GB to 11GB).
CVPR'26Omni-Attack: Adversarial Attacks on Open-Ended VQA in Black-Box Multimodal LLMs — black-box transfer attacks on open-ended multimodal question answering.

3 · Defend — adversarial training, purification, and (rarely) proof

Three defense families: bake the attack into training (adversarial training), scrub a perturbed input back toward the clean manifold (purification), or prove a safe radius (certification). The corpus shows the first two thriving and the third almost absent — a finding in itself.
CVPR'26A Combination of Noise and Bilateral Filters Achieve Supralinear and Scalable Adversarial Robustness — noise + filtering combine super-linearly — #2 on AutoAttack at a fraction of adversarial training's cost.
CVPR'26Mitigating Error Amplification in Fast Adversarial Training — fixes the catastrophic overfitting that makes cheap single-step training fail — robust and 3-5x faster.
CVPR'26Verifying Neural Network Robustness with Dual Perturbations — formal verification that finds up to 99% adversarial examples on networks previously claimed 100% robust — the cleanest certified-vs-empirical cautionary tale.
CVPR'26A Provable Energy-Guided Test-Time Defense — a training-free, theoretically-backed test-time defense for vision-language models.

4 · Poison the training — backdoors

A different attack in time: instead of perturbing a test input, corrupt the training so the model learns a hidden trigger-to-target rule — invisible on clean data, catastrophic on the trigger. A large, self-contained cluster, increasingly aimed at generative models and the model supply chain.
CVPR'26Unleashing Stealthy Backdoor Pandemic by Infecting a Single Diffusion Model — one infected diffusion model passively propagates backdoors into unlimited downstream classifiers — a supply-chain pandemic.
CVPR'26When LoRA Betrays: Backdooring Text-to-Image Models by Masquerading as Benign — a backdoor hidden inside a benign-looking LoRA adapter — the shareable-adapter economy weaponized.
CVPR'26Logit-Margin Repulsion for Backdoor Defense — a universal defense against classic and quantization-conditional backdoors using 0.1% clean data.

5 · Name the tradeoff — why robustness costs accuracy

Robust features and merely-discriminative features partly conflict, so a model hardened against worst-case inputs is a little worse on ordinary ones. A line of work makes this geometric and measurable — and extends it to a three-way tension with out-of-distribution generalization.
CVPR'26The Geometry of Robustness (GRACE): Loss Landscape Curvature and Feature Manifold Alignment — names the three-way in-distribution / OOD / adversarial tradeoff and resolves it via curvature + manifold alignment — the definitive tradeoff paper.
CVPR'26Revisiting Visual Corruptions in LVLMs: A Shape-Texture Perspective on Model Failures — traces fragility to shortcut features — models leaning on texture over shape — bridging adversarial and corruption robustness.
CVPR'26Taming the Long Tail: Rebalancing Adversarial Training via Adaptive Perturbation — shows robustness collapses on minority classes — fragility is class-imbalanced.

6 · Attack the sensor, not the pixels — physical attacks on robots

In robotics the attack surface is the physical sensor. A spoofed laser or radar return feeds a false world-model straight into a committed maneuver — no digital access required.
ROBOTICS'25Slamspoof: Practical Lidar Spoofing Attacks on Localization Systems — laser-injection guided by a per-point vulnerability score forces over 4.2m (a full lane) of localization error on real vehicles.
ROBOTICS'25Gradient-Based Adversarial Attacks on Deep LiDAR Odometry — perturbs consecutive point clouds to corrupt the ego-motion estimate — a new attack surface.
ROBOTICS'25Multi-Task Robustness Enhancement Framework against Various Adversarial Patches — self-supervised patch localization + inpainting to defend the classic sticker-on-a-sign threat across tasks.

7 · When a wrong output is a wrong action — policies & VLAs

The defining robotics twist: an end-to-end policy maps pixels straight to torques, so a fooled input silently produces a wrong action with no label to inspect. The language-model era makes the instruction itself an attack surface.
ROBOTICS'25Jailbreaking LLM-Controlled Robots — adversarial prompts bypass guardrails and elicit harmful physical actions at up to 100% success on a deployed commercial robot — the headline wrong-label-becomes-wrong-action result.
ROBOTICS'25On the Vulnerability of LLM/VLM-Controlled Robotics — formalizes input perturbations; simple instruction or visual tweaks degrade manipulation success by up to 22%.
ROBOTICS'25Towards Physically Realizable Adversarial Attacks in Embodied Vision Navigation — multi-view, physically-transferable perturbations that misguide a navigation agent.

8 · Know when perception is unreliable — the safety layer

A robot has no ground-truth label at runtime, so it must estimate its own competence and fall back to a safe action when the percept is out-of-distribution. This is the single largest robotics robustness bucket — robustness as a monitor, not just a hardened network.
ROBOTICS'25Competency-Aware Planning for Probabilistically Safe Navigation Under Perception Uncertainty (PaRCE) — a region-level competency score feeds the planner and measurably cuts collisions with unfamiliar obstacles.
ROBOTICS'25OCCUQ: Efficient Uncertainty Quantification for 3D Occupancy Prediction — calibrated epistemic uncertainty under fog, missing sensors and adversarial corruption — beats deep ensembles at OOD detection.
ROBOTICS'25Secure Safety Filter: Safe Flight Control under Sensor Attacks — a control-barrier safety filter that stays provably safe under both noise and adversarial sensor attacks — hardware-validated.

What changes when the loss is a trajectory

The same fragile boundary, but the stakes and the response differ sharply.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
What breaksa label is wrong (cat → dog)an action is wrong — a car misreads a sign, a robot's SLAM drifts a full lane and it crashes
The attack surfacedigital pixelsphysical sensors — laser into LiDAR, radar spoofing, an adversarial obstacle or prompt
When it must be caughtoffline, oncereal time, closed loop — detect within ~100ms and modulate the next command
The defense shapeharden the classifierharden it, plus a runtime competence monitor (know when to distrust perception), plus a certified safety filter
The through-line. In computer vision the loss is a number; in robotics the loss is a trajectory. So robotics rarely stops at “make the network harder to fool” — it adds a detect-and-fall-back loop and often a provable safety envelope, because the system has to keep acting safely even while it is being fooled. Same paper-thin boundary; the difference is what waits on the other side of it.
