Attention · how the field actually uses it

One primitive, everywhere — attention across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued attention from first principles: each token chooses, by content, what to look at. This page shows how universal that one primitive has become — the Transformer is now the default backbone across vision and, increasingly, robotics — and how the two fields spend their energy on it differently. How big it is

Attention is not one method among many — it is the substrate. The footprint across the two corpora (a keyword floor; the true share is higher because most papers take the ViT/DiT backbone as unstated infrastructure):

corpustransformer / attentionwhere the energy goes
CVPR 2026 (vision)~30%+ keyword floor; the near-universal backbonefighting the O(n²) cost (token reduction, cheaper kernels, Mamba) and re-routing attention
Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)~317 papers (8.8%), co-dominant with diffusionsensor fusion, reasoning over agents, and the transformer as a control policy

The split to hold onto: in vision the biggest research effort is making attention cheaper; in robotics it is using attention to fuse senses and reason about other agents. Same primitive, different pressures.

The uses — what attention does

Eight recurring uses. Uses 1-5 are the vision research energy (mostly fighting the cost and steering the routing); 6-8 are the robotics cross-over (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Make attention cheaper — the quadratic wall

By far the biggest research thrust. Every token comparing itself to every other token costs the square of the sequence length — and a 4K image or a long video has a huge number of tokens (in some video models three-quarters of all compute is attention). Two escape routes: send in fewer tokens, or do cheaper math.
Mathematical move

Quantity. In 1 · Make attention cheaper — the quadratic wall, the recurring quantity is which item should look at which other item.

Rule. The mathematical rule is to compute a content-based weighted average, then restrict, compress, or redirect that lookup when full all-pairs comparison is too costly or too loose.

Why this use exists. All uses differ in what the items are: image patches, text tokens, sensors, agents, or action history.

3 · Tell attention where things are — positional encoding

Attention is permutation-blind: it has no built-in sense of where a token sits, so position must be injected. How you inject it decides whether a model trained on short/small inputs generalizes to long/large ones. RoPE is the 2026 workhorse.

6 · Fuse a robot's senses — cross-attention

The most robotics-specific use. A robot sees through fundamentally different modalities — RGB, LiDAR, radar, tactile, proprioception, language — in different frames and densities. Cross-attention is the natural “align X to Y by learned correspondence” operator: queries from one modality attend over another.

7 · Attend to other agents

Unlike a static image, a robot's scene contains other decision-makers — cars, pedestrians, teammate robots — whose futures depend on each other. Attention over an agent set (often graph attention) is the mechanism for “which other agents matter to me right now,” with permutation-invariance over a variable agent count.

What robotics needs that pure vision doesn't

The same weighted-average lookup, under different pressures — and the differences come from what a robot faces that a static image never does.

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025 (ICRA+IROS)
What it attends overpatches of one imageheterogeneous sensors in different frames — camera, LiDAR, radar, touch, language
Who else is in the scenenobody — a still imageother decision-makers whose futures are coupled to yours (attention over agents)
What it outputsa label, a box, a picturean action — the transformer as a control policy (decision/action transformers)
The binding constraintfidelity (offline is fine)real-time on-robot cost — so efficient attention & Mamba matter far more
The through-line. The lookup that lets the word “it” find its noun is the same lookup that lets a robot align a LiDAR return with a camera pixel, attend to the pedestrian stepping out, or emit its next action. Vision spends its energy making that lookup cheaper; robotics spends its energy pointing it at new kinds of things. Learn attention once and both fields open up.
