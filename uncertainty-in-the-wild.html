Knowing what you don't know — uncertainty across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued it from first principles: a model's confidence is untrustworthy — overconfident when wrong, certain even on inputs it has never seen — so you calibrate the number and use disagreement as a real 'I don't know'. This page shows the many fronts, from calibrating and OOD-flagging classifiers to catching a VLM's hallucinations, to a robot deciding when to slow down or ask.

What changes between vision and a robot

axisCVPR 2026 (vision)Robotics 2025
What uncertainty isa number to trust, a flag on strange inputs, a hallucination alarma control input — a reason to slow down, grasp cautiously, or ask
The failure it preventsa confident wrong label or a fabricated answera confident wrong action into the world
Typical tooltemperature scaling, conformal sets, ensembles, OOD scoresrisk-bounded planning, abstention, human-in-the-loop
The stakesa bad predictiona physical mistake
The through-line. Uncertainty is the model's own doubt made measurable — calibrate the confidence, flag the unknown, catch the hallucination. In vision it guards a prediction; on a robot it steers the hand, becoming a safety signal to defer, slow, or ask — the same primitive, a number to trust versus an action to change.
