Self-supervised learning · the collapse sandbox

Watch it collapse — and watch the negatives save it

The why page says the deepest idea in self-supervised learning is collapse: if the only rule is “make two views of a thing agree,” the model can cheat by mapping everything to one point. Here you can trigger it. Each dot is an image (coloured by its hidden class). The attractive force pulls views together; the negatives push different images apart. Turn the negatives off and press Train — watch the whole cloud implode.

Negatives on: press Train and the cloud organizes into clusters. Then flip negatives off and Train again.
each dot = an image (colour = its hidden class) · spread = how much the embeddings vary; spread → 0 is collapse
What to notice. (1) With negatives on, Train: the pull-together force and the push-apart force reach a balance, and because same-class images push each other less, the classes settle into separated clusters — structure, with no labels. (2) Flip negatives off and Train: now nothing resists the pull, so every dot slides to the same point — collapse, spread → 0, and the model has learned nothing. (3) The push strength is the temperature-like knob: too weak and clusters smear together, too strong and they scatter. One attractive force, one repulsive force — that balance is all of contrastive learning.
