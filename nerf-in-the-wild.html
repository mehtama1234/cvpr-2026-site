A scene inside a network — NeRF across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the idea from first principles: store a scene as a continuous function and render it by marching rays and integrating colour and density — beautiful, but hundreds of network queries per pixel, which is exactly why Gaussian splatting overtook it. This page shows where neural fields survived: not as the default scene representation, but as the tool you reach for when the signal is continuous, non-RGB, or physically constrained — and, in a robot, as a map you build online and query to act. Real papers for every use, from the analyzed corpora.

Two things to hold onto. In vision, splatting won photorealistic novel-view synthesis of ordinary scenes, so neural fields kept the frontier where a continuous function is the right model — 4D dynamics, pose-free surfaces, physics and medical signals, uncertainty. In robotics, the answer people want is not colour but geometry and free space — where can I put my gripper, where will I crash.

The uses — from a prettier view to a map a robot plans in

Nine threads. Uses 1-5 are the vision work (reconstruction + the cost fight, dynamic 4D, implicit SDF, beyond-RGB physics/INR, and the honest ground it lost to splatting); 6-9 are the robotics inversion into a queryable map (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · The original job, and the cost fight — reconstruction

The classic NeRF task: recover a scene from photos and render new viewpoints. Nobody re-proposes vanilla NeRF now; papers harden it against real-world nuisances (distractors, exposure drift, unknown poses) or make the volume-rendering step fast enough to compete with splatting — because querying a network hundreds of times per pixel is exactly what splatting beat.
CVPR'26Radiance Meshes for Volumetric Reconstruction — exact volume rendering on a Delaunay tetrahedral mesh — 32% faster than 3D Gaussian splatting at 1440p with no popping.
CVPR'26DiffSoup: Direct Differentiable Rasterization of Triangle Soup for Extreme Radiance Field Simplification — distills a radiance field down to under 20,000 triangles at 3500 FPS on a phone.
CVPR'26MU-GeNeRF: Multi-view Uncertainty-guided Generalizable Neural Radiance Fields for Distractor-aware Scene — decomposes uncertainty into source- and target-view terms to suppress transient distractors.

2 · When the scene moves — dynamic 4D fields

A static field cannot represent a moving scene. These add a time axis — a deformation field warping a canonical field per frame, or a latent scene-state evolving over time — so you can render any moment and sometimes extrapolate past the clip. A niche where continuous-time neural fields still edge out frame-by-frame splatting.
CVPR'26Node-RF: Learning Generalized Continuous Space-Time Scene Dynamics with Neural ODE-based NeRFs — couples a Neural ODE latent state that evolves continuously in time with a NeRF renderer, extrapolating beyond the training frames.
CVPR'26RF4D: Neural Radar Fields for Novel View Synthesis in Outdoor Dynamic Scenes — a 4D space-time neural field driven by radar, rendering novel views in adverse weather where RGB and LiDAR fail.
CVPR'26Inter-Photon-Limited Videography — Neural Flux Fields — a spatiotemporal neural field reconstructing video in extreme low-photon regimes where scene changes outpace photon arrivals.

3 · The zero-crossing is the surface — implicit SDF

Instead of colour-and-density, make the network output a signed distance (how far to the nearest surface) — its zero-crossing is the geometry, giving clean watertight surfaces a fuzzy point cloud cannot. A related use is inverse rendering: query geometry, material, and lighting so the scene can be relit. Many 2026 entries are hybrids — an SDF riding inside a splatting pipeline.
CVPR'26ManifoldNeuS: Manifold-aware View Optimizability for Pose-Free Neural Surface Reconstruction — a NeuS-style SDF surface reconstruction that works without accurate camera poses by analyzing the optimization manifold.
CVPR'26Opti-NeuS: Neural Reconstruction for Dual-Layered Transparent and Opaque Objects — material-aware volume rendering with refraction ray-tracing to reconstruct transparent-over-opaque objects.
CVPR'26Distilling Unsigned Distance Function for Surface Reconstruction from 3D Gaussian Splatting — distills a distance-function prior into a splatting optimization for stable surfaces — an SDF-as-component hybrid.

4 · Where there are no blobs to splat — physics, medical & INRs

The healthiest neural-field niche, precisely because there is nothing to splat: the scene is a continuous physical quantity — X-ray attenuation, plasma emissivity, radio power, an SEM height field — and a coordinate-network with volume rendering is the natural forward model. Includes pure implicit-neural-representation methodology (fitting arbitrary signals with a coordinate MLP).
CVPR'26Dynamic Black-hole Emission Tomography with Physics-informed Neural Fields — two MLPs (emissivity + velocity) constrained by geodesic ray-tracing to reconstruct plasma around a black hole from telescope data.
CVPR'26Generalizable Radio-Frequency Radiance Fields for Spatial Spectrum Synthesis — a generalizable neural radiance field for radio-wave propagation that transfers across scenes without retraining.
CVPR'26SASNet: Spatially-Adaptive Sinusoidal Networks for INRs — fixes SIREN's frequency leakage with spatially-localized masks, strengthening image/SDF/volumetric fitting.

5 · Where it lost ground — generation & semantics moved to splatting

An honest pattern: the DreamFusion-era idea of optimizing a neural field to match a 2D diffusion prior, and distilling CLIP features into a neural feature field, have largely migrated. At CVPR 2026 almost all text-to-3D generates meshes or Gaussians, and language-embedded 3D carries semantics on Gaussians. The field-native survivors are the minority.
CVPR'26LoG3D: Ultra-High-Resolution 3D Shape Modeling via Local-to-Global Partitioning — a 3D VAE generating unsigned distance fields, handling the open/non-manifold topology that signed fields choke on.
CVPR'26Semantic Foam: Unifying Spatial and Semantic Scene Decomposition — attaches explicit semantic feature fields to a volumetric Voronoi decomposition for editable semantics.
CVPR'26Learning Convex Decomposition via Feature Fields — learns continuous feature fields along shape surfaces to decompose objects into convex parts, self-supervised.

6 · A map you build as you move — NeRF/implicit SLAM

Robotics inverts the goal. A robot cannot collect all the photos first — it grows a neural map while it drives from a moving camera or LiDAR, and simultaneously figures out where it is. The headaches are drift, moving objects, and speed, so the work is smoothness constraints, better keyframe picking, and hash-grid tricks to update the field live.
ROBOTICS'25DVN-SLAM: Dynamic Visual Neural Slam Based on Local-Global Encoding — neural-implicit SLAM robust to moving objects without needing semantics or optical flow.
ROBOTICS'25HS-SLAM: Hybrid Representation with Structural Supervision for Improved Dense SLAM — a hash-grid + tri-plane hybrid field with bundle adjustment for global consistency.
ROBOTICS'25Category-level Meta-learned NeRF Priors for Efficient Object Mapping — meta-learned per-category NeRF priors for real-time multi-object mapping, 5x faster than prior-free NeRF.

7 · Fill in the geometry to grasp it — manipulation

To grab an object a robot needs its 3D shape, but the hardest objects — glass, shiny metal, half-hidden clutter — are exactly what depth cameras miss. A neural field fills in that geometry from a few views, then a grasp is read off it. The same continuous queryable function also models the robot's own body for clearance checks.
ROBOTICS'25NeuGrasp: Generalizable Neural Surface Reconstruction with Background Priors for Material-Agnostic Object Grasp Detection — a neural implicit surface plus occupancy prior for grasping transparent and specular objects from sparse views.
ROBOTICS'25Implicit Articulated Robot Morphology Modeling with Configuration Space Neural Signed Distance Functions — a neural SDF of the arm itself, conditioned on joint angles, for whole-arm grasp planning in clutter.
ROBOTICS'25Estimating High-Resolution Neural Stiffness Fields Using Visuotactile Sensors — a continuous implicit stiffness field of deformable objects from a visuotactile sensor.

8 · Is this spot free? — implicit maps for planning

Planning a safe path means asking, over and over, is this spot free, how far to the nearest obstacle, which way does that distance grow. A signed-distance field answers all three in one query — and because it is smooth and differentiable you can pour those queries straight into a trajectory optimizer as collision constraints, and match a live scan against the map to localize.
ROBOTICS'25RMMI: Reactive Mobile Manipulation using an Implicit Neural Map — a neural SDF gives differentiable distance and gradient queries feeding a solver that co-optimizes base and arm motion in clutter.
ROBOTICS'25EASEIR: Efficient and Adaptive Safe-set Estimation via Implicit Representation for High-dimensional Motion Planning — maps workspace coordinates to collision-free configuration sets, 43x faster than analytic methods.
ROBOTICS'25Neuraloc: Visual Localization in Neural Implicit Map With Dual Complementary Features — dual descriptor-and-context implicit fields for localization, 45x storage reduction over prior NeRF localization.

9 · Capture a place, train inside it — digital twins

Robot policies are hungry for photorealistic scenes, and standard simulators look fake enough that policies fail on the real robot. NeRF captures a real place once, then renders it from any new viewpoint — a photorealistic, controllable digital twin to fly a virtual drone through or manufacture training views, bent toward sim-to-real rather than pretty pictures.
ROBOTICS'25FalconGym: A Photorealistic Simulation Framework for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Vision-Based Quadrotor Navigation — NeRF-rendered sim yields zero-shot transfer — 95.8% success over 120 live gate crossings.
ROBOTICS'25The Radiance of Neural Fields: Democratizing Photorealistic and Dynamic Robotic Simulation — dual-NeRF rendering plus crowd simulation for vision-based social-navigation training.
ROBOTICS'25FreeDriveRF: Monocular RGB Dynamic NeRF Without Poses for Autonomous Driving via Point-Level Dynamic-Static Decoupling — pose-free dynamic NeRF reconstruction of driving scenes for data generation.

What changes when the answer is free space, not colour

Same continuous field — opposite question asked of it.

The cost problemslow rendering — often fixed by meshing the fielda real obstacle a robot cannot afford — many moved to splatting or hybrid maps
The through-line. A neural field is a scene you query rather than one you place. Splatting won the photorealism race, so in vision neural fields retreated to continuous, non-RGB, or physically-constrained signals where blobs fit poorly. In robotics the same field is a map the robot grows from its own moving sensor and queries for the geometry it must act on — and NeRF's slow per-ray cost is exactly why many robots moved to splatting or hybrid maps. Vision asks the field for a picture; a robot asks it where it can go.
