Flow matching · how the field actually generates faster

Straight to the picture, everywhere — flow matching across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued the idea from first principles: diffusion walks a curved path from noise to data in dozens of steps; flow matching trains a straight-line velocity field you can cross in a few. This page shows how far that reaches. In vision flow matching is now the default generative backbone; in a robot, few-step generation is the difference between a policy that reacts in time and one that does not. Two things to hold onto. In vision, nearly every paper's payoff is the same sentence — the quality of a many-step diffusion model at a fraction of the sampling cost. In robotics, that same speed-up is not a nicety that saves GPU dollars; it is whether you can act in time at all.

The uses — from a faster image to a real-time policy

Nine threads. Uses 1-6 are the vision work (straight-path image generation, consistency/distillation, video/ 3D/motion, better paths/MeanFlow, restoration/editing, flow matching as a general tool); 7-9 are the robotics inversion into real-time action (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS).

1 · Straighten the path — flow matching for image generation

Standard diffusion samples slowly along a curved noise-to-image path. These train a velocity field that transports noise to images along a straight (or straighter) line, so generation needs far fewer steps — a better objective, a better schedule, or a smarter architecture, all aimed at high-quality images in a few forward passes.

2 · One shot instead of fifty — consistency & distillation

The single largest sub-theme. A pretrained diffusion/flow model is great but slow; these turn it into a 1-4-step generator — distilling a slow teacher into a fast student, or training a consistency model / MeanFlow that jumps straight from noise to data — while keeping the teacher's quality.

4 · Make the transport smarter — better paths & MeanFlow

The straighter and better-coupled the noise-to-data path, the fewer steps you need. One family redesigns the source coupling (pair each data point with a smart noise sample, or use optimal transport); another — MeanFlow — trains the average velocity over an interval so a single big step lands correctly.

5 · Same input, faithful output, fast — restoration & editing

7 · An action in one step — flow-matching policies

Robotics inverts the stakes. A robot must react now, not after 50 denoising steps. Flow matching learns a straight path from noise to action, so a good move pops out in one-to-a-few passes — same multimodal generative behavior, a fraction of the compute. 8 · Make the slow policy real-time — accelerating diffusion policies

The largest, most concrete robotics cluster: teams with a working diffusion policy just want it fast, without retraining. These keep the trained model and change how you sample — reuse computation across steps, warm-start each control tick from the last, or fully denoise only the immediate action. A real-time diffusion policy with no distillation pipeline.

9 · A whole trajectory in one pass — motion generation

Beyond low-level actions, robots need whole trajectories — smooth feasible paths, or a spread of possible futures for other agents. Flow matching generates a whole trajectory (or many) in a single forward pass, and can respect dynamics like acceleration so the paths are actually executable.

What changes when few steps means real-time control

Same lever — straighten or shortcut the sampling path — opposite stakes.

The through-line. Flow matching won because a straight-line, deterministic velocity field makes the speed-up easy to reason about and easy to steer. In vision that buys cheaper, faster generation and has already become the default backbone. In robotics the very same few-step trick is a hard deployment gate: fast enough to control the robot, or not deployable at all. Vision optimizes how expensive generation is; robotics optimizes whether you can act in time.
