NeRF · the volume-rendering sandbox

March a ray, add up the colour

The why page renders a NeRF by shooting a ray through the scene, sampling many points along it, and blending their colours by how much light each one blocks. Here you can turn the one knob that controls it: how many samples to take along each ray. Too few and the picture falls apart; more and it converges — but the number of scene queries climbs right along with it.

What to notice. At 1-2 samples per ray the render is dark and patchy — the ray steps right over the thin coloured regions and misses them, so most pixels come back empty. Add more samples and the blobs fill in and the picture sharpens. But look at the query count: it is the image size times the samples per ray, and it climbs in lockstep — hundreds of network evaluations for a single frame. A Gaussian-splatting renderer instead projects each blob onto the screen once with no per-pixel network at all. That is the whole reason splatting overtook NeRF for capturing ordinary scenes.
