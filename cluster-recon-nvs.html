The problem — from first principles

You have a handful of photographs of a scene or object—taken from different angles—and you want to recreate what that thing looks like from viewpoints where you never pointed a camera. This is the deepest question in computational vision: how do pixels, which are flat and 2D, encode the 3D structure of the real world? And once you understand that structure, how do you render it convincingly from a novel angle?

The core challenge is that the same flat image can come from infinitely many 3D scenarios. A shadow might be a crease or a stain; an edge might be a physical corner or just a sharp texture. The structure is ambiguous. Multiple images give you constraints—a point in one photo can be triangulated to 3D if you know the camera geometry—but real images are noisy, lighting varies unpredictably across shots, and some areas (specularity, transparency, occlusions) actively hide their 3D truth. You must infer geometry that is often invisible and learn to render it faithfully under different lighting and viewing angles.

The payoff justifies the effort. Once you have 3D understanding, you can synthesize what the scene looks like from any angle, edit it, animate it, or ground language and robotics in real spatial structure. The last few years have seen an explosion of competing approaches—neural fields (implicit functions learned in tiny coordinate networks), explicit primitives (clouds of 3D Gaussians), mesh-based methods, and lightweight feed-forward transformers that skip per-scene optimization altogether.

The families — how the field tackles it

Neural radiance fields and implicit function learning

The lineage starting with NeRF treats 3D space as an implicit function: you query a tiny neural network with a 3D coordinate and view direction, and it outputs density and color. Rendering then traces rays through the volume. This has given rise to continuous variants: Evidential Neural Radiance Fields quantifies both aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty in volumetric rendering; Node-RF extends NeRFs to dynamic scenes by coupling Neural ODEs for temporal extrapolation; InfiniDepth uses implicit fields for arbitrary-resolution depth with localized feature gathering; and Generalized Sparse-View 3D Reconstruction integrates both appearance disentanglement for lighting variation and occlusion handling in a feed-forward paradigm. MU-GeNeRF applies uncertainty weighting to suppress transient distractors while preserving geometry, and Seeing through Light and Darkness explicitly models both RGB and event-camera response functions to bridge physical radiance and sensor measurement. These methods operate in continuous space, offering theoretical elegance and memory efficiency, though they trade off against the rendering speed and explicit geometric interpretability of discrete approaches.

Gaussian primitives and splatting

Gaussian Splatting replaces implicit functions with explicit 3D Gaussians—thousands or millions of little ellipsoids, each with position, covariance, spherical harmonic color, and opacity. Rasterizing them directly to 2D is orders of magnitude faster than volumetric ray tracing. This explicitness has spawned a huge family: SR3R eliminates per-scene optimization by predicting 3D Gaussians feed-forward from sparse views; DualSplat leverages reconstruction failures as explicit priors for transient detection; Intrinsic Geometry-Appearance Consistency Optimization enforces coupled geometric-appearance regularization for sparse views; EDGS initializes densely from correspondences instead of starting sparse; Faster-GS consolidates training optimizations for 5x speedup; 3D Gaussian Splatting with Self-Constrained Priors derives geometric priors from rendered depth; and PRIMU adds post-hoc uncertainty estimation without retraining. Radiance Meshes bridges Gaussians and meshes by using Delaunay tetrahedralization for volumetric rendering via triangle rasterization, achieving faster rendering than 3DGS while maintaining quality. The speed and interpretability have made Gaussians the dominant workhorse, but they remain discrete and must carefully manage point density and opacity.

Feed-forward reconstruction without per-scene optimization

Rather than optimize a separate representation for each scene, a growing family trains a transformer once and deploys it at inference time. VGGT (Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer) and its successors—VGGT-Ω, PanoVGGT, VGG-T3, iLRM, FlashVGGT—predict point maps or 3D geometry directly from multi-view images in a single pass. Learning Compact 3D Representations from Feed-Forward Novel View Synthesis distills this into ~2K Gaussians (65x compression); GIFSplat uses lightweight generative priors for iterative refinement; AREA3D unifies 3D perception with vision-language guidance for active view selection; and Flow3r conditions source geometry on target-view camera latents for asymmetric flow supervision. TokenSplat performs feature aggregation at token level with pose-reasoning disentanglement, and From None to All jointly predicts Gaussians and camera parameters from unposed images. ZipMap achieves linear-time complexity with test-time training. These methods trade per-scene optimization for amortized training cost, enabling real-time inference on mobile or streaming settings, though they may generalize less sharply to out-of-distribution geometry.

Multi-view consistency and cross-view fusion

Many challenges—transient objects, reflections, specularities—appear or vanish depending on viewpoint. Methods that explicitly reason about multi-view geometry and consistency can suppress these artifacts. MV-TAP (Tracking Any Point in Multi-View Videos) leverages cross-view information and camera geometry for robust point tracking; LiDAR Prompted Spatio-Temporal Multi-View Stereo fuses multi-view cost volumes with temporal consistency; DriveMVS combines prompt-anchored cost construction with transformer fusion; Multi-view Pyramid Transformer operates inter-view (local-to-global) and intra-view (fine-to-coarse) attention hierarchically; and Parallax to Align Them All uses parallax-aware attention to handle depth-dependent view relationships. WorldStereo maintains geometry-aware memory across multiple trajectories for consistent video generation; 3D-Aware Multi-Task Learning integrates cross-view cost volume correlations explicitly; and Geometry-as-context replaces non-differentiable 3D reconstruction with an interleaved autoregressive model combining geometry and RGB tokens. These approaches embed the physics of multi-view geometry into learning, providing strong geometric priors.

Inverse rendering, illumination, and material decomposition

Many scenes contain reflections, specularity, and complex materials that break the assumption of a simple radiance function. Inverse rendering separates illumination and material properties. Intrinsic Image Fusion for Multi-View 3D Material Reconstruction aggregates inconsistent 2D predictions into consistent 3D material space via distributional matching; PPISP predicts ISP (camera) parameters for fair, realistic novel-view evaluation; Physically Inspired Gaussian Splatting for HDR Novel View Synthesis decomposes appearance into reflectance and illumination with dual-branch design; MatSpray fuses diffusion-based 2D PBR priors with 3D Gaussians via ray tracing and learned merging; LuxRemix enables per-light control with 3D consistency; WildCap combines diffusion priors with differentiable texel-grid lighting; Lafite learns a generative latent field for native 3D texturing; and MatMart offers two-stage progressive inference for faithful texture preservation. These decouple appearance factors, enabling relighting and material editing after reconstruction.

Dynamic scene reconstruction and 4D modeling

Static reconstruction is one problem; capturing motion is another. Deformable Gaussian Occupancy decouples rigid and nonrigid motion via per-Gaussian rigidity masks and factorized 4D distillation; Mesh4D is a feed-forward 4D mesh model with unified latent animation encoding; V-DPM extends Dynamic Point Maps to video, enabling 4D reconstruction and scene flow; MotionScale jointly optimizes appearance, geometry, and motion in dynamic scenes; TagSplat maintains manifold topology through dynamic updates (densification/pruning); 4DSurf uses SDF flow regularization for temporal consistency; Neu-PiG combines multi-scale preconditioned grids with Sobolev preconditioning for fast optimization; DynamicVGGT extends point-map prediction to 4D with explicit future-map prediction; Scaling4D uses optical flow as self-supervised training signal; Any4D factorizes 4D representation to decouple egocentric and allocentric factors; and ClipGStream addresses large-scale motion and long sequences through clip-level streaming. These handle articulation, deformation, and flow while maintaining temporal coherence.

Specialized domains and challenging conditions

The general methods above assume cooperative lighting, opaque objects, and posed images. Real scenes are messier. Thermal is Always Wild addresses photometric drift and limited dynamic range in thermal-only novel view synthesis; NimbusGS decomposes weather degradation into global transmission and local particulates; eRetinexGS combines Retinex modeling with event-frame guidance for low-light enhancement; Dark3R enables Structure from Motion at photon-starved SNR via LoRA-based teacher distillation; RF4D uses radar as primary input for outdoor dynamic scenes; DirectFisheye-GS natively integrates the Kannala-Brandt fisheye model with exact Jacobians; Pose-Free Omnidirectional Gaussian Splatting handles 360-degree videos with depth consistency; PolarGuide-GSDR embeds polarization priors for reflective scenes; SunFaded separates intrinsic albedo from view-dependent lighting in lit scenes; BrepGaussian reconstructs CAD B-Rep geometry from multi-view images; and Long-Tail Internet Photo Reconstruction exploits rather than fights unstructured internet photo distributions. These extend reconstruction beyond studio conditions into the wild.

Applications: avatars, humans, hands, and articulation

Humans are a major application and a special challenge—deformable bodies, cloth, hair, occlusion. HumanNOVA scales avatar creation (100K synthetic assets) via SMPL-conditioned token fusion; Large-scale Codec Avatars shows million-scale pretraining resolves fidelity-generalization tradeoff; UIKA establishes multi-view correspondence via UV-guided dual-space attention; FHAvatar decouples face and hair using distinct Gaussian types; Feed-forward Gaussian Registration achieves 10x speedup via dense correspondence prediction; Registration-Free Learnable Multi-View Face Reconstruction recovers FLAME parameters from predicted vertex positions without correspondence data; Glove2Hand translates gloved HOI videos into bare-hand photorealism; ForeHOI reconstructs 3D objects directly from hand-object interaction videos; RHINO reconstructs 4D hand-object interactions from monocular video; and PARTICULATE predicts complete articulated structures (parts, kinematics, motion) feed-forward from static meshes. Specialized mesh and Gaussian variants handle non-rigid surface and texture.

Where it stands

The field has matured into a spectrum of production-ready techniques. Feed-forward transformers are now the go-to for real-time and mobile applications, Gaussian Splatting dominates quality-vs-speed tradeoffs, and implicit methods remain theoretically elegant for unbounded scenes or uncertainty quantification. A clear trend is the elimination of per-scene optimization: methods like VGGT, iLRM, and their successors train once and ship, enabling interactive applications that were impossible five years ago. Multimodal vision-language models are being integrated (REALM, VLM-3R, MLLMSplat) to ground reconstruction in semantic understanding.

Open problems remain: dynamic scenes and 4D reconstruction are still computationally expensive and less robust than static; robust handling of transparency, specularity, and exotic materials (polarimetry, radar, events) is becoming important for autonomous systems; and generalization to radically out-of-distribution geometry (extreme poses, novel objects, artistic styles) still requires fine-tuning or test-time adaptation. The emergence of foundation models (SAM, CLIP, DINOv2) as supervisory signals for reconstruction is accelerating, as are hybrid approaches that couple optimization with learning. The frontier is now real-world robustness, extreme-view extrapolation, and seamless integration into embodied AI systems that must act in 3D space.
