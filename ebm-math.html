Energy-based & score models — why it works, mathematically

Low energy = high probability. Train and sample the gradient without knowing the partition function.

The problem, as a quantity

Probabilities are unnormalized: p(x) = exp(−E(x)) / Z. The partition function Z = ∫ exp(−E(x)) dx is intractable — you cannot compute it over all of space. This breaks supervised learning (need log p), sampling (need samples from p), and composition (combining multiple models).

The honest pain: MCMC mixing is slow; samples can get stuck in local energy wells.

The core ideas — and why they work

Energy = negative log probability

foundation

Define energy E(x) such that low energy attracts high probability density. Rewrite p(x) = exp(−E(x)) / Z. The unnormalized density does not require Z: just use the GRADIENT.

∇log p(x) = −∇E(x). The partition function Z vanishes in the gradient — it is a constant.

This is the escape hatch: if you only need gradients, you never compute Z.

Langevin dynamics: sample by gradient descent + noise

core

Walk downhill in energy. At each step: x ← x − (ε/2)·∇E(x) + √ε·noise. This is gradient descent with Gaussian noise injected.

WHY IT WORKS: the noise term ensures the chain explores; without it you get a point estimate. With noise, the stationary distribution is p(x). The mixing is slow (MCMC pain), but it requires only ∇E, not Z.

Score matching: learn the gradient directly

core

Instead of learning E(x), learn the score s(x) = ∇log p(x) = −∇E(x) directly via regression on data.

Minimize E_data[(s(x) − ∇log p(x))²]. The trick: you do not need p(x). Rewrite the loss as E_data[||s(x)||² + 2·div(s(x))] using integration by parts; no partition function appears.

Once you have s(x), Langevin sampling works; or you integrate it in diffusion.

Contrastive divergence: push down on data, pull up on samples

training

Gradient of log-likelihood under the energy model: ∇θ log p(x) ∝ ∇θ E(x). The full gradient is E_data[∇θ E(x)] − E_model[∇θ E(x)]. This says: lower energy on real data, raise it on samples from the model.

WHY: the gap between the two expectations is the divergence. This trains the energy landscape without ever computing Z.

The approaches, as variations on the math

Energy for composition: combine multiple models by summing energies: E_combined(x) = E_1(x) + E_2(x). Product of distributions = sum of energies.

Diffusion as modern EBM: diffusion models learn the score s(x, t) = ∇log p(x|t) at noise level t. This is score-based generation; it is the modern reincarnation of energy-based learning. The noise schedule replaces MCMC mixing.

Energy for anomaly detection: high energy = out-of-distribution. Train on clean data; use E(x) as an anomaly score. The model learns an implicit manifold without explicit density.

Energy-guided planning: control and planning add a reward or cost to the energy. Then ∇E guides the system toward goals. Diffusion-based planners use this to sample plans.

1Energy-based generation and composition
E_combined(x) = E_1(x) + E_2(x) + ... Additive energies = product of distributions. Knob: compose arbitrary behaviors by summing learned energy potentials without retraining.
Energy models encode behaviors as unnormalized densities. The key insight is linearity: if p(x) = exp(−E_1(x))/Z_1 and p'(x) = exp(−E_2(x))/Z_2, then p(x)p'(x) ∝ exp(−(E_1 + E_2)), so combined behaviors follow by summing energies. This sidesteps the intractable partition function entirely—you never compute Z—and enables flexible mixing of learned motion primitives, arm policies, and part-level constraints without additional fine-tuning.
Towards Decompositional Human Motion Generation with Energy-Based Diffusion Models — Models complex motion as sum of learned energy primitives: p(motion) = exp(−sum E_i). Diffusion operates over primitive energies, enabling recombination without retraining.
EnergyAction: Unimanual to Bimanual Composition with Energy-Based Models — Composes arm policies via additive energies: E_bimanual = E_left + E_right + E_interaction. Interaction potentials enforce spatial-temporal coherence, dodging bimanual training data scarcity.
FrankenMotion: Part-level Human Motion Generation and Composition — Hierarchical energy scoring over part, action, sequence levels. Automatic LLM-driven part annotation creates structured dataset; energy prefers coherent part compositions over independent fragments.
From Attraction to Equilibrium: Physics-Inspired Semantic Gravitons for Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection — Text-vision alignment modeled as energy equilibrium: gravitons minimize E_interaction between embeddings. Alternating coupling refines attraction/equilibrium forces without multimodal fine-tuning.
2Score matching and distribution matching: learning the gradient of density
s(x,t) = ∇_x log p(x|t) learned via regression on noise-free score matching. Knob: partition the diffusion process into SNR subintervals; train local score experts per phase, chain backward via distribution matching.
Score matching learns the gradient ∇log p directly without computing the partition function Z. By regression on synthetic noise or matching intermediate distributions in diffusion, you obtain s(x) = −∇E(x), enabling either Langevin sampling or deterministic flow. Phased training splits the path into easier subproblems, each with cleaner score signal, dramatically lowering memory and reducing collapse in distilled few-step models.
Phased DMD: Few-step Distribution Matching Distillation via Score Matching within Subintervals — Partitions diffusion into SNR subintervals, training separate score experts per phase. Novel score matching objective derives unbiased estimates at intermediate timesteps without clean samples, chaining phases backward.

InstantViR: Real-Time Video Inverse Problem Solver with Distilled Diffusion Prior — Amortized posterior q(x|y) minimizes KL = reconstruction + score matching against diffusion teacher. Causal autoregressive student with KV caching learns single-pass restoration via distribution matching.
Sketch2Colab: Sketch-Conditioned Multi-Human Animation via Controllable Flow Distillation — Distills sketch-conditioned diffusion teacher into rectified-flow student via energy-based guidance on keyframes, trajectories, contacts. Continuous-time Markov planner modulates flow via phase mixing.
3Energy for the open world: detecting the unknown
E(x) = distance to in-distribution manifold. High energy ⇒ anomalous. Knob: learn orthogonal energy subspaces (known vs. unknown), contrastive margins, and adaptive class-conditional calibration.
Standard classifiers output softmax over seen classes and fail on unknowns. Energy-based OOD detection inverts this: train an energy landscape on clean data, where low energy is familiar and high energy signals novelty. By explicitly modeling unknown modes (separate subspace) and using contrastive margins to repel unknown embeddings away from the known-class energy landscape, the model learns an active representation of anomaly rather than mere soft thresholding on classifier uncertainty.
DEUS: Detecting Unknown Objects via Energy-based Separation for Open World Object Detection — Two orthogonal ETF subspaces (known, unknown) with Helmholtz free energy. Contrastive margins drive unknowns away from known-class energy landscape; contrastive scores prevent interference during continual learning.

The through-line.

The core insight: unnormalized energies hide the intractable partition function in the constant offset. Gradients expose only what you need to learn and sample. Modern diffusion models inherit this freedom.
