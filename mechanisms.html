How the core ideas work

The handful of mechanisms that power computer vision in 2026, each drawn as a simple diagram and explained from first principles — no jargon.

The field looks like a thousand methods, but it runs on a few reusable ideas. Here are the core ones as plain pictures. Each shows up across many of the eight themes — which is why a good idea in one corner lifts the others.

The one bridge — pixels ↔ meaning

A camera turns a 3-D world into a flat grid of brightness numbers; nothing in that grid says fox or 3 metres away. All of computer vision is crossing that gap in two directions: perceive (pixels → meaning) and generate (meaning → pixels). Every theme is one stretch of this bridge.

Diffusion — drawing by un-noising

Start from pure static and remove a little noise at each step, steered by a prompt, until a coherent image appears. The same denoising idea now powers image, video, and 3-D generation. The 2026 twist is distillation: teaching a model to make the same trip in 1–4 steps instead of 25 — the speed-up you saw live on the demos page.

Gaussian Splatting — a scene as blobs

Instead of a mesh, hold a 3-D scene as a cloud of thousands of small, soft, coloured ellipsoids (“Gaussians”). They are fast to render from any viewpoint, so you can fit them to a handful of photos and then fly a virtual camera anywhere. This is why it became the default 3-D representation — it is both accurate and real-time.

The perceive → decide → act loop

For a robot or a car, seeing is not the goal — acting is. Perception turns pixels into a picture of the world; a planner turns that into a decision; the body acts; and because acting changes the world, the loop runs again. Embodied vision is this whole circuit, where a mistake is a wrong turn, not a wrong label.

LoRA — steering a frozen giant

You can't afford to retrain a billion-weight model for every task. So freeze it and learn a tiny low-rank correction B·A that adds onto its weights. It needs little data, adds almost nothing at inference, and can be merged, routed, or even generated — the modular-knowledge idea behind the mixture-of-LoRA work, and the trick that distils diffusion to a few steps.
