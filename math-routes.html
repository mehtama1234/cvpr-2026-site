A single alphabetical grid is useful when you already know what you want. It is not how understanding actually grows. Understanding grows along routes: one idea creates the need for the next one, and the next one reveals why the earlier one was not enough.

Use these routes as guided passes through the corpus. Each path has a center of gravity, but the borders are deliberately porous. Bayes touches Kalman; kernels touch attention; Fourier touches graph smoothing; optimization touches everything because every learnable system eventually has to move its numbers.

Route 1

Probability: from surprise to belief

This route starts with the training objective and ends with uncertainty. Maximum likelihood says: make the data you saw unsurprising. Information theory explains what surprise costs. Bayes says how evidence should update belief. Variational inference, EM, and MCMC are three different compromises when the exact Bayesian answer is too expensive. Kalman filtering is the clean running version: predict, receive a noisy glimpse, correct, and carry the uncertainty forward.

Route 2

Geometry: from similarity to meaning

This route is about turning raw objects into places in a space. Kernels begin with a simple move: judge a new thing by similarity to known things. Embeddings learn that similarity space. CLIP aligns two such spaces, images and words, so meaning can cross between them. Attention turns similarity into a live retrieval mechanism, and Hopfield memory shows the older energy-based version of the same idea: store patterns as attractors, then let a partial cue pull you back toward one.

KernelstoEmbeddingstoCLIPtoAttentiontoHopfield memorytoManifold hypothesis
Route 3

Signals: from waves to neighborhoods

Fourier analysis says a signal can be read as smooth and sharp waves. Convolution uses the same repeated local operation everywhere, and in the frequency view that sliding operation becomes multiplication. Spectral graph theory carries the idea to irregular domains: a graph has its own smooth and sharp modes. GNNs then become learnable smoothing and mixing over that graph, while Mamba and state-space models keep the signal route alive across long sequences.

Route 4

Optimization: how learning moves

Backprop gives every number its share of the blame. Optimizers decide how to spend that blame as a step. Normalization keeps signals in a range where the step is meaningful. Residual connections keep deep systems from losing the path backward. Convex duality is the clean mathematical world where bottoms and constraints can be understood exactly. Natural gradient then asks a sharper question: what if the right step is not the one that moves the parameters farthest, but the one that changes the model's behavior most appropriately?

Route 5

Generation: how distributions move

Generative modeling is the art of moving probability mass from something simple to something structured. VAEs use a tidy latent code with a likelihood objective. GANs create a duel between a generator and a discriminator. Optimal transport makes the movement cost explicit. Normalizing flows require every movement to be reversible. Neural ODEs make the movement continuous. Diffusion and flow matching learn the path from noise to data in different but neighboring languages.

VAEtoGANtoOptimal transporttoNormalizing flowstoNeural ODEstoDiffusiontoFlow matching
Route 6

Structure: how models avoid wasting capacity

This route is about the shape of useful representation. SVD shows that large tables often have a few dominant directions. Bias and variance explain the cost of being too rigid or too jumpy. The information bottleneck asks which bits deserve to survive. Vector quantization turns a continuous space into a learned codebook. Mixture-of-LoRA and distillation compress adaptation and knowledge into smaller forms. Scaling laws then show the outer curve: how error changes as data, parameters, and compute grow.

SVDtoBias-variancetoInformation bottlenecktoVector quantizationtoMixture of LoRAtoDistillationtoScaling laws
Route 7

Control: from prediction to action

Control adds consequence. Autoregressive models make the next piece depend on the pieces before it. World models learn an internal simulator of what happens next. Reinforcement learning optimizes actions by delayed reward. Q-learning stores the future value of a move. Vision-language-action models close the loop with perception and language in the real world. Test-time compute spends extra reasoning at the moment of decision, and adversarial robustness asks whether the decision survives pressure.

Conceptual ladders — how the question changes

The routes are not just lists. Each rung changes the question. That change is where the depth is: the same data is being looked at through a stronger mathematical lens.

Probability ladder

  1. MLE: Which settings make the data we saw least surprising?
  2. Information theory: How many bits does that surprise cost?
  3. Bayes: After seeing evidence, which hidden cause should become more likely?
  4. VI and EM: If exact belief is too large to compute, which usable belief should stand in for it?
  5. MCMC: If sums are impossible, can repeated visits stand in for probability?
  6. Kalman: When evidence arrives over time, how should uncertainty decide the next correction?

Geometry ladder

  1. Kernels: What if similarity is the real coordinate system?
  2. Embeddings: Can meaning be placed into distances and directions?
  3. CLIP: Can images and words share one space?
  4. Attention: Can each item retrieve what it needs from that space while learning the retrieval rule?
  5. Hopfield: Can retrieval be seen as rolling toward a stored pattern?
  6. Manifolds: Is real data a thin surface inside a much larger space?

Signal ladder

  1. Fourier: Which slow and fast waves make this signal?
  2. Convolution: What happens when the same local test is reused everywhere?
  3. Spectral graph: What are slow and fast changes when the domain is a graph?
  4. GNN: How far should information be smoothed across edges?
  5. Mamba: What summary of the past is enough for the next step?
  6. Video: Which facts persist through time, and which ones are changing?

Optimization ladder

  1. Backprop: How does one error become blame for every internal number?
  2. Optimizers: Given the blame, how should the numbers move?
  3. Normalization: How do we keep the numbers in a range where movement still works?
  4. Residuals: How do we let depth refine information instead of destroying it?
  5. Duality: How do constraints show up as prices and certificates?
  6. Natural gradient: What if distance should be measured by changed predictions, not changed settings?

Generation ladder

  1. VAE: Can data be rebuilt from a tidy random code?
  2. GAN: Can a learned judge teach realism better than a blunt pixel loss?
  3. Optimal transport: What is the cheapest way to move one probability shape into another?
  4. Flows: Can the movement stay reversible so likelihood remains exact?
  5. Neural ODE: Can generation be a continuous learned motion?
  6. Diffusion and flow matching: Can noise be turned into data by following a learned direction field?

Structure ladder

  1. SVD: Which few directions carry most of this table?
  2. Bias-variance: Is the model too stiff, or too eager to chase accidents?
  3. Bottleneck: Which bits predict the target, and which bits distract?
  4. VQ: Can continuous signals become a useful learned vocabulary?
  5. LoRA and distillation: Which small changes or small students preserve the large model's useful behavior?
  6. Scaling laws: How does error change as resources grow?

Control ladder

  1. Autoregression: What should come next, given what has already happened?
  2. World models: What future can be imagined before acting?
  3. RL: Which action leads to the best long-run result?
  4. Q-learning: How can future value be stored as a number for each move?
  5. VLA: How do seeing and language become physical action?
  6. Test-time compute and robustness: When the decision matters, how much extra search and stress-testing should happen before acting?

The shortest serious pass

If you only want one high-leverage route, read this chain: Backprop, MLE, Information theory, Kernels, Attention, Diffusion, Kalman filtering. That sequence gives you learning, probability, geometry, retrieval, generation, and state estimation without drowning in the full map.
