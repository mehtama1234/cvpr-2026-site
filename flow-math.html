Optical flow — why it works, mathematically

Motion from intensity change — when one constraint meets two unknowns

The problem, as a quantity

A moving point keeps its brightness: I(x,y,t) = I(x+u, y+v, t+1). If you linearize with a first-order Taylor expansion, you get I_x·u + I_y·v + I_t = 0 — one equation, two unknowns (u,v). This is the aperture problem: you can only recover motion along the image gradient (perpendicular to edges); motion parallel to an edge is invisible. One gradient direction gives one constraint; motion has two degrees of freedom; you need a second independent gradient or spatial information to pin it down.

The core ideas — and why they work

Brightness constancy

foundation

A pixel's intensity doesn't change as it moves — only its position does. Differentiate I(x(t), y(t), t) with respect to time: dI/dt = I_x·(dx/dt) + I_y·(dy/dt) + I_t = 0, where (dx/dt, dy/dt) = (u,v) is the velocity. This is exact under pure translation and Lambertian surfaces. It's one linear constraint per pixel, but motion is 2D — you always need more.

Aperture problem

core limitation

At a horizontal edge (only I_y ≠ 0), the equation I_x·u + I_y·v + I_t = 0 becomes I_y·v = -I_t. You can solve for v but u is unconstrained — you cannot tell if the edge is moving left, right, or standing still along its own direction. The gradient vector points normal to edges; only motion normal to the edge leaves a brightness signature. Edges hide along-edge motion from local intensity alone.

Lucas-Kanade: corners unlock flow

local resolution

At a corner, gradients point in two independent directions (I_x and I_y both nonzero and uncorrelated). Within a small window over a corner, you have multiple independent linear equations in (u,v) — enough to solve least-squares. The corner acts as a natural anchor for 2D motion. Smooth regions and single-orientation edges still fail, but corners everywhere (textured scenes) anchor the whole field.

Horn-Schunck: smoothness prior

global resolution

Instead of requiring corners, assume the motion field is smooth: neighboring pixels move similarly. Minimize a total energy = Σ(I_x·u + I_y·v + I_t)² + λ·(‖∇u‖² + ‖∇v‖²). The first term enforces brightness constancy; the second term penalizes spatial gradients in u and v. Smooth prior propagates information from constrained regions (corners, edges) to underconstrained regions (flat areas). Trade-off λ controls smoothness strength.

Variational methods: dense, regularized flow

modern framework

Generalize Horn-Schunck to learned regularizers, anisotropic smoothness, or learned priors trained on real motion statistics. Deep networks learn to balance data (brightness) and prior (smoothness) adaptively. The core remains: one pixel = one brightness constraint, two unknowns → you need regularization or local multi-directional gradients. Learned regularizers replace hand-tuned λ.

The approaches, as variations on the math

Window-based (Lucas-Kanade): gather all brightness equations in a small patch; solve 2×2 least-squares per pixel. Fast, preserves edges, fails on flat textureless regions. Variational (Horn-Schunck, FlowNet, RAFT): minimize global energy over the whole frame with a smoothness prior or learned regularizer. Handles textureless regions, spreads information spatially, slower. Matching-based: search for the best correspondence patch between frames (template matching, cost volumes). Coarse, robust to appearance change, requires descriptors. Coarse-to-fine: estimate flow at low resolution, upsample, refine at high resolution. Handles large displacements without huge search windows. Learning-based (FlowNet, PWCNet, RAFT): train a CNN or recurrent architecture end-to-end on synthetic data (FlyingChairs, FlyingThings3D). Learn features, cost volumes, and iterative refinement implicitly.

The through-line.

One pixel sees only one gradient; two degrees of freedom are underdetermined. Corners (two independent gradients) or spatial smoothness priors resolve the aperture problem. Motion is observable only along the gradient; along-edge motion is invisible without additional structure.
