Diffusion · how the field actually uses it

One denoiser, many outputs — across CVPR & Robotics

The why page argued diffusion from first principles: start from noise and undo it, step by step. This page shows how far that one idea reaches — from pictures to 3D to robot motion — across CVPR 2026 and the 2025 robotics conferences, with real papers for every use. The through line: it is the same reverse-diffusion machinery every time; only the thing being denoised changes.

How big it is

Diffusion is not one technique among many — it is now the default generative substrate, the way CNNs were once the default backbone. The footprint across the two corpora:

CVPR 2026 (vision)~1,107~27% of the whole conferencetext-to-image / video generation (~489)

The headline for the whole page: in vision the biggest use is making pixels; in robotics the biggest use is making actions. Same denoiser, different output.

The uses — what each one denoises

Nine recurring uses, from pixels to motion. Each is a first-principles answer to a real problem; the papers are real exemplars (CVPR'26 vision · ROBOTICS'25 ICRA/IROS). Uses 1-5 & 9 are vision; 6-8 are the robotics cross-over.

1 · Make pixels from meaning — text-to-image & video

The flagship. Because diffusion learns to reverse noising, it samples p(image | text) with stable training (no GAN collapse) and scales cleanly with data and compute — which is why the field moved off GANs entirely. The frontier now is resolution, length, and real-time streaming.
CVPR'26UltraFlux: Data-Model Co-Design for Native 4K Text-to-Image Generation — co-designs positional encoding, VAE and optimization for native 4K multi-aspect generation.
CVPR'26PixelDiT: Pixel Diffusion Transformers for Image Generation — a diffusion transformer operating directly in pixel space, not a latent.
CVPR'26StreamDiT: Real-Time Streaming Text-to-Video Generation — low-latency streaming text-to-video.
CVPR'26LoL: Longer than Longer, Scaling Video Generation to an Hour — pushes video diffusion to hour-scale temporal horizons.
CVPR'26FARMER: Flow AutoRegressive Transformer over Pixels — unifies normalizing flows and autoregression for tractable-likelihood pixel generation.

2 · Lift a 2D prior into 3D — shapes, scenes, novel views

3D ground truth is scarce, but a 2D (or video) diffusion model already knows what objects look like from every angle. Papers lift that prior into 3D — or run diffusion natively over 3D latents, Gaussians or meshes — to escape slow per-scene optimization.
CVPR'26DIFF4SPLAT: Repurposing Video Diffusion Models for Dynamic Scene Generation — feed-forward deformable 3D Gaussians straight from a video diffusion model, no test-time optimization.
CVPR'26Gen3R: 3D Scene Generation Meets Feed-Forward Reconstruction — uses a reconstruction model (VGGT) as the geometry encoder of a diffusion VAE, unifying reconstruction and generation.
CVPR'26MeshFlow: Efficient Artistic Mesh Generation via MeshVAE and Flow-based DiT — native mesh generation via a flow-matching diffusion transformer.
CVPR'26CUPID: Generative 3D Reconstruction via Joint Object and Pose Modeling — a flow-based joint model over canonical 3D objects and camera pose from a single image.

3 · Edit a real image — invert, then re-generate

If you can invert a real image back to the noise that would generate it, you can re-run the reverse process under a new instruction and edit while preserving identity. The whole game is the tension between faithfulness to the source and following the edit.
CVPR'26FlashIn: Fast and Accurate Image Inversion for Real-time Image Editing — attacks the inversion bottleneck that gates every edit pipeline.
CVPR'26Delta Rectified Flow Sampling for Text-to-Image Editing — a time-dependent shift term cancels background drift in rectified-flow edits.
CVPR'26FlowDirector: Training-Free Flow Steering for Precise Text-to-Video Editing — steers the flow ODE at inference for video edits with no training.
CVPR'26SliderEdit: Continuous Image Editing with Fine-Grained Instruction Control — dials edit strength continuously by scaling an instruction-specific update.

4 · Solve an inverse problem — restoration & super-resolution

Restoration is ill-posed: infinitely many clean images explain one degraded input. A diffusion prior supplies the missing detail as a learned distribution, turning “hallucinate plausibly” into a principled posterior sample.
CVPR'26UniLDiff: Unlocking the Power of Diffusion Priors for All-in-One Image Restoration — one model handles compound degradations with no predefined degradation labels.
CVPR'26PnP-CM: Consistency Models as Plug-and-Play Priors for Inverse Problems — casts a consistency model as a proximal operator, solving linear and nonlinear inverse problems in 2-4 network evals.
CVPR'26Residual Diffusion Bridge Model for Image Restoration — unifies diffusion bridges via residual noise modulation for spatially adaptive restoration.
CVPR'26gQIR: Generative Quanta Image Reconstruction — a latent diffusion prior bridges extreme photon-starved single-photon imaging.

5 · The generator as a perception engine — diffusion as a prior

The most provocative reframing. The internal features a text-to-image model learns are extraordinarily rich in geometry and semantics, so papers repurpose a frozen generator as a backbone for depth, segmentation, correspondence, even reasoning — trading “generate pixels” for “generate labels.”
CVPR'26UniPercept: A Unified Diffusion Model for Generalizable Visual Perception — one diffusion model does classification, detection and segmentation via task-conditional generation.
CVPR'26Visual Diffusion Models are Geometric Solvers — solves open geometric problems purely as pixel-space diffusion — the generator as a reasoning engine.
CVPR'26StableMTL: Repurposing Latent Diffusion Models for Multi-Task Learning — a pretrained latent diffusion model as the foundation for multi-task dense prediction.
CVPR'26FE2E: From Editor to Dense Geometry Estimator — adapts a pretrained image-editing model for dense geometry — editing models carry better inductive bias than raw generators.

6 · Generate a robot ACTION — diffusion policy

The cross-field surprise, and the single largest robotics use of diffusion (~45-50 papers). For one situation a human might go left or right around an obstacle — the good actions are multimodal. A regression policy averages them into the invalid middle (into the obstacle); a diffusion policy samples one coherent action from the full distribution.
ROBOTICS'25The Ingredients for Robotic Diffusion Transformers — the canonical study — the image diffusion transformer, repurposed to generate robot actions, on 1500+ step bimanual tasks.
ROBOTICS'25Diffusion-Based Approximate MPC: Fast and Consistent Imitation of Multi-Modal Action Distributions — the cleanest first-principles case for why denoising beats regression for control — L2 averages modes, diffusion doesn’t — at 250 Hz.
ROBOTICS'25Scaling Diffusion Policy in Transformer to 1 Billion Parameters (ScaleDP) — stabilizes diffusion-policy scaling from 10M to 1B parameters.
ROBOTICS'25Streaming Diffusion Policy: Fast Policy Synthesis with Variable Noise Diffusion Models — partially-denoised action streams for reactive, real-time control.
ROBOTICS'25Generalizable Humanoid Manipulation with 3D Diffusion Policies — a 3D diffusion policy on a 25-DoF humanoid, generalizing from single-scene data.

7 · Plan by denoising a whole trajectory

A trajectory is just a high-dimensional vector; a diffusion model learns a prior over feasible, smooth, collision-free paths. The killer feature is guidance — inject a cost or constraint gradient into each reverse step and the plan bends to obey it, no retraining. Diffusion as a planner.
ROBOTICS'25PRESTO: Fast Motion Planning Using Diffusion Models Based on Key-Configuration Representation — configuration-space diffusion trajectory priors for narrow passages.
ROBOTICS'25DIAL-MPC: Full-Order Sampling-Based MPC via Diffusion-Style Annealing — proves sampling-based optimal control (MPPI) IS single-step diffusion annealing — real-time legged control, training-free.
ROBOTICS'25Cascaded Diffusion Models for Neural Motion Planning — global-to-local diffusion with online collision repair.
ROBOTICS'25D4orm: Multi-Robot Trajectories with Dynamics-aware Diffusion Denoised Deformations — training-free — uses the denoising steps themselves as the optimizer for 16-robot teams.

8 · Generate grasps, worlds, and futures

Beyond actions and paths: diffusion samples the other multimodal, uncertain objects robotics needs. Valid grasps form a set over SE(3); safe futures are many; data is scarce and safety-critical, so diffusion synthesizes scans, scenes and demonstrations too.
ROBOTICS'25GAGrasp: Geometric Algebra Diffusion for Dexterous Grasping — SE(3)-equivariant grasp diffusion plus differentiable-physics refinement — structure image-gen never needs.
ROBOTICS'25Imagine-2-Drive: High-Fidelity World Models via Multi-Modal Diffusion Policies — a diffusion world model (parallel future generation) plus a diffusion policy actor, in one system.
ROBOTICS'25LidarDM: Generative LiDAR Simulation in a Generated World — a latent-diffusion 4D world with a LiDAR sensor render, for data-starved perception.
ROBOTICS'25GDTS: Goal-Guided Diffusion with Tree Sampling for Multi-Modal Pedestrian Prediction — forecasts inherently-multimodal human motion a regressor would average away.

9 · The relentless race for fewer steps

Diffusion’s one weakness is that sampling needs many sequential denoising steps — and our own measured curve shows most quality lands in the first handful. So both fields attack step count from every angle: distill the many-step teacher into one, straighten the walk with flow matching, or cache features across steps. In robotics this is existential (control needs 100s of Hz), which is why robotics leans hardest on flow matching.
CVPR'26MeanFlow Transformers with Representation Autoencoders — 1-step ImageNet-256 FID 2.03 with 83% less training compute — the strongest one-step result.
CVPR'26Phased DMD: Few-step Distribution Matching Distillation — partitions the SNR range into subintervals to avoid one-step degeneration.
CVPR'26GeoRK2: Geometry-Guided Runge-Kutta Integration for DiT Acceleration — a curvature-aware ODE solver fighting manifold drift at 4-5x speedup.
ROBOTICS'25FlowNav: Combining Flow Matching and Depth Priors for Efficient Navigation — robotics abandons many-step diffusion for flow matching purely to hit control-rate latency.
ROBOTICS'25R2Flow: Fast LiDAR Data Generation with Rectified Flows — rectified flow for few-step LiDAR generation.

What robotics needs that image-gen doesn't

Same machinery, different pressures — and the differences come from what each field is short of.

The punchline — the denoiser and the controller are the same algorithm

Two robotics papers prove the deepest version of the connection. DIAL-MPC and Diffusion-Approximate-MPC independently show that diffusion sampling is mathematically the same procedure as sampling-based optimal control — the MPPI planner robotics has used for years is single-step diffusion annealing. So the image denoiser that draws a cat and the controller that steers a legged robot are the same iterative-refinement algorithm. They differ in exactly one place: whether the nudge applied at each step is a text-alignment score or a collision cost. Learn diffusion once and you have learned a generator, a planner, and a controller.

That is why diffusion is the theme most worth understanding across both fields at once: it is not an image trick that happens to work on robots, it is a general recipe for sampling from any complicated distribution — of pixels, of shapes, or of things to do.
