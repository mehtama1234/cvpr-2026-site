Does the square commute?

The why page defines equivariance as a promise: rotate the input and the answer rotates the same way. That is something you can watch. In each panel below, the teal arrow is the network's prediction on the rotated input; the gold arrow is the original prediction, rotated by the same amount. If the network is truly equivariant, the two must always line up. Drag the rotation and see which one keeps its promise.

What to notice. For the equivariant network the teal and gold arrows sit exactly on top of each other at every rotation — predict-then-rotate equals rotate-then-predict, so the square commutes and symmetry holds by construction. For the plain network they agree only near zero (where it was effectively trained) and split apart as you rotate away — it never truly learned the symmetry, it just memorized one region. That gap is precisely why the plain net in the run collapsed on unseen orientations.
