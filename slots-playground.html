Slots competing for a scene

The why page says object-centric learning works by a few slots competing to claim the parts of an image. Here is that competition, live: each pixel is coloured by whichever slot won it, and each slot settles into the middle of what it owns. Turn the number of slots down and watch two objects get crammed into one — the merge that starves an object of its own identity.

What to notice. When there are enough slots, each object's pixels are claimed by a different slot — you can see the objects painted in distinct colours, each a separate handle you could grab. Slide the count below the number of objects and two of them are forced to share a slot: they fuse into one colour, one entangled representation with no way to tell them apart. Too few slots and the binding collapses; enough and the scene falls neatly into objects.
